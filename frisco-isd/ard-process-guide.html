Answer: In Frisco ISD, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education process where parents and district staff determine eligibility, services, and placement. Parents in Frisco ISD are equal committee members and must receive at least five school days’ written notice, have the right to audio record meetings, and can request a meeting at any time.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Frisco ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Frisco ISD

  • Notice Timeline: Minimum 5 school days' written notice required for all Frisco ISD ARD meetings.
  • Recording Rights: Parents may audio record the meeting; it is standard practice to notify the campus coordinator in advance.
  • Parent Role: Mandatory equal member; you are the primary expert on your child.
  • Annual Requirement: The committee must meet at least once every 12 months to review and revise the IEP.
  • Initial ARD Timing: Must occur within 30 calendar days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

Common ARD Questions in Frisco ISD

Can I request an ARD meeting at any time in Frisco ISD?

Yes, parents in Frisco ISD may request an ARD meeting whenever they have concerns about their child's progress or services. The district must respond to your written request within five school days to either schedule the meeting or explain why they refuse.

What happens if I disagree with the ARD committee?

If the committee cannot reach a consensus, Frisco ISD typically offers a 10-day recess to allow for further data collection and negotiation. You have the right to sign the document in disagreement and pursue mediation or a grievance.

Who leads the ARD meetings in Frisco ISD?

Meetings are generally led by a campus administrator or a designated ARD Facilitator. These individuals ensure the meeting stays student-focused and that all required procedural elements are covered.

How to Prepare for a Frisco ISD ARD Meeting

  1. Review Recent Data: Examine your child's current IEP progress reports and most recent evaluation data from the school.
  2. Request Draft Goals: Ask your child’s special education teacher for draft goals at least 3-5 days before the meeting to avoid surprises.
  3. Draft Parent Concerns: Write down specific goals, concerns, or observations you want recorded in the official "Parent Input" section.
  4. Gather Outside Info: If applicable, provide copies of private medical or therapeutic reports you wish the committee to consider.
  5. Notify of Attendees: Inform the school if you are bringing an advocate or therapist to ensure proper space and notice for all members.

How ARD Meetings in Frisco ISD May Differ From Other Districts

Due to its high volume of students, Frisco ISD frequently utilizes specialized ARD Facilitators at the secondary level to maintain neutral, student-centered discussions. The district places a heavy emphasis on Future-Ready Learners, meaning transition planning (starting as early as age 14) is a major focus in local ARD deliberations. Additionally, the district uses standardized digital systems (like SuccessEd) for sharing draft documents, which may allow parents to review proposed plans electronically before the meeting starts.
