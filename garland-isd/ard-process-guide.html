Answer: In Garland ISD, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education process where parents and district staff determine eligibility, services, and placement. Parents in Garland ISD are equal committee members and must receive at least five school days’ written notice, have the right to audio record meetings, and can request a meeting at any time to discuss their child's IEP.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Garland ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Garland ISD

  • Notice Timeline: Minimum 5 school days' written notice required for all Garland ISD ARD meetings.
  • Recording Rights: Parents may audio record the meeting; providing advance notice to the campus is a recommended professional courtesy.
  • Parent Role: You are an equal, mandatory member of the committee with decision-making authority.
  • Annual Requirement: The committee must meet at least once every 12 months to review and update the student's program.
  • Initial ARD Timing: Must occur within 30 calendar days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

Common ARD Questions in Garland ISD

Can I request an ARD meeting at any time in Garland ISD?

Yes, parents in Garland ISD may request an ARD meeting whenever they have concerns about their child's progress or services. The district must respond to your written request within five school days to either schedule the meeting or explain why they refuse.

Who leads the ARD meetings at my child's campus?

Garland ISD utilizes specialized ARD Committee Managers (also known as facilitators) to lead meetings and ensure local, state, and federal guidelines are met. These staff members act as a central point of contact for families during the IEP process.

What happens if I disagree with the committee's decision?

If a consensus cannot be reached, Garland ISD typically offers a 10-day recess to allow for further data collection and negotiation. You have the right to sign the document in disagreement and explore mediation or grievance options.

How to Prepare for a Garland ISD ARD Meeting

  1. Review Recent Data: Analyze your child's most recent FIE, progress reports, and classroom data before the meeting starts.
  2. Request Draft Documents: Ask your child’s ARD Committee Manager for a draft copy of proposed goals at least 3-5 days in advance.
  3. Prepare Parent Input: Write a specific "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the official meeting deliberations.
  4. Gather Outside Info: If available, bring copies of private medical or therapeutic reports you wish the Garland ISD committee to consider.
  5. Confirm Attendance: Notify the school if you are bringing an advocate, therapist, or support person so proper arrangements can be made.

How ARD Meetings in Garland ISD May Differ From Other Districts

Garland ISD is unique for its extensive use of dedicated ARD Committee Managers, who are specifically trained to facilitate neutral, student-centered discussions and support families with technical special education questions. For transition planning, GISD begins formal discussions as early as age 14, placing a high priority on self-advocacy and preparing students for life after high school through local programs and vocational resources. Additionally, the district frequently hosts Special Education Parent Workshops and social events, such as "Coffee & Conversation," to help families navigate the local process more effectively than generic state-wide guides.
