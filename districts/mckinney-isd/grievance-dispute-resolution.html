⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Strict deadlines apply to all complaints. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. Local district grievances in McKinney ISD (FNG Local) typically must be filed within 15 district business days of the time you knew or should have known of the issue. Failure to file on time often results in automatic dismissal.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with McKinney ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with McKinney ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or IEP.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP lists specific services (e.g., speech therapy, inclusion support, dyslexia intervention) but McKinney ISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

McKinney ISD has missed the 45-school-day timeline for completing the Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) or refuses to evaluate without valid cause.

ARD Meeting Issues

The district refuses to convene an ARD meeting, excludes you from placement decisions, or fails to provide proper notice before meetings.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child needs a different instructional setting (e.g., specialized behavior support vs. general education) than what MISD is offering.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated unfairly, harassed, or excluded from school activities specifically because of their disability.

Retaliation

You feel district staff are retaliating against you or your child because you advocated for their rights or filed a previous complaint.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

In many cases, informal resolution is faster and less adversarial. Consider:

  • Meeting with the campus Principal or Special Education Lead.
  • Contacting the MISD Special Populations Department at (469) 302-4000.
  • Emailing the Executive Director of Special Populations, Brynne Riley.
  • Requesting a reconvene ARD meeting to address your concerns formally.

Tip: Always document your conversations (date, time, who you spoke with) and follow up with an email summary. If informal steps fail, proceed below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides a hierarchy of options for resolving special education disputes, ranging from local district processes to federal investigations:

Level 1

District Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Best for: Local policy violations, staff conduct, or failure to follow the IEP within MISD.

Timeline: Must file within 15 district business days of the incident (verify current FNG Local policy).

Cost: Free

Binding: No - decisions can be appealed to the School Board and then to the TEA Commissioner.

Learn about MISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of special education law (IDEA), missed timelines, or procedural errors.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of the violation; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds a violation, MISD is legally required to correct it.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties are willing to compromise and want a neutral third party to facilitate.

Timeline: Voluntary process; typically scheduled within 30 days.

Cost: Free (TEA provides the mediator).

Binding: Agreements reached in mediation are legally binding contracts.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disputes about eligibility, placement (LRE), or FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education).

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of violation; hearing typically within 45 days.

Cost: May require attorney fees; families may recover fees if they prevail.

Binding: Yes - hearing officer's decision is legally binding.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504/ADA) or retaliation.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the discrimination.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - OCR enforcement is federal.

McKinney ISD Grievance Process (Level 1)

McKinney ISD uses Board Policy FNG (LOCAL) for student and parent complaints. This is a formal, tiered process that requires you to submit written forms within strict timelines.

Where to Submit Grievances

Address: McKinney ISD Administration, 1 Duvall Street, McKinney, TX 75069

Attention: Principal (for campus issues) or Office of Legal Affairs (if against Principal)

Phone: (469) 302-4000

Tip: We strongly recommend submitting via certified mail or hand-delivering to the front desk and asking for a date-stamped receipt.

Step-by-Step Filing Guide

  1. Obtain the Level 1 Form

    You must use the official MISD "Student/Parent Resolution Form - Level One." This can be downloaded from the McKinney ISD Grievance Page or requested from the administration building.

  2. File Within 15 Days

    You must file your complaint typically within 15 district business days of the time you knew, or should have known, about the event or policy you are complaining about. Check the current FNG (Local) policy to confirm this exact window.

  3. Level 1 Conference

    After filing, a district administrator (usually the principal) will schedule a conference with you to hear the complaint. They will provide a written response (Level 1 Decision) within 10 business days after the meeting.

  4. Appeals (Level 2 & 3)

    If you are not satisfied with the Level 1 decision, you can appeal to Level 2 (Superintendent's designee) and then Level 3 (Board of Trustees). Each appeal must be filed within 10 business days of receiving the previous decision.

Filing a Complaint with TEA

If McKinney ISD has violated a state or federal special education law, you can file a formal complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA).

What TEA Investigates

  • Failure to provide services listed in the IEP.
  • Failure to meet evaluation or ARD timelines.
  • Failure to provide Prior Written Notice (PWN).
  • Procedural violations in the ARD process.

How to File:

  • Download the TEA Model Complaint Form.
  • Email the completed form to: spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov
  • You MUST send a copy of the complaint to the McKinney ISD Superintendent at the same time.

Due Process Hearings

A Due Process Hearing is a formal legal proceeding similar to a trial. It is presided over by an Independent Hearing Officer appointed by TEA.

Note: Due Process is complex and adversarial. While not required, it is highly recommended to consult with an attorney or qualified advocate before filing.

Common Reasons for Due Process

  • Eligibility: MISD says your child is not eligible for special education, but you disagree.
  • Placement: You believe the district's proposed placement violates the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).
  • FAPE: The district's program is not reasonably calculated to enable your child to make appropriate progress.

Statute of Limitations: In Texas, you generally have one year from the date you knew or should have known about the alleged violation to request a hearing.

Office for Civil Rights (OCR)

If you believe your child has been discriminated against based on their disability, you can file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education Office for Civil Rights.

Examples of Discrimination

  • Refusing to allow a service animal or necessary accommodations.
  • Excluding a student from a field trip due to disability.
  • Bullying or harassment based on disability that the school fails to address.
  • Retaliation against parents for advocacy.

Documentation is Key

Regardless of which path you choose, success depends on evidence. Keep a "Paper Trail Binder" containing:

  • All IEPs and FIEs (Full Individual Evaluations).
  • Copies of all emails to/from the school.
  • Notes from phone calls and meetings (date, time, attendees, summary).
  • Work samples showing lack of progress.
  • Discipline referrals or incident reports.
