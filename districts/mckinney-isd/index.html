Local Authority Guide: McKinney ISD Special Populations

McKinney ISD's special education department is distinctive for its centralized Early Childhood Special Education (ECSE) model. While many districts distribute preschool services across multiple elementary campuses, McKinney ISD centralizes the majority of its 3-to-5-year-old services at Lawson Early Childhood School. This centralization allows for a high concentration of specialized staff, including occupational therapists and speech pathologists, at a single location.

Under Board Policy EHBA(LEGAL), McKinney ISD must complete a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) within 45 school days of receiving written parental consent. If your child is school-aged, you should submit this written request directly to the Campus Counselor at your local school (e.g., McKinney North High School or Faubion Middle School) to trigger the legal timeline.

For students with significant support needs, the district utilizes Structured Teach and Functional Academics instructional environments. Families should also engage with the P.O.W.E.R. Parenting series, a local district initiative designed to bridge the gap between home strategies and campus-level IEP implementation. Be aware that the district is currently implementing a realignment plan for the 2026-2027 school year, which may alter program locations for secondary students.

Secondary Leadership Structure

At the high school level, McKinney ISD employs a unique Co-Department Chair model. This means that at campuses like McKinney Boyd or McKinney High, there are two specialized leaders: one dedicated to instructional quality and one focused strictly on state and federal compliance.
