Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in McKinney ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review and update your child's educational program
  • You are an equal member of the committee and a vital partner in planning
  • McKinney ISD provides draft goals and data for your review prior to the meeting
  • Texas law allows parents to audio record ARD meetings

ARD Meeting Guide for McKinney ISD Parents

Navigating the special education process in Collin County. This guide helps McKinney ISD families prepare for, participate in, and follow up on ARD committee meetings.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are new to the DFW area or the state of Texas, you may be accustomed to the term "IEP Team." In Texas, the committee that develops the Individualized Education Program is known as the Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

The "ARD" acronym describes the committee's specific legal actions:

  • Admission: Determining if a student meets Texas eligibility criteria and admitting them to the program.
  • Review: An annual or periodic review of the student’s progress, goals, and accommodations.
  • Dismissal: Deciding, based on evaluation data, that a student no longer requires special education services.

In McKinney ISD, the ARD committee is the sole authority for making educational decisions for your child. The resulting document is the IEP, which acts as a legal contract between the school and the family.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in McKinney ISD?

The committee is comprised of individuals who offer a 360-degree view of your child’s academic and functional performance.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

The expert on the child. You provide essential information about your child’s strengths, challenges, and life outside school.

Special Education Teacher

Provides data on specialized instruction and progress toward individual academic and functional goals.

General Education Teacher

Ensures that the student has access to the general curriculum and explains grade-level TEKS expectations.

Administrator

A campus principal or assistant principal who ensures that McKinney ISD resources are committed to the plan.

Diagnostician or LSSP

Responsible for interpreting evaluation results and helping the team understand the child's learning profile.

The Student

Included for transition planning starting at age 14; otherwise included at parental discretion.

Additional Support Members:

  • Advocates: You may bring a professional special education advocate to support you in the meeting.
  • Related Service Providers: Speech pathologists, OTs, or PTs if your child receives these services.
  • Translators: McKinney ISD provides interpreters for families whose primary language is not English.

McKinney ISD Policy: If you plan to bring an attorney or record the meeting, it is helpful to notify the campus facilitator 48 hours in advance so the district can prepare accordingly.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in McKinney ISD

Strategic preparation helps ensure that your priorities are addressed and that the meeting remains focused on your child's needs.

1-2 Weeks Before: The Data Review

  • Review the Draft IEP: Ask your child’s case manager for draft goals and PLAAFPs at least 5 days early.
  • Examine progress reports: Are goals being met? If not, why?
  • Check report cards and work samples via the McKinney ISD Parent Portal (HAC).
  • Gather any private evaluation reports from doctors or therapists.

3 Days Before: Final Planning

  • Write down your "Parent Concerns" statement. This must be included in the official deliberations.
  • Identify the top 3 skills you want your child to learn this year.
  • Prepare a list of questions regarding service minutes or accommodations.
  • Notify the school of any additional guests you are bringing.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

In McKinney ISD, meetings follow a standard agenda to ensure all legal components of the IEP are addressed.

The Typical LISD Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and responsibilities.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of current performance levels in all areas.
  3. Evaluation Review: If a re-evaluation was recently completed.
  4. Parent Input: Your chance to share your vision and your specific concerns.
  5. Goals & Objectives: Reviewing and revising measurable annual goals.
  6. Accommodations & Modifications: Determining the supports needed for the classroom and testing.
  7. LRE/Placement: Determining the "Least Restrictive Environment" for services.
  8. Consensus: The team works to reach agreement on the final document.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for breaks at any time during the discussion.
  • ✓ You can request the meeting be recessed and finished on another day.
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification of any acronyms or educational terminology.
  • ✓ Audio Recording: You have the legal right to record the meeting in Texas.
  • ✓ You can disagree with any part of the school's proposal.

Communication Tips:

  • Ask to see the data that supports the school's recommendations for service time.
  • Ensure all goals are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Attainable, Relevant, Time-bound).
  • If you feel rushed, remind the committee that you have the right to participate meaningfully.
  • Take your own notes or bring someone to take them for you.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting marks the beginning of the new IEP cycle.

The Three Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

The committee reaches a consensus. You sign "Agree," and the new plan is implemented. You will receive a final copy for your records.

Partial Agreement

In Texas, you can sign "Agree" to the parts of the IEP you like so those services start immediately, while noting your disagreement on disputed sections.

Non-Consensus and Recess

If you disagree with a major component, McKinney ISD must offer a 10-day recess. This allows everyone to gather more information and attempt to reach a compromise.

Monitor Implementation:

The IEP is a legal document. Check that accommodations (like extra time or preferential seating) are actually being provided in the classroom. If progress is not being made, you don't have to wait for the next annual meeting—request a "Brief ARD" to address the issue immediately.
