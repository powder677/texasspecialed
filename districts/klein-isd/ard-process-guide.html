Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Klein ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (the Texas name for the IEP team).
  • Must be held at least once per year to review and update your child's program.
  • You are an equal member of the committee with a legal right to participate in all decisions.
  • Initial ARD meetings must be held within 30 days of the completion of an evaluation report.
  • You have the right to record meetings and bring advocates for support.

ARD Meeting Guide for Klein ISD Parents

Navigating the special education journey in Klein ISD. Learn how to prepare for, participate in, and advocate for your child's needs at the ARD committee table.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are new to Klein or Texas, you may be familiar with the "IEP Team." In Texas, this group is officially called the ARD Committee.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial meeting where eligibility is determined and a student is admitted into special education.
  • Review: The annual meeting to review progress and update the Individualized Education Program (IEP).
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting special education when services are no longer required.

In Klein ISD, the ARD committee is the sole decision-making body for your child's special education program.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Klein ISD?

The committee is a team of professionals and you, working together to develop a plan tailored to your child's strengths.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are an equal member. Your knowledge of your child's life outside of school is vital for a complete educational plan.

Special Education Teacher

The provider responsible for specialized instruction and monitoring progress toward IEP goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in a general education environment.

District Representative

Usually a campus principal or assistant principal who can authorize district resources.

Evaluator

A Diagnostician or LSSP who interprets evaluation results and testing data.

The Student

Required for transition discussions (age 14+) and encouraged for self-advocacy whenever appropriate.

You May Also Invite:

  • Special Education Advocate: A professional hired to help protect your rights and ensure your child's needs are met.
  • Legal Counsel: You have a right to bring an attorney. Note that districts often request notice if an attorney will attend.
  • Private Specialists: Outside therapists or doctors who have relevant information to share.
  • Translators: Klein ISD must provide an interpreter if you are not fluent in English.

How Klein ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Klein ISD typically utilizes Evaluation & ARD Specialists (Diagnosticians) to help facilitate meetings and ensure all legal documentation is complete. Meetings are generally held at your child's home campus, but virtual options via Zoom are often available for parent convenience.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Klein ISD

Preparation is key to being a proactive member of the committee. Use the following timeline to stay organized.

2 Weeks Before: Gather Information

  • Review the current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Gather recent work samples, report cards, and private medical reports.
  • Talk with your child about what is helping them in class and what is difficult.

1 Week Before: Define Your Goals

  • Draft a "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the meeting records.
  • Identify 3-5 specific changes or new goals you want to discuss.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a draft of proposed goals to review in advance.
  • Notify the campus coordinator if you are bringing an advocate or plan to record.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

ARD meetings follow a legally mandated agenda to ensure all necessary parts of your child's education are covered.

Typical ARD Meeting Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and confirming all required members are present.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Sharing your concerns, observations from home, and vision for your child.
  4. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the coming year.
  5. Services & Accommodations: Determining classroom support, therapy minutes, and modifications.
  6. LRE & Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment where services will be provided.
  7. Review of Deliberations: Checking the minutes for accuracy before signing.

Your Rights at the Table:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point during the meeting.
  • ✓ You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee reaches a stalemate.
  • ✓ You can audio record the meeting (Texas allows this without school consent).
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any term or acronym (e.g., PLAAFP, LRE, FAPE).
  • ✓ You can disagree with recommendations and have it noted in the record.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting is the plan; the following weeks are where the plan is put into action.

Full Agreement

If everyone agrees, services typically begin within 5-10 school days unless a later start date is set in the IEP.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to some parts (like goals) while disagreeing with others (like placement). The agreed portions can start while you resolve the dispute.

Formal Disagreement

If you cannot reach a consensus, you can request a recess, file a grievance, or pursue mediation.

Meeting Frequency

Klein ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: To update and review the current IEP.
  • Triennially: For a full reevaluation (REED process) every 3 years.
  • On Request: Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss concerns.
