⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

While TEA complaints must be filed within one year, Katy ISD's local grievance policy is more generous than many other districts but still has strict deadlines. You generally have 60 days from the date you knew of the issue to file a formal local complaint. However, do not delay—early action is often most effective.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP lists specific services (e.g., 30 minutes of speech therapy weekly) but Katy ISD is not providing them consistently.

Evaluation Delays

The district has not completed the Full and Individual Initial Evaluation (FIIE) within the required 45 school days after receiving your written consent.

ARD Meeting Issues

The Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee refuses to consider your outside evaluations or finalizes decisions without your meaningful input.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child is being placed in a setting that is too restrictive (e.g., a centralized program) and could be successful in a general education classroom with support.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being excluded from campus activities, field trips, or electives solely because of their disability.

Retaliation

You feel that campus staff are treating you or your child unfairly because you have previously advocated for special education services.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

Katy ISD encourages resolving issues at the lowest possible level. Consider these steps first:

  • Campus Level: Speak with the teacher, Assistant Principal, or Principal.
  • Department Level: Contact the Special Education Department at 281-396-2630 to discuss your concerns.
  • Leadership: Escalate to Brian Malechuk, Executive Director of Special Education.
  • ARD Meeting: Request a reconvene ARD meeting to formally document your disagreement.

Tip: If you cannot resolve the issue informally, you can access the formal grievance process via the "MyKaty" parent portal.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides multiple pathways for resolving special education disputes. Here's the hierarchy from informal to formal:

Level 1

District Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Best for: District-specific policy violations, staff misconduct, communication issues, and service implementation.

Timeline: File within 60 days of the incident.

Cost: Free.

Binding: No - can be appealed to the School Board.

Learn about KISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of state or federal special education law (IDEA), such as missed timelines or failure to implement the IEP.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of the violation; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds a violation, KISD must comply with corrective actions.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties want to reach a compromise with the help of a professional mediator.

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 30 days.

Cost: Free (TEA provides the mediator).

Binding: Yes - the resulting agreement is legally binding.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Major disputes regarding eligibility, placement (LRE), or FAPE that cannot be resolved otherwise.

Timeline: 1-year statute of limitations; hearing held within 45 days of the resolution period ending.

Cost: High. Attorney representation is strongly recommended.

Binding: Yes - decision by a Hearing Officer.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504/ADA), retaliation, or denial of access.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the discrimination.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - federal investigation.

Katy ISD Grievance Process

Katy ISD follows Board Policy FNG(LOCAL). Unlike many other Texas districts that have a 15-day window, Katy ISD policy states you may access the process as long as the request is made within 60 days of when you first knew of the issue.

The Three Levels of Grievance

Level One: Campus Principal

File your complaint using the forms available in the "MyKaty" parent portal or request them by emailing Legal@katyisd.org.

  • Submit the form, evidence, and desired resolution.
  • A conference will be scheduled with the principal or appropriate administrator.
  • You will receive a written response after the conference.

Level Two: Appeal to Administration

If the Level One decision does not resolve your concern, you may appeal.

  • You must submit the Level Two Appeal form within 20 business days of receiving the Level One decision.
  • A central administration designee (often an Assistant Superintendent) will hear the appeal.

Level Three: Board of Trustees

If you remain dissatisfied, you may appeal to the Katy ISD Board of Trustees.

  • You must submit the Level Three Appeal form within 20 days of receiving the Level Two decision.
  • The Board will hear the grievance at a scheduled meeting.
  • The Board's decision is final for the local process.

Access Forms: Log in to the MyKaty Portal and look for the "Parent Complaints" folder to find the official forms.

Filing a Complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA)

A TEA complaint is a formal allegation that the district has violated federal or state special education laws. This process is handled entirely by the state agency in Austin.

Requirements

  • Complaint must be in writing and signed.
  • Must allege a specific violation of IDEA or state rules.
  • Violation must have occurred within the last one year.
  • You must forward a copy of the complaint to Katy ISD at the same time you send it to TEA.

The Process

  1. Submission: Email your complaint to spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov or mail it to the TEA offices in Austin.
  2. Investigation: TEA has 60 calendar days to investigate. They will review documents from both you and Katy ISD.
  3. Decision: TEA issues a Letter of Findings. If non-compliance is found, Katy ISD will be required to complete corrective actions (e.g., staff training, compensatory services).

Due Process Hearings

Due process is the most formal dispute resolution option. It involves a legal hearing before a Special Education Hearing Officer. This path is often necessary for deep disagreements about a child's educational needs or placement.

Note: Due process hearings are legal proceedings. Katy ISD will be represented by attorneys. It is highly advisable for parents to seek legal counsel before filing for due process.

Common Reasons for Due Process

  • Disagreement over the child's eligibility for special education.
  • Disagreement over the "Least Restrictive Environment" (LRE).
  • Claims that the district failed to provide a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE).

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaints

If your dispute involves discrimination, harassment, or retaliation based on disability, you may file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights.

TEA Complaint vs. OCR Complaint

Feature TEA Complaint OCR Complaint
Primary Law IDEA (Educational Services) Section 504 & ADA (Civil Rights)
Typical Issues IEP goals, service minutes, evaluations Bullying, denial of access, retaliation
Filing Window 1 Year 180 Days (6 months)

To file a complaint with OCR, you do not need to have a lawyer. The process is generally more accessible to parents than a due process hearing.

Documentation is Key

Successful dispute resolution relies on evidence. Keep a detailed "Special Education Binder" with:

  • Communication Logs: Dates, times, and summaries of every conversation with school staff.
  • Emails: Save all email correspondence.
  • Evaluations: Copies of all FIEs and outside evaluations.
  • IEP/ARD Documents: Every draft and finalized IEP.
  • Work Samples: Examples of classwork that support your concerns.
