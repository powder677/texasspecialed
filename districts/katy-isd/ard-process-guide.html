Answer: In Katy ISD, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education process where parents and district staff determine eligibility, services, and placement. Under Texas law, Katy ISD parents must receive at least five school days’ written notice, have the right to audio record all proceedings, and serve as equal members of the ARD committee.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Katy ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Katy ISD

  • Notice Timeline: Minimum 5 school days' written notice required.
  • Recording Rights: Parents may audio record; notification to the campus is requested.
  • Parent Role: Mandatory, equal member of the ARD committee.
  • Annual Frequency: Required at least once every 12 months.
  • Initial ARD Timing: Must occur within 30 days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

How ARD Meetings in Katy ISD Differ From Other Districts

Katy ISD’s large-scale infrastructure impacts how ARD meetings are facilitated and documented:

  • Digital IEP Systems: Katy ISD utilizes a centralized digital system for all IEP documentation; parents typically receive copies via the district's secure parent portal.
  • Facilitator Availability: For complex or high-conflict cases, Katy ISD may provide an impartial district facilitator to help the committee remain focused on student data.
  • Virtual Accessibility: Meetings are typically held at the home campus, but Katy ISD offers robust virtual participation options via Zoom or Microsoft Teams for parent convenience.

How to Prepare for an ARD Meeting in Katy ISD

Step 1: Audit and Review Data

Review the current IEP goals and the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) to determine which milestones have been mastered.

Step 2: Request Draft Documents

Request a draft copy of the proposed IEP goals from your Katy ISD case manager at least one week before the meeting to ensure you are not reviewing new proposals for the first time during the ARD.

Step 3: Document Parent Concerns

Write a formal "Parent Concerns" statement. Under Texas procedural safeguards, this must be included in the formal deliberations of the ARD meeting.

Common Katy ISD ARD Questions

Can I bring an advocate to a Katy ISD ARD?

Yes, parents have the legal right to bring advocates, experts, or legal counsel to any Katy ISD meeting. If bringing an attorney, the district usually requires 48-72 hours' notice.

What happens if I disagree with the committee's decision?

If consensus is not reached, you can request the meeting be recessed for up to 10 school days to gather more data or seek a compromise before reconvening.
