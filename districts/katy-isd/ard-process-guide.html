Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Katy ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas-specific term for IEP team)
  • Meetings occur at least annually to review and update your child's educational program
  • Parents are equal members of the ARD committee with full decision-making rights
  • Texas law allows you to bring advocates, experts, or legal counsel to any meeting
  • You may request an ARD meeting at any time if you have concerns about progress

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are relocating to the Katy area from outside of Texas, you might be looking for an "IEP Meeting." In Texas, we use the term ARD Committee. While the result is the same—a legally binding Individualized Education Program—the terminology is unique to the Lone Star State.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: When your child is first found eligible for special education services.
  • Review: The annual check-in to adjust goals, services, and accommodations.
  • Dismissal: When the committee determines a student no longer requires specialized instruction.

In Katy ISD, this committee ensures your child receives a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) tailored to their specific strengths and challenges.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Katy ISD?

The ARD committee is a collaborative group. In a large district like Katy ISD, the specific staff members present will depend on your child’s campus and specific disability category.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are the most important member. You provide the longitudinal history and "real world" perspective that school data sometimes misses.

Special Education Teacher

The specialist who designs and implements the modified curriculum or specialized instruction.

General Education Teacher

Provides insight into the expectations of the grade-level curriculum and how your child functions among peers.

District Representative (Administrator)

Usually a principal or assistant principal who ensures the school can provide the staffing and resources agreed upon.

Evaluator (Diagnostician/LSSP)

Professional who explains testing results and how they translate to classroom needs.

The Student

Mandatory for transition planning (starts at age 14 in Texas) but often helpful for younger students to share their own goals.

Additional Support You Can Bring:

  • Special Education Advocate: A professional who knows Texas law and can help you negotiate for services.
  • Legal Counsel: You have the right to bring an attorney (Katy ISD usually requires 48-72 hours notice if you bring a lawyer).
  • Outside Therapists: Private BCBAs, speech pathologists, or tutors who work with your child outside of school.
  • A Friend: Simply having someone there to take notes allows you to focus on the conversation.

Expert Tip: Always notify your campus ARD facilitator in writing at least 2 days before the meeting if you are bringing guests.

How Katy ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Katy ISD utilizes a centralized digital system for IEP documentation. Meetings are typically held at the student's home campus, though the district offers virtual attendance options for parents via Zoom or Microsoft Teams. For complex cases, the district may provide an impartial "facilitator" to help keep the committee focused on the student's needs.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Katy ISD

Walking into an ARD meeting can feel overwhelming. Preparation is the antidote to anxiety. Use this timeline to stay organized.

2 Weeks Before: Audit & Review

  • Read the current IEP: What goals were actually mastered?
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Look through work samples, tests, and homework for patterns of struggle.
  • Gather any private medical or psychological reports from the last year.

1 Week Before: Strategy & Communication

  • Write down your "Parent Concerns" statement—the school must include this in the IEP.
  • List the top 3 items you want to see changed (e.g., more speech time, different seating).
  • Request a Draft IEP: You have the right to see the school's proposed goals before the meeting starts.
  • Confirm the attendance of your advocate or support person.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Katy ISD meetings follow a legal sequence. Knowing the steps helps you identify when to bring up specific concerns.

The ARD Meeting Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Confirming everyone is present and identifying roles.
  2. Review of Data: Discussing Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance (PLAAFP).
  3. Parent Input: This is your primary time to speak. Share your observations from home.
  4. Goals & Objectives: Drafting the specific skills your child will work on this year.
  5. Accommodations & Modifications: Deciding what "tools" (like extra time or shortened assignments) your child needs.
  6. Placement & Services: Determining where the services happen (General Ed vs. Resource) and for how many minutes.
  7. Closing: Reviewing the "Deliberations" (the minutes of the meeting) and signing.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any time to consult with your spouse or advocate.
  • ✓ You can ask the school to explain any acronyms (LRE, BIP, FBA, etc.) in plain English.
  • ✓ Recording: Texas is a one-party consent state. You have the legal right to audio record the ARD meeting.
  • ✓ You can disagree. If you don't like a proposal, you do not have to sign "Agreement."
  • ✓ You can request the meeting be recessed (paused) for 10 days to think about the proposals.

Tips for Success:

  • Ask for Data: If the school wants to reduce a service, ask: "What data shows that my child no longer needs this?"
  • Focus on Measurability: Ensure goals have numbers. "Will improve reading" is not a goal; "Will read 80 words per minute with 90% accuracy" is.
  • Don't Sign in a Rush: You can take the IEP home to review. You are not required to sign "Agreement" the moment the meeting ends.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting is the plan; the weeks following are the execution. Stay involved to ensure the plan becomes reality.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You and Katy ISD agree on the entire plan. Services typically begin within 5-10 school days. You will receive a final copy of the IEP via the parent portal.

Partial Agreement

You agree with the goals but disagree with the placement or minutes. In Texas, you can sign to implement the parts you agree on while continuing to dispute the rest.

Non-Agreement (10-Day Recess)

If you disagree on a fundamental level, the committee will likely "recess." You will meet again within 10 school days to try to find a compromise.

Implementation Check:

Two weeks after the meeting, email your child's teachers. Ask: "Now that the new IEP is in place, how are the new accommodations working in the classroom?" This ensures everyone has actually read the document.

When do we meet again?

Katy ISD must hold an ARD meeting for your child at least once every 12 months. However, you can request a "Brief ARD" or "Revision ARD" at any time if you see a lack of progress or a new need arises.
