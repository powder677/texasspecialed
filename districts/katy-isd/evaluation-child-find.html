Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal IDEA law and Texas Education Code, Katy ISD has a "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with disabilities (birth through age 21) who may need special education services. This mandate applies regardless of the severity of the disability or whether the child attends a public, private, or home school within the district.

You Have the Right To:

  • Request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) at any time.
  • Receive a response (Prior Written Notice) within 15 school days of your written request.
  • Have your child assessed in all areas of suspected disability, including health, vision, hearing, social-emotional status, and motor abilities.
  • Receive a copy of the Procedural Safeguards explaining your legal rights.
  • Request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense if you disagree with the district's findings.

New to Special Education?

If you're concerned about your child's learning, behavior, or development, you don't need a medical diagnosis to act. Katy ISD evaluates based on educational need to determine if a student qualifies for services.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Katy ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

The process begins with a written request for evaluation. For enrolled students, address the letter to your School Principal. For preschool-aged children not yet enrolled, address it to the Special Education Director.

2

District Reviews Your Request

Katy ISD has 15 school days from the receipt of your written request to provide you with a Prior Written Notice. This notice states whether the district proposes to evaluate your child or refuses to do so, along with their reasoning.

3

Sign Consent for Evaluation

If the district agrees to evaluate, they must provide you with an opportunity to sign a Written Consent form.

Crucial Point: The legal timeline does NOT begin until the district receives your signed consent.

Tip: Keep a copy of the dated consent form. This is Day 1 of the 45-school-day timeline.
4

Evaluation is Conducted

Katy ISD specialists (LSSPs, Diagnosticians, and Speech Therapists) will assess your child in all areas related to the suspected disability. This may include:

  • Psychological Evaluation: Assessing cognition and IQ.
  • Academic Performance: Testing reading, math, and writing.
  • Communicative Status: Evaluating speech and language skills.
  • Social and Emotional Status: Reviewing behavior and mental health.
  • Dyslexia Assessment: Under Texas HB 3928, dyslexia evaluations are now conducted through the special education process.
5

Initial ARD Meeting is Scheduled

Once the evaluation (FIIE) is complete, the district must hold an Initial ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting within 30 calendar days.

At this meeting, the ARD committee (including you) will review the results and determine if your child meets eligibility for special education and requires an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Texas Timeline Requirements (15-45-30)

Katy ISD must adhere to the standard Texas special education timelines:

Day 0

Written request submitted to school.

15 School Days

District provides Consent Form or Refusal.

45 School Days

Evaluation completed after consent is signed.

30 Calendar Days

ARD meeting held to review results.

What If the Deadline is Missed?

If Katy ISD misses these legal deadlines, you have recourse:

  • Contact the Campus Principal or the District Special Education Department (281-396-2630).
  • File a formal complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA) via SPEDTex.
  • Request a Due Process Hearing if the delay impacts your child's education.

Common Questions About Evaluations in Katy ISD

Can the school refuse to evaluate my child?

Yes, but they must provide you with a Prior Written Notice explaining the reason for the refusal and provide you with a copy of your Procedural Safeguards.

What if my child is in private or home school?

Children attending private or home schools within Katy ISD boundaries are still eligible for evaluation under Child Find. Contact the district Special Education Department to initiate the referral.

How does HB 3928 change dyslexia evaluations?

As of 2023, Texas law requires that evaluations for dyslexia occur through the IDEA (special education) process rather than just Section 504. This provides students with more robust protections and potential specialized instruction through an IEP.
