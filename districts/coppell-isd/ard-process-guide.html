Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Coppell ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee with final say on consent for initial services
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate, attorney, or support person
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time, and the district must respond within 5 school days

ARD Meeting Guide for Coppell ISD Parents

Navigating the special education process in Coppell ISD can feel overwhelming. This guide empowers you with the knowledge to be your child's best advocate during Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee meetings.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to the Dallas/Fort Worth Metroplex from another state, you might be looking for "IEP meeting" information. In Texas, the process is governed by the ARD committee. While the result is still an Individualized Education Program (IEP), the vocabulary used in Coppell ISD and throughout the state is unique to Texas law.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: This is the initial meeting where the committee determines if your child is eligible for special education services based on an evaluation.
  • Review: This is the most common type of meeting. The committee meets at least once a year to review progress, update goals, and adjust services.
  • Dismissal: If data shows a student no longer requires specialized instruction to access the curriculum, the committee meets to formally exit the student from the program.

In Coppell ISD, the ARD process is designed to be a collaborative bridge between home and school. The committee’s primary goal is to ensure your child receives a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) in the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Coppell ISD?

The composition of the ARD committee is strictly defined by law to ensure all relevant perspectives are considered when planning your child's education.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are the most important member. You provide the "Parental Input" that forms the foundation of the IEP. You have the right to participate in every decision regarding placement and services.

Special Education Teacher

Usually your child's case manager or a provider of specialized instruction. They propose goals and track progress on accommodations.

General Education Teacher

At least one general education teacher is required if your child participates (or may participate) in the general education environment. They provide insight into the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS).

District Representative

In Coppell ISD, this is typically a principal or assistant principal. They must have the authority to commit district resources and ensure services are implemented.

Diagnostician or LSSP

The Educational Diagnostician or Licensed Specialist in School Psychology (LSSP) interprets evaluation data and explains how your child’s disability impacts their learning.

The Student

Students are encouraged to attend when appropriate. Once a student reaches age 14 in Texas, they must be invited to discuss transition services and post-secondary goals.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocate or Educational Consultant: Many Coppell parents choose to bring a professional advocate to help navigate complex legal requirements.
  • Legal Counsel: You have the right to bring an attorney. It is common practice to notify the Coppell ISD special education office 48-72 hours in advance if an attorney will attend.
  • Service Providers: You can invite private therapists (Speech, OT, PT) who work with your child outside of school to provide additional data.
  • Support System: A friend or relative can attend to take notes or provide emotional support.

Pro-Tip: Always provide Coppell ISD with a list of who you are bringing at least a few days before the meeting to ensure there is adequate space and the district can prepare their own team accordingly.

How Coppell ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Coppell ISD typically conducts ARD meetings on the student's home campus. Since 2020, the district has also embraced virtual meeting options (via Zoom or Microsoft Teams) to accommodate working parents. If you prefer an in-person meeting, you have the right to request one. The district strives for a "consensus" model, meaning everyone works together to reach an agreement rather than voting.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Coppell ISD

Preparation is the key to a successful ARD. Because these meetings can be fast-paced and data-heavy, walking in with a plan ensures your voice is heard.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review the Current IEP: Look at the "PLAAFPs" (Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance). Do they accurately describe your child today?
  • Check Progress Reports: Have the goals from the last year been met? If not, why?
  • Organize Records: Gather recent report cards, work samples, and any private evaluations from doctors or therapists.
  • Medical Updates: Note any changes in medication, sleep patterns, or health that might affect school performance.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Draft Your Parent Statement: Write a short summary of your child's strengths and your biggest concerns. This can be pasted directly into the IEP.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a draft copy of the proposed goals. While not legally required, most Coppell ISD staff will provide this so you aren't seeing goals for the first time during the meeting.
  • Finalize Your Ask: Are you asking for more inclusion time? Different accommodations? Specialized reading support? Know your "must-haves."
  • Invite Your Team: Confirm attendance with any advocates or outside providers you are bringing.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

In Coppell ISD, ARD meetings generally follow a standardized agenda to ensure all legal requirements are met. Understanding this flow helps you know when to speak up.

Typical Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: The "Statement of Confidentiality" is read, and everyone identifies their role.
  2. Review of Evaluation: The Diagnostician or LSSP reviews any new testing (FIE).
  3. PLAAFP Discussion: This is the "heart" of the IEP. The team discusses your child's current strengths and weaknesses. This is where you should share your parent statement.
  4. Goals & Objectives: The committee reviews proposed goals for the coming year. Ensure they are SMART (Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound).
  5. Accommodations & Modifications: Discussion of what supports (like extra time or shortened assignments) are needed in the classroom.
  6. Schedule of Services: Determining how many minutes of Special Education, Speech, or OT your child will receive and in what setting.
  7. Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment (General Ed vs. Resource vs. Self-Contained).
  8. Closing: The "deliberations" (meeting minutes) are read back, and the committee signs the document.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • Right to Record: In Texas, you have the legal right to audio record your ARD meeting. You do not need district consent, but it is a courtesy to inform them you are doing so.
  • Right to a Break: If the meeting becomes emotional or you need to consult with your advocate, you can ask for a 10-minute recess at any time.
  • Right to Plain Language: If the team uses "eduspeak" or acronyms you don't understand, stop the meeting and ask for an explanation.
  • Right to Disagree: You do not have to agree with the district's proposal. If you disagree, you can request a "10-day recess" to gather more information and meet again.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Use "I" Statements: Instead of "You aren't helping him," try "I have noticed he struggles more when the homework isn't modified."
  • Ask for Data: If the school says a service isn't needed, ask to see the progress monitoring data that supports that claim.
  • Take Notes: If you aren't recording, have a dedicated notebook for ARD notes to track what was promised.
  • Stay Child-Centered: Keep the conversation focused on your child's unique needs rather than school policy or budget.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The work doesn't end when the meeting adjourns. The implementation phase is where the "paper IEP" becomes your child's "real education."

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

If you agree with the plan, you sign "In Agreement." Coppell ISD will begin implementing the new IEP immediately or on the date specified in the document.

Disagreement & The 10-Day Recess

In Texas, if the parent and district do not agree on the IEP, the meeting is "recessed." You will reconvene within 10 school days to try and reach a compromise. This time should be used to look at new data or alternative solutions.

Non-Consensus

If you still disagree after the recess, you can sign "In Disagreement." The district will implement their plan anyway, but you have the right to file for mediation or a due process hearing.

Implementation Timeline:

Once an IEP is signed, the district is legally bound to follow it. Check in with your child's teachers 2-3 weeks after the ARD to ensure the new accommodations are being used in all classes.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

While the law requires an "Annual ARD," you are not limited to once a year. In Coppell ISD, you can request a "Brief ARD" or an "Amendment" at any time to address specific concerns without waiting for the anniversary date.
