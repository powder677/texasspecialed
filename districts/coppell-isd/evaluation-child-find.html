How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Coppell ISD

A step-by-step guide to navigating the special education evaluation and Child Find process in Coppell ISD.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal IDEA regulations, Coppell ISD is responsible for identifying, locating, and evaluating children from birth through age 21 with known or suspected disabilities who reside within district boundaries. This "Child Find" duty applies regardless of whether a child is enrolled in public school, private school, or is homeschooled.

Your Essential Rights:

  • The right to request an initial evaluation in writing at any time.
  • The right to receive Prior Written Notice and Procedural Safeguards if the district agrees or refuses to evaluate.
  • The right to an evaluation at no cost to the parent.
  • The right to have evaluations administered in the child's native language.
  • The right to request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) if you disagree with the school's results.

Birth to 3 Years

If your child is under age 3 and you suspect developmental delays, you should contact Early Childhood Intervention (ECI) providers: The Warren Center for Dallas County (971-490-9055) or ECI of North Central Texas for Denton County (817-446-8000).

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Coppell ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

Initial requests for evaluation must be made in writing to a district administrative staff member. While anyone concerned about a child can make a referral, a formal letter from a parent ensures specific legal timelines are met.

2

The 15-Day Response

Coppell ISD has 15 school days from the date they receive your written request to provide a formal response. They must either:

  • Provide Prior Written Notice of their proposal to evaluate and obtain your informed consent.
  • Provide Prior Written Notice of their refusal to evaluate, along with the reasons for that decision.
3

Informed Consent

Evaluation cannot begin until the district receives your signed, written consent. "Informed" means you have a clear understanding of the tests being proposed and the scope of the evaluation.

Timeline: Once consent is received, the district generally has 45 school days to complete the Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) report.
4

The Evaluation (FIIE)

A multidisciplinary team including educational diagnosticians and school psychologists will assess your child in all areas of suspected disability.

  • Academic, developmental, and functional performance.
  • Cognitive and psychological functioning.
  • Speech, occupational, or physical therapy needs.
  • Dyslexia: Under Texas HB 3928, dyslexia is recognized as a specific learning disability under IDEA. Evaluations for dyslexia now occur via the special education framework to ensure full parent rights.
5

Initial ARD Meeting

The ARD committee must meet within 30 calendar days of the date the evaluation report is completed to determine eligibility. As a parent, you are a required member of this committee.

If eligible, an Individualized Education Program (IEP) will be developed during this meeting.

Texas Evaluation Timelines

15 School Days

Time for District to respond to your written request.

45 School Days

Deadline to complete the evaluation after signed consent.

30 Calendar Days

Deadline to hold the Initial ARD meeting after the report is finished.

Summer Timelines

If consent is obtained at least 35 but less than 45 school days before the last day of school, the report must be provided to the parent by June 30th. The ARD committee would then meet by the 15th school day of the following year.

Common Questions for Coppell Parents

Do I need a medical diagnosis first?

No. School districts are required to evaluate children based on "suspected disability" that impacts educational performance. A medical diagnosis can be shared with the district, but the school must still conduct its own evaluation for educational eligibility.

Can the district refuse to evaluate?

Yes, if they do not suspect a disability. However, they must provide Prior Written Notice explaining why and provide you with your Notice of Procedural Safeguards so you know how to challenge that decision.

What has changed with dyslexia testing?

With the passage of HB 3928, all dyslexia evaluations must now follow IDEA (Special Education) procedures rather than Section 504. This ensures parents have more robust rights and children have access to specialized instruction if eligible.
