About Plano ISD Special Education

Plano Independent School District (PISD) is widely recognized for its robust specialized programming, often serving as a hub for services that smaller surrounding districts may not offer. The Special Education Department operates with a philosophy of providing a "full continuum of services," meaning placements range from full inclusion in general education to specialized separate campuses. A key hub for these services is the Beaty Early Childhood School, which is entirely dedicated to serving preschool-aged children (ages 3-5) with disabilities, a rarity in public school districts.

For older students, Plano ISD offers the Solomon Center for Adult Transition. This specialized campus serves students ages 18-21 who have completed their high school credit requirements but need continued support in vocational training and independent living skills. If your child is approaching graduation, your ARD committee should discuss this "18+ Transition" option well before their senior year.

Parents should be familiar with PISD's specific program acronyms. You may hear terms like RISE (Reaching Independence through Structured Education) for students with significant cognitive and behavioral needs, or EAC (Essential Academics Class) for students requiring a modified curriculum. Unlike some districts that use generic "life skills" labels, Plano's specific designations help ensure staff are trained for the precise profile of students in their room.

One unique resource in Plano is the "Lift Off to Learning" Help Line (469-752-4299), specifically designed for Child Find inquiries. If you are new to the district or suspect a disability, this is your direct line to the assessment team, bypassing the need to navigate complex campus phone trees.
