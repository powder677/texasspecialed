Answer: In Plano ISD, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education meeting where parents and district staff collaborate to determine a student's eligibility, placement, and individualized services. Parents must receive at least five school days' written notice, have the right to audio record the meeting, and are recognized as equal members of the ARD committee.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Plano ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Plano ISD

  • Notice Timeline: Minimum of 5 school days' written notice prior to the meeting.
  • Recording Rights: Parents may audio record meetings but should provide advance notice to allow the district to record as well.
  • Parent Role: Parents are mandatory, equal members of the committee with shared decision-making power.
  • Annual Requirement: The committee must meet at least once every 12 months to review the IEP.
  • Initial ARD: Must be held within 30 calendar days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

Common ARD Questions in Plano ISD

Can I request an ARD meeting at any time in Plano ISD?

Yes. Parents in Plano ISD may request an ARD committee meeting at any time to discuss educational concerns, placement, or IEP goals. The school must either grant the request or provide written notice within five school days explaining why they refuse to convene a meeting.

What happens if I disagree with the Plano ISD ARD committee?

If the committee cannot reach a mutual agreement, Plano ISD must offer a 10-school-day recess to allow for data collection and further discussion. If disagreement persists after reconvening, the district may implement the IEP it deems appropriate while providing parents with notice of their dispute resolution rights.

Will I receive a draft of the IEP before the meeting?

In Plano ISD, it is a standard practice to send parents a copy of the draft IEP, particularly the Present Levels (PLAAFP) and proposed goals, prior to the ARD meeting. This allows parents to review data and prepare questions in advance.

How to Prepare for an ARD Meeting in Plano ISD

Plano ISD families can use these steps to effectively navigate the special education process.

1. Review the PLAAFP and Draft Goals

Request and review the draft "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance" (PLAAFP) and goals from your Plano ISD campus team. Ensure these statements accurately reflect your child's current classroom performance and your home observations.

2. Prepare Your Parent Input Statement

Draft a "Parent Vision Statement" or a list of specific concerns regarding your child's academic or functional needs. Plano ISD is required to consider parent input when developing the IEP.

3. Notify the Campus of Additional Attendees

If you plan to bring an advocate, private therapist, or attorney to the meeting, notify the Plano ISD campus principal or ARD facilitator in advance. This ensures the district can have the appropriate personnel present to maintain professional conduct and address your concerns.

How ARD Meetings in Plano ISD May Differ From Other Districts

As a high-performing district in North Texas, Plano ISD has specific structural tendencies in their special education department:

  • Draft Documentation: Plano ISD emphasizes providing draft documents to parents as a "facilitation" strategy to ensure meetings are conducted efficiently.
  • Administrative Attendance: In Plano ISD, if a parent brings an attorney, the Director of Special Education or district general counsel may attend, which may require rescheduling the meeting to a mutually agreeable time.
  • Continuum of Services: Because Plano ISD offers a wide range of specialized programs (e.g., centralized classes vs. inclusion), the ARD committee focus is often heavily weighted on finding the "Least Restrictive Environment" within this specific district continuum.
