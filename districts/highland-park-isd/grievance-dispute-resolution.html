File a Special Education Complaint in Highland Park ISD

Understand your dispute resolution options in Highland Park Independent School District, which serves the Park Cities area of Dallas. Learn the escalation process, know your rights, and access templates for HPISD's Board Policy FNG grievance structure.

Quick Answer: To file a special education complaint in Highland Park ISD, start with your campus ARD chair or principal. If unresolved, escalate to the district level by submitting a written Level One grievance under Policy FNG to the campus principal. You can also file directly with the Texas Education Agency (TEA) or request mediation. Highland Park ISD, which serves approximately 6,500-7,000 students in the Dallas Metroplex, follows a formal three-level grievance process and routes special education disputes through the Director of Special Programs.

Quick Facts: Dispute Resolution in Highland Park ISD

  • Your Rights: You can disagree with any ARD decision without fear of retaliation from HPISD staff.
  • Start Here: Informal campus-level resolution with the Special Education Coordinator or Principal.
  • Escalate To: Formal Level One Grievance (Policy FNG) within 15 business days.
  • State Option: File a formal TEA complaint within 1 year of the alleged violation.
  • Legal Option: Request a Special Education Due Process Hearing (legal counsel advised).

Common Questions About Highland Park ISD Dispute Resolution

How do I file a special education complaint in HPISD?

Start at campus level. Contact your child's ARD committee chair or campus principal first to attempt informal resolution. If the issue is not resolved within 10 business days, you must submit a formal Level One written grievance. Highland Park ISD requires this to be filed with the campus principal, who will then schedule a formal conference to hear your concerns. HPISD assigns a case manager from the Special Programs office to oversee these formal disputes.

What is the difference between a district complaint and a TEA complaint?

District complaints are handled internally; TEA complaints trigger state investigation. A district complaint is filed directly with Highland Park ISD and follows Board Policy FNG (LOCAL). A TEA (Texas Education Agency) complaint is a formal state-level investigation into violations of IDEA. In HPISD, parents are encouraged to use the local grievance process first to resolve issues quickly, but you have the right to file with TEA at any time.

What if the school is not following my child's IEP?

Document the failure and request an ARD meeting. If Highland Park ISD is failing to provide services (like speech therapy or inclusion minutes), document every instance. If an ARD meeting does not solve the implementation gap, your next step is a formal grievance. HPISD typically uses an internal audit system to track service delivery once a formal complaint is filed regarding service implementation.

How do I request mediation in HPISD?

Mediation is voluntary for both sides. You can request mediation through the TEA website or by asking the HPISD Special Programs Director. Because HPISD is part of Region 10, they utilize TEA-trained mediators who are neutral third parties. Mediation is often successful in HPISD when there is a disagreement over placement or specific IEP goals that has reached a stalemate in the ARD committee.

Dispute Resolution Escalation Path in Highland Park ISD

Follow this recommended sequence to resolve special education disputes in HPISD:

Level 1

Campus-Level Resolution

Contact: Your child's ARD chair or the Campus Special Education Coordinator.

Method: Request an informal meeting or "Parent-Teacher" conference to discuss specific IEP implementation issues.

Goal: Resolve the issue without formal paperwork by making adjustments to the current ARD implementation.

Level 2

District-Level Grievance (Policy FNG)

Method: Submit the HPISD Level One Grievance form to the Principal.

Contact: Dr. Cynthia Hollingshead, Director of Special Programs

Address: 7015 Westchester Dr, Dallas, TX 75205

Phone: (214) 780-3000

Timeline: You must usually file within 15 days of the incident. The district will respond in writing following a conference.

Level 3

State-Level Dispute

TEA Complaint: File online if HPISD violates a specific legal requirement (e.g., missed evaluation timelines).

Stay Put: In Due Process cases, your child remains in their current placement while the dispute is heard.

Protection Against Retaliation

Parents who file complaints in Highland Park ISD are protected by Section 504 and IDEA from retaliation.

  • ✓ HPISD cannot reduce services because you filed a complaint.
  • ✓ Staff cannot bar you from campus or ARD participation.
  • ✓ Your child cannot be disciplined more harshly due to your advocacy.
