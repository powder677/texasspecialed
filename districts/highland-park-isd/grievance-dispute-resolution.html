⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Many complaints have strict filing deadlines. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. District grievances (Policy FNG) often have much shorter deadlines, typically requiring a Level One filing within 15 business days of the incident.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Highland Park ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Highland Park ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or ARD committee decisions.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's Individualized Education Program (IEP) lists specific services like speech therapy or inclusion support, but Highland Park ISD is failing to implement them.

Evaluation Delays

The district has failed to meet the 45-school-day evaluation timeline or refused to evaluate a suspected disability without valid justification.

ARD Meeting Issues

HPISD refuses to hold an ARD meeting, excludes you from decision-making, or ignores independent evaluations.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child needs a more or less restrictive setting (LRE) than what the ARD committee is proposing.

Discrimination Concerns

Your child is being treated differently because of their disability, including unfair discipline or denial of access to extracurricular activities.

Retaliation

You feel district staff are retaliating against you or your student for advocating for special education rights.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

Informal resolution is often the fastest way to solve problems while maintaining a cooperative relationship with the school. Consider:

  • Meeting directly with the teacher or campus Special Education Coordinator.
  • Contacting the HPISD Director of Special Programs, Dr. Cynthia Hollingshead, at (214) 780-3090.
  • Requesting a formal ARD meeting to document your concerns in the official record.
  • Utilizing a local or TEA-provided independent IEP facilitator.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Level 1

District Grievance (Policy FNG Local)

Best for: Concerns regarding specific campus staff, communication, or local implementation.

Timeline: Typically 15 days to file a Level One grievance.

Cost: Free.

Binding: No; decisions can be appealed to the Superintendent and then the Board of Trustees.

Learn about HPISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Alleging violations of IDEA federal law or state special education rules.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes; TEA can order corrective actions like compensatory services.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: Mutually reaching a compromise with a neutral third party.

Timeline: Voluntary; available at any time if both parties agree.

Cost: Free (TEA pays for the mediator).

Binding: Only if a written agreement is signed by both parties.

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Disagreements on eligibility, placement, or whether a child is receiving a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE).

Timeline: File within 1 year; results in a trial-like hearing.

Cost: May require hiring a private attorney.

Binding: Yes; the hearing officer's decision is legally binding.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Disability-based discrimination, accessibility, or Section 504 violations.

Timeline: File within 180 days of the alleged discrimination.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes; OCR can enforce compliance through federal oversight.

Learn about OCR complaints ↓

The Highland Park ISD Local Grievance Process

Board Policy FNG (LOCAL) governs complaints by students and parents. This process is designed for issues that can be resolved by campus or district administrators.

Process Overview:

  • Level One: Formal written complaint to the campus principal. A conference is held within 10 days of the filing.
  • Level Two: If unresolved, appeal to the Superintendent's designee. They will review the Level One record and provide a written response.
  • Level Three: Final appeal to the HPISD Board of Trustees. The Board will hear the appeal in a public or closed meeting and make a final local decision.

Filing a TEA Special Education Complaint

A TEA complaint alleges that Highland Park ISD has violated a requirement of IDEA or state special education rules.

Filing Requirements:

  • The complaint must be in writing and signed.
  • Include the specific violation and the facts supporting it.
  • Include a proposed resolution.
  • You must send a copy of the complaint to HPISD at the same time you send it to TEA.

TEA Contact: spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov | Fax: (512) 463-9560.

Due Process Hearings

Due process is the most formal dispute resolution method, involving legal testimony and evidence presented before an Independent Hearing Officer.

Resolution Session

After filing, the district must hold a resolution meeting within 15 days to try and settle the case before the hearing begins.

Stay Put Provision

During a due process proceeding, the student generally remains in their current educational placement (the "stay put") unless both parties agree otherwise.

OCR Complaints (Section 504 & ADA)

Complaints involving discrimination or harassment based on disability are handled by the Office for Civil Rights. In Highland Park ISD, Dr. Cynthia Hollingshead serves as the District Section 504 Designee.

Common OCR issues include:

  • Denial of accommodations listed in a 504 plan.
  • Disproportionate suspension of students with disabilities.
  • Failure to provide an accessible learning environment.

Crucial Documentation for Disputes

Evidence is the foundation of any successful grievance. Maintain an organized file including:

Communication Logs: Dates, names, and summaries of all phone calls and emails.
Prior Written Notice (PWN): Any document from HPISD explaining why they refused or initiated a change in services.
The IEP Record: All versions of your child's ARD documents and progress reports.
Work Samples: Tangible evidence if you are claiming a lack of progress.
