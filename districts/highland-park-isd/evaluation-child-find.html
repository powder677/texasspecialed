How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Highland Park ISD

A step-by-step guide for parents in Dallas/Park Cities to secure a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) for their child.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under the federal Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA), Highland Park ISD (HPISD) is legally mandated to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with suspected disabilities within its boundaries. This "Child Find" responsibility includes children attending private schools within the Park Cities or those who are homeschooled.

Your Legal Protections:

  • The right to request an evaluation regardless of whether your child is passing their classes.
  • The right to a written response (Prior Written Notice) within 15 school days of your request.
  • The right to have your child tested in their native language.
  • The right to an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at district expense if you disagree with the HPISD assessment.

No Diagnosis Required

In Highland Park ISD, you do not need a private doctor's diagnosis of ADHD, Dyslexia, or Autism to start the process. The school's evaluation is designed to determine "educational eligibility," which is a separate legal standard from a medical diagnosis.

2

The 15-Day Response

HPISD must respond within 15 school days. They will either:

  • Provide you with "Prior Written Notice" (PWN) explaining why they agree to evaluate, along with consent forms.
  • Provide you with PWN explaining why they refuse to evaluate, along with your Procedural Safeguards.
3

Signing Consent

The 45-school-day timeline for completion does not start until HPISD receives your signed, written consent.

Pro-Tip: Review the "areas of suspected disability" listed on the consent form. Ensure it includes everything you are concerned about (e.g., speech, social skills, or dyslexia).
4

The Evaluation (FIIE)

HPISD professionals will conduct the Full Individual and Initial Evaluation. Per HB 3928, all dyslexia evaluations in Highland Park must now be conducted under the special education (IDEA) framework. Evaluations may include:

  • Intelligence and cognitive processing.
  • Academic achievement in reading, math, and writing.
  • Social-emotional and behavioral health.
  • Speech/Language or physical therapy assessments.
5

The Initial ARD Meeting

Once the report is finished, HPISD has 30 calendar days to hold the ARD meeting to discuss eligibility. You are a required member of this committee.

FAQs for Highland Park Families

Can the school use RTI to delay my evaluation?

No. Under Child Find, a district cannot use "Response to Intervention" (RTI) to delay or deny an evaluation for a child suspected of having a disability.

What if my child attends a private school in Dallas?

HPISD is responsible for evaluating children in private schools located within district boundaries. Contact the Special Education Director's office for private school evaluation protocols.

How has HB 3928 changed Dyslexia testing?

It mandates that all dyslexia evaluations happen via Special Education (IDEA), ensuring children in HPISD get full legal protections and the potential for a customized Individualized Education Program (IEP).
