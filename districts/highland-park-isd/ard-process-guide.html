Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Highland Park ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas specific terminology).
  • These meetings occur at least once every 12 months to update your child's educational program.
  • You are an equal partner in the committee; your input on your child's goals is legally protected.
  • Parents have the right to be accompanied by an advocate, therapist, or legal counsel.
  • An ARD can be requested at any time if you notice a lack of progress or new educational needs.

ARD Meeting Guide for Highland Park ISD Parents

Navigating special education in the Park Cities requires a clear understanding of the ARD process. This guide prepares Highland Park ISD families to advocate effectively for their child’s Individualized Education Program (IEP).

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have moved to Dallas from another state, you are likely familiar with "IEP Meetings." In Highland Park ISD and throughout Texas, this process is known as an Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee meeting.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial determination that a student qualifies for services.
  • Review: The ongoing evaluation of progress and adjustment of goals.
  • Dismissal: The formal process of exiting special education when a student no longer requires specialized instruction.

While the names differ, the goal remains the same: creating a legal document (the IEP) that ensures a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) for your student.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Highland Park ISD?

HPISD meetings are typically professional and data-driven. State law requires specific members to be present for the meeting to be valid.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are a mandatory member. Your knowledge of your child's history and strengths is vital to the committee's success.

Special Education Teacher

Usually your child's case manager, they propose academic goals and track modifications.

General Education Teacher

At least one teacher who understands the curriculum requirements for your child's grade level.

District Representative

A campus administrator (Principal or AP) who can authorize district resources and specialized services.

Diagnostician or LSSP

The professional who interprets evaluation results and explains how testing impacts the educational plan.

The Student

Students must be invited starting at age 14, though many HPISD parents choose to include them earlier to encourage self-advocacy.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocates: Professionals who assist in navigating the legal nuances of the ARD process.
  • Private Evaluators: If you have had private testing done, those professionals can attend to explain their findings.
  • Legal Counsel: You have a right to an attorney; HPISD usually requests 48 hours notice if an attorney will be present.
  • Emotional Support: A friend or relative to take notes or provide support during the meeting.

Unique ARD Features in Highland Park ISD

Highland Park ISD is known for its "Learner Profiles" and a focus on high achievement. Meetings often involve detailed discussions on how to balance rigorous academic standards with necessary modifications. For specific campus procedures, contact your school’s Special Education Campus Coordinator.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Highland Park ISD

A successful ARD starts long before the meeting begins. Preparation allows you to stay calm and focused on your child's needs.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review the previous IEP and the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Look over the most recent progress reports—are the percentages where they should be?
  • Gather work samples and independent reports from private therapists (Speech, OT, ABA).
  • Note any significant changes in behavior or health.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Identify your "Top 3" goals for your child for the coming year.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the school for the proposed goals at least 5 days early so you can review them in detail.
  • Write out your "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the formal record.
  • Notify the school of any outside attendees you have invited.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

In HPISD, meetings generally follow a formal agenda to ensure all legal compliance boxes are checked.

Typical Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: Everyone confirms their name and their role in the student's education.
  2. Evaluation Results: Reviewing any new testing or medical data.
  3. PLAAFPs: Developing the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance"—the baseline for all goals.
  4. Parent Input: This is your primary opportunity to voice your concerns and your child's strengths.
  5. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the next 12 months.
  6. Services & Accommodations: Deciding on support like extra time, shortened assignments, or specialized equipment.
  7. Placement: Deciding the "Least Restrictive Environment" (General Education, Resource, or Self-Contained).
  8. Signatures: The committee indicates whether they agree or disagree with the plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ Audio Recording: Texas law allows you to record the meeting. We recommend notifying the team as a courtesy.
  • ✓ Request Breaks: You can ask for a 10-minute recess to process information or consult with an advocate.
  • ✓ Ask for "Plain Language": If the team uses acronyms like "BIP" or "LRE" without explaining them, ask for clarification.
  • ✓ Right to Reschedule: If you feel you need more time to review new information, you can ask to recess and reconvene later.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

Once the meeting ends, the implementation phase begins. This is where the plan actually goes into action.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You agree with all parts of the plan. Services begin immediately on the date specified in the IEP.

Disagreement & Recess

If you disagree with a major part of the plan, the meeting is "recessed." You will meet again within 10 school days to try and reach a compromise.

Partial Agreement

In Texas, you can implement the parts of the IEP you agree with while moving to dispute resolution for the parts you don't.

Implementation Timeline:

The school must provide you with a final copy of the IEP within a reasonable timeframe. We suggest checking in with your child's teachers two weeks after the ARD to ensure accommodations are being utilized as written.
