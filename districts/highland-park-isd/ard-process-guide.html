Answer: In Highland Park ISD, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education process where parents and district staff determine a student's eligibility, services, and placement. Under Texas law, Highland Park ISD parents are equal members of the ARD committee, must receive at least five school days’ written notice, and have the right to audio record all proceedings.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Highland Park ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Highland Park ISD

  • Notice Timeline: Minimum 5 school days' written notice required.
  • Recording Rights: Parents may audio record; 24-48 hour courtesy notice is typically requested.
  • Parent Role: Mandatory, equal member of the ARD committee.
  • Annual Frequency: Required at least once every 12 months.
  • Initial ARD: Must occur within 30 days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

How ARD Meetings in Highland Park ISD Differ From Other Districts

Highland Park ISD operates with a high-achievement culture that influences the ARD process in several specific ways:

  • Learner Profiles: HPISD committee members often utilize "Learner Profiles" to bridge the gap between rigorous Park Cities academic standards and necessary IEP modifications.
  • Staffing Patterns: Meetings in HPISD frequently include both a Diagnostician and a Licensed Specialist in School Psychology (LSSP), especially when addressing twice-exceptional (2e) learners.
  • Centralized Coordination: Unlike larger districts, HPISD’s smaller size often allows for more direct involvement from Campus Special Education Coordinators during complex ARD transitions.

How to Prepare for an ARD Meeting in Highland Park ISD

Step 1: Request and Review Draft Documents

Request a draft copy of the proposed IEP goals and the most recent Evaluation (FIE) from your Highland Park ISD case manager at least five days before the meeting. Reviewing these ahead of time allows you to identify areas of disagreement early.

Step 2: Document Parent Concerns

Write a "Statement of Parent Concerns." Under Texas procedural safeguards, this statement must be included in the formal ARD deliberations to ensure your voice is part of the permanent record.

Step 3: Track Progress Data

Gather independent data, such as private therapy reports or work samples, to compare against the district’s progress monitoring. In HPISD, data-driven requests are the most effective way to secure service adjustments.

Common Highland Park ISD ARD Questions

Can I bring an advocate to a Highland Park ISD ARD?

Yes, parents have the legal right to bring advocates, private therapists, or legal counsel to any ARD meeting. Highland Park ISD typically requests 48 hours' notice if an attorney will be attending.

What happens if I disagree with the ARD committee's decision?

If a consensus is not reached, the meeting is typically recessed for no more than 10 school days. During this time, both the parent and Highland Park ISD staff may gather more data before reconvening to reach an agreement.
