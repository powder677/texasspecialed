Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Round Rock ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate or attorney
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time

ARD Meeting Guide for Round Rock ISD Parents

Serving families in Round Rock, Austin, and Cedar Park, this guide breaks down how to prepare for and participate in ARD committee meetings within Round Rock ISD.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently relocated to Central Texas, you may be familiar with "IEP Meetings." In Texas, we use the term ARD Committee, but the purpose remains the same: developing an Individualized Education Program (IEP) for your child.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial entry into special education.
  • Review: Annual or periodic check-ins on progress and goals.
  • Dismissal: Exiting special education when services are no longer required.

In Round Rock ISD, the ARD process is designed to be a collaborative partnership. The district believes that parents provide the most comprehensive perspective on the student's needs and long-term goals.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Round Rock ISD?

The committee is a multidisciplinary team designed to address every facet of your child's education.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are an equal member. Your input is required by law and essential for a successful outcome.

Special Education Teacher

Provides data on specialized instruction and progress toward academic or functional goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in general education settings.

District Representative

Typically a campus administrator (Principal or AP) who can commit district resources.

Evaluator (Diag/LSSP)

The Licensed Specialist in School Psychology (LSSP) or Diagnostician who interprets testing data.

The Student

Must be invited starting at age 14 for transition services; welcome earlier if appropriate.

Additional Support You Can Bring:

  • Professional Advocates: You may bring an educational consultant or advocate to help you navigate the law.
  • Private Providers: Outside therapists (OT, PT, Speech) who have relevant information about your child.
  • Spanish/Other Language Support: Round Rock ISD provides interpreters for ARD meetings; notify the school as soon as the meeting is scheduled.

District Tip: If you plan to record the meeting, it is common courtesy to notify the campus 24-48 hours in advance so they can also have recording equipment ready.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Round Rock ISD

Phase 1: Gathering Information

  • Review your child's current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Review work samples and testing data from the RRISD Parent Portal (Home Access Center).
  • Request a Draft IEP at least 5 school days before the meeting to avoid surprises.
  • Gather any recent private evaluations or doctor's reports.

Phase 2: Defining Your Goals

  • Write down your top 3 concerns for the school year.
  • List questions you want to ask the teacher or the diagnostician.
  • Determine if you want to request any new evaluations (like an FBA or AT evaluation).
  • Draft a short "Parent Statement" to be included in the official record.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

The Standard RRISD Meeting Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Everyone identifies their role in the committee.
  2. PLAAFP Review: Discussing "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance."
  3. Review of Progress: Looking at data from previous goals.
  4. Evaluation Review: Discussion of any new FIE (Full Individual Evaluation) data.
  5. Parent Concerns: This is your primary time to speak and have your concerns documented.
  6. Goal Development: Writing new, measurable goals for the coming year.
  7. Related Services: Determining minutes for Speech, OT, PT, or Counseling.
  8. Placement/LRE: Deciding the "Least Restrictive Environment" for the child.
  9. Consensus: Agreeing or disagreeing with the committee's decision.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break or a recess at any time.
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any educational jargon or acronyms.
  • ✓ Audio Recording: Texas law allows you to record the meeting. We suggest informing the committee at the start.
  • ✓ You have the right to disagree with the district's recommendations.
  • ✓ You can take the IEP home to review for up to 5 days before signing "Agree" or "Disagree."

Advocacy Tips:

  • Keep the conversation focused on the student's needs rather than staff availability.
  • Ask "What data supports this recommendation?"
  • If you disagree, ask for your specific reasons for disagreement to be written into the meeting minutes.
  • Stay calm but firm; you are your child's most important advocate.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

If Consensus is Reached:

Agreement

The new IEP is signed and becomes effective immediately (or on the specified start date). Ensure you receive a final digital or paper copy of the document.

If Consensus is NOT Reached:

The 10-Day Recess

In Texas, if you disagree, the committee must offer a 10-day recess. This allows everyone to gather more data and attempt to reach an agreement. If you still disagree after the reconvene, you have the right to move toward mediation.

Monitor Implementation:

The IEP is a legal document. Check that accommodations (like extra time or sensory breaks) are actually being provided in the classroom. If they aren't, request a "Brief ARD" to address the issue.

When is the Next ARD?

At a minimum, Round Rock ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually (the "Annual ARD").
  • Every three years (the "Triennial Re-evaluation").
  • Any time a parent or teacher requests one in writing.
