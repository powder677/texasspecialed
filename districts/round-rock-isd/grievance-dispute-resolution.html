⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Strict deadlines apply to all complaints. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. Local district grievances in Round Rock ISD (FNG Local) typically must be filed within 15 district business days of the time you knew or should have known of the issue. Do not wait if you believe your child's rights have been violated.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Round Rock ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Round Rock ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or IEP implementation.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP mandates specific services (e.g., speech therapy, counseling, dyslexia support) but RRISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

RRISD has missed the 45-school-day timeline for completing the Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) or refuses to evaluate without Prior Written Notice.

ARD Meeting Issues

The district refuses to convene an ARD meeting, excludes you from placement decisions, or fails to implement the "Stay Put" provision during a dispute.

Placement Disagreements

You disagree with the instructional arrangement (e.g., General Ed vs. Resource vs. Functional Academics) proposed by the district.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being excluded from extracurriculars, field trips, or other school activities specifically due to their disability.

Retaliation

You feel that campus staff are retaliating against you or your child because you have advocated for their special education rights.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

Informal resolution is often faster and less adversarial. Before filing forms, consider:

  • Meeting with the campus principal or Assistant Principal.
  • Requesting a staffing with the campus Department Chair or Lead Diagnostician.
  • Contacting the RRISD Special Education Department at (512) 464-5140.
  • Contacting the Executive Director of Special Education.
  • Requesting a reconvene ARD meeting to formally document your disagreement.

Tip: Always follow up verbal conversations with an email ("Just to confirm what we discussed..."). If these steps fail, proceed below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides a hierarchy of options for resolving special education disputes, ranging from local grievance policies to federal investigations:

Level 1

District Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Best for: Disputes about district policy, staff conduct, or failure to follow the IEP within RRISD.

Timeline: Must file within 15 district business days of the incident (verify current FNG Local policy).

Cost: Free

Binding: No - decisions can be appealed to the School Board and then to the TEA Commissioner.

Learn about RRISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of federal special education law (IDEA), missed timelines, or procedural errors.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of the violation; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds a violation, RRISD is legally required to correct it.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties are willing to compromise and want a neutral third party to facilitate.

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 30 days.

Cost: Free (TEA covers the cost of the mediator).

Binding: Agreements signed in mediation are legally binding contracts.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disagreements regarding eligibility, placement (LRE), or the appropriateness of the program (FAPE).

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year; hearing typically held within 45 days.

Cost: High (legal fees); families may be reimbursed for attorney fees if they win.

Binding: Yes - the Hearing Officer's decision is final (unless appealed to federal court).

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504/ADA) or retaliation.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the discrimination.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - OCR enforcement is federal.

Round Rock ISD Grievance Process (Level 1)

Round Rock ISD utilizes Board Policy FNG (LOCAL) for student and parent complaints. This formal process ensures your complaint is heard by administration, but strict adherence to the form and timeline is required.

Where to Submit Grievances

Address: Round Rock ISD Administration, 1311 Round Rock Avenue, Round Rock, TX 78681

Attention: Office of the Superintendent / Grievance Officer

Phone: (512) 464-5000

Note: We recommend submitting via certified mail or hand-delivering to the front desk and asking for a date-stamped receipt.

Step-by-Step Filing Guide

  1. Obtain the Level 1 Form

    You must use the official "Level One Student/Parent Complaint Form." This is typically available on the RRISD Policy Online site under FNG (Exhibit) or from the administration building.

  2. File Within 15 Days

    You must file the complaint within 15 district business days of the incident or the date you first knew of the problem. If you wait too long, the district can dismiss your grievance purely on timing.

  3. Level 1 Conference

    The appropriate administrator (often the Principal or a Director) will schedule a conference with you to hear the complaint. They must provide a written decision within 10 business days after this meeting.

  4. Appeals (Level 2 & 3)

    If the Level 1 decision does not resolve the issue, you can appeal to the Superintendent's designee (Level 2) and subsequently the Board of Trustees (Level 3). Appeals must be filed within 10 business days of receiving the prior decision.

Filing a Complaint with TEA

If you believe Round Rock ISD has violated federal or state special education laws (IDEA), you may file a complaint directly with the Texas Education Agency.

Common Violations TEA Investigates

  • District failure to implement the IEP (e.g., student not receiving specified minutes of service).
  • District failure to provide Prior Written Notice (PWN) before changing a placement.
  • District failure to meet evaluation timelines (45 school days).
  • District failure to include parents in the ARD meeting.

How to File:

  • Download the TEA Special Education Complaint Form.
  • Submit the form via email to: spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov
  • Crucial: You must forward a copy of the complaint to the RRISD Superintendent at the same time you send it to TEA.

Due Process Hearings

A Due Process Hearing is the most formal dispute resolution option. It acts like a courtroom trial where an independent Hearing Officer hears evidence and issues a legally binding decision.

Legal Counsel: While you are allowed to represent yourself ("pro se"), Due Process is highly technical. Most parents find it necessary to hire a special education attorney or a qualified advocate.

When is Due Process Used?

  • Eligibility Disputes: RRISD says your child does not have a disability, or has a different disability than you believe.
  • Placement (LRE): You believe your child needs a private placement or a more inclusive setting than offered.
  • FAPE Denial: The proposed IEP is not sufficient to allow your child to make meaningful progress.

Statute of Limitations: You have one year from the date of the alleged violation to request a hearing in Texas.

Office for Civil Rights (OCR)

The U.S. Department of Education Office for Civil Rights enforces Section 504 and the ADA. This is the correct venue for discrimination and civil rights issues, rather than educational programming disputes.

Grounds for OCR Complaints

  • Failure to provide disability-related accommodations (e.g., wheelchair access, large print).
  • Disability harassment or bullying that the school ignores.
  • Retaliation against a student or parent for asserting disability rights.
  • Different treatment in discipline (e.g., harsher suspensions for students with disabilities).

To file a complaint with OCR, you do not need to have a lawyer. The process is generally more accessible to parents than a due process hearing.

Documentation is Critical

In any dispute resolution process, the party with the best documentation usually prevails. Maintain a binder with:

  • All current and past IEPs and FIEs.
  • A communication log (dates, times, names, summary of conversations).
  • Printed copies of all emails (do not rely on them staying in your inbox).
  • Work samples, progress reports, and report cards.
  • Any audio recordings of ARD meetings (if you notified the district in advance).
