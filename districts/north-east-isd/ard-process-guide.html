Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in North East ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team).
  • Must be held at least once per year to review and revise your child's educational program.
  • Parents are equal members of the committee with equal standing to district staff.
  • Initial ARD meetings follow within 30 days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Texas law allows parents to record ARD meetings without school permission.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to San Antonio from another state, you might be looking for an "IEP meeting." In Texas, these are officially known as ARD Committee Meetings.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The process of determining eligibility and "admitting" a student to special education.
  • Review: The annual review of a student's progress and the effectiveness of the current IEP.
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting a student from special education when services are no longer required.

In North East ISD, this committee is responsible for creating and maintaining your child's Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Who Attends ARD Meetings in North East ISD?

The ARD committee includes several key members, each bringing a specific perspective to the table.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are an equal member. Your knowledge of your child’s history and daily life is essential for a complete plan.

Special Education Teacher

The provider responsible for specialized instruction and monitoring progress toward specific goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if your child is or may be participating in the general education classroom.

District Representative

A school administrator (Principal or Assistant Principal) who can authorize district resources.

Evaluator

A Diagnostician or LSSP who interprets evaluation results and testing data.

The Student

Required when transition services are discussed (age 14+) and encouraged for self-advocacy.

Additional Attendees You May Bring:

  • Advocate: Someone knowledgeable about special education law to help you navigate the process.
  • Attorney: You have the right to legal counsel. (Note: It is polite to notify NEISD if an attorney will attend).
  • Outside Specialists: Private therapists or physicians who can share relevant data.
  • Translators: NEISD must provide an interpreter if you are not fluent in English.

How NEISD Conducts ARD Meetings

North East ISD typically holds meetings on the student's campus. Depending on the campus, you may work with a dedicated **ARD Facilitator** who helps manage the documentation and keeps the meeting focused on the student's needs. Virtual attendance options are commonly available for parent convenience.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in North East ISD

Preparing beforehand ensures that you can advocate effectively and that the meeting remains focused on your child's progress.

2 Weeks Before: Information Gathering

  • Review the current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Gather recent work samples, report cards, and any private evaluations.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Ask your child what they feel is working well and what is difficult in class.

1 Week Before: Setting Priorities

  • Draft a "Parent Concerns" statement for the meeting minutes.
  • Identify 3-5 specific changes or goals you want to discuss.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a copy of proposed goals to review in advance.
  • Confirm the attendance of your advocate or support person.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Meetings follow a legal agenda to ensure every necessary part of your child's education is covered.

The Typical ARD Agenda in NEISD:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and ensuring all required members are present.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Sharing your concerns, observations, and vision for your child.
  4. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the coming year.
  5. Services & Accommodations: Determining modifications, classroom support, and therapy minutes.
  6. Review of Minutes: Checking the "deliberations" (meeting notes) for accuracy before signing.

Your Rights at the Table:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point.
  • ✓ You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee reaches a stalemate.
  • ✓ You can audio record the meeting (Texas allows this without school consent).
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any term or acronym (e.g., BIP, LRE, FAPE).
  • ✓ You can disagree with recommendations and have it noted in the record.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting is the start of the new implementation phase.

Full Agreement

Services usually begin within 5-10 school days or on the start date listed in the IEP.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to some parts while disagreeing with others. Agreed-upon services start while you resolve the dispute.

Formal Disagreement

If agreement is not reached, you can explore mediation, file a grievance, or request a due process hearing.

Meeting Frequency

North East ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: At minimum, once every 12 months.
  • Triennially: Every 3 years for a full reevaluation (REED process).
  • On Request: Parents can request a meeting at any time to discuss concerns.
