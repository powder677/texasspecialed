Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Humble ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate or attorney
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time

ARD Meeting Guide for Humble ISD Parents

Everything you need to know about preparing for and participating in ARD committee meetings in Humble ISD, Texas.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you're new to the Houston metro area or special education in general, you may have heard the term "IEP meeting" used in other states or on national advocacy websites. In Texas, we use specific terminology that defines the committee's purpose.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: Determining if a student is eligible for special education and admitting them into the program.
  • Review: Reviewing the student's progress, the effectiveness of the Individualized Education Program (IEP), and making necessary adjustments.
  • Dismissal: Deciding when a student no longer requires special education services based on comprehensive evaluation data.

The result of an ARD meeting is the IEP (Individualized Education Program). In Humble ISD, this committee is the legal engine that drives your child's educational supports. Understanding this process is the first step toward effective advocacy.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Humble ISD?

The makeup of the ARD committee is strictly defined by law to ensure that all necessary perspectives—instructional, administrative, and parental—are represented.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

That's you! You are an equal member of the ARD committee. Your input carries the same weight as district staff. You are the expert on your child's life outside of the classroom.

Special Education Teacher

This individual provides direct input on specialized instruction, accommodations, and progress toward specific IEP goals. They are often the primary point of contact for your child's services.

General Education Teacher

A general education teacher is required if your child spends any time in general education classes, or if the student is being considered for participation in general education.

District Representative

Typically a campus administrator (principal or AP) who has the authority to commit Humble ISD resources and ensures that the services in the IEP can actually be delivered.

Diagnostician or Psychologist

Commonly referred to as the "Diag" in Humble ISD, this person interprets evaluation results and explains what the testing data means for your child’s learning profile.

Your Child (if appropriate)

In Texas, students must be invited when transition services are discussed (starting at age 14). However, Humble ISD parents often find it helpful for students to participate even earlier to build self-advocacy skills.

You May Also Bring:

You have the right to bring individuals who have knowledge or special expertise regarding your child. In Humble ISD, it is common for parents to include:

  • Advocate or Educational Consultant: A professional who understands Texas special education law and can help you navigate disagreements or complex technical language.
  • Attorney: You have the legal right to bring counsel. If you plan to do so, Humble ISD requires advance notice so they can also have their legal representation present.
  • Outside Evaluator: If you have had a private Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) or your child sees a private speech therapist or ABA provider, their input can be invaluable.
  • Family Member or Friend: ARD meetings can be emotional. Having a "second set of ears" to take notes and provide support is highly recommended.

Note: While not strictly required, it is a courtesy to inform the Humble ISD ARD facilitator in advance if you are bringing additional people so they can ensure the room or virtual meeting link is prepared.

How Humble ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Humble ISD typically holds ARD meetings on the student's home campus. Since 2020, the district has maintained robust virtual options via Zoom or Microsoft Teams, allowing parents to participate from home or work. Most Humble ISD campuses utilize a "Facilitated ARD" approach for complex meetings, where a neutral party helps manage the conversation to keep it focused on the student's needs.

For specific questions about your school's scheduling window or the specific staff involved, you can contact the Humble ISD special education department.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Humble ISD

Preparation is the difference between a meeting where you are simply "told" what will happen and a meeting where you "collaborate" on what should happen.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review your child's current IEP in detail—what was promised last year?
  • Review recent progress reports to see if goals are actually being met.
  • Gather recent report cards and any district-wide testing scores (like STAAR or MAP).
  • Collect work samples from your child's backpack that show areas of struggle or success.
  • Review any private evaluations or therapist reports you've received recently.
  • Note any changes in your child's behavior, sleep, or medication.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Write down your top 3-5 concerns or goals for the coming year.
  • List specific questions you want the "Diag" or the teachers to answer.
  • Identify any services (like Speech, OT, or Counseling) you feel should be adjusted.
  • Decide if you are bringing an advocate and notify the school.
  • Crucial: Request a "Draft IEP" from your Humble ISD case manager so you aren't reading the goals for the first time during the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Walking into a room full of professionals can be intimidating. Knowing the "script" of a typical Humble ISD ARD meeting can help you stay grounded.

Typical Meeting Flow in Humble ISD:

  1. Introductions - The facilitator will start the meeting and everyone will state their role.
  2. Review of Current Status (PLAAFP) - The team discusses the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance." This is the foundation of the whole IEP.
  3. Evaluation Results - If it's a 3-year reevaluation year, the diagnostician will present the FIE.
  4. Parent Input - This is your dedicated time to share your concerns. Do not let the team skip over this.
  5. Discussion of Services - The committee discusses accommodations (like extra time) and modifications (changing the curriculum).
  6. IEP Development - The committee reviews proposed goals and determines the "Placement" (where the services will be delivered).
  7. Consensus Building - The facilitator will check to see if everyone is in agreement.
  8. Signature and Next Steps - You will be asked to sign the document indicating your participation and agreement/disagreement.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ Breaks: You can ask for a 5-minute break at any time if the conversation becomes overwhelming.
  • ✓ Rescheduling: If the meeting is running too long and you have to leave, you can request it be recessed and finished at a later date.
  • ✓ Plain Language: You have the right to ask the team to stop using acronyms (like BIP, FBA, or LRE) and explain things in plain English.
  • ✓ Disagreement: You have the right to disagree. Disagreement does not make you a "difficult parent"; it's a legal right.
  • ✓ Recording: In Texas, you have the right to audio record the ARD meeting. You do not need the district's permission, though it is standard practice to notify them that you are doing so.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Ask if someone can take the official minutes for you so you can focus on the conversation.
  • Use "I" statements: "I've noticed that homework is taking 4 hours, which makes me concerned the accommodations aren't working."
  • Stay focused on the "Data." If the school says your child is doing "great," ask to see the progress monitoring data that proves it.
  • Ask for specific, measurable goals. Instead of "John will improve reading," look for "John will read 100 words per minute with 90% accuracy."
  • Don't feel pressured to sign immediately. You have the right to take the IEP home and review it for a day before making a final decision.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting is over, but the work of ensuring your child's success continues. What happens next depends on the outcome of the meeting.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You and Humble ISD agree on all aspects of the IEP. You sign "Agree," and the new services or accommodations typically begin within 5-10 school days.

Partial Agreement

You might agree with the goals but disagree with the "Placement" (the setting where services are provided). In Texas, you can sign "Agree" to the parts you like so they start immediately, while formally noting your disagreement on the specific sections you dispute.

Full Disagreement

If there is no consensus, the ARD committee can offer a "10-day recess." This gives everyone a chance to gather more data or cool off before trying to reach an agreement again. If you still disagree after the reconvened meeting, you can move toward formal dispute resolution.

Implementation Timeline:

Once the IEP is finalized, Humble ISD is legally bound to implement it as written. We recommend checking in with your child's teachers two weeks after the meeting to ensure they have received the new "IEP at a Glance" and understand the updated accommodations.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

At a minimum, Humble ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: To review and update the IEP (the "Annual ARD").
  • Triennially: Every three years for a full reevaluation.
  • Upon Request: You can request an ARD meeting at any time if you feel the current plan isn't working. The district has 15 school days to respond to your request.
