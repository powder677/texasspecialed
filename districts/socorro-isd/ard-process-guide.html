Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Socorro ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Meetings are required annually to review your child's educational program
  • Parental Partnership: You are a legal, equal member of the decision-making team
  • Socorro ISD utilizes a collaborative approach to develop individualized goals
  • You have the right to request an ARD meeting at any time during the school year

ARD Meeting Guide for Socorro ISD Parents

Serving families in the El Paso area, this guide explains how Socorro ISD conducts ARD committee meetings and how you can be your child's best advocate.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

In most parts of the country, the group that writes a student's educational plan is called the "IEP Team." In West Texas and across the state, we use the term ARD Committee.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD represents the committee's purpose:

  • Admission: Determining eligibility for special education services.
  • Review: An annual review of progress, goals, and the need for accommodations.
  • Dismissal: Deciding if a child no longer requires specialized services based on data.

In Socorro ISD, the ARD committee creates the Individualized Education Program (IEP), which is the legal contract between the school district and the family regarding the student's education.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Socorro ISD?

The committee is composed of individuals with various areas of expertise to ensure all of your child's needs are addressed.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

The Expert on the Child. Your role is to provide the team with information about your child’s life, strengths, and challenges outside of school.

Special Education Teacher

The service provider who implements specialized instruction and tracks progress on IEP goals.

General Education Teacher

Ensures that the student has access to the general curriculum (TEKS) and grade-level standards.

Administrator

A campus leader who can authorize district resources and ensures legal compliance.

Evaluator (Diag/LSSP)

The Educational Diagnostician or Psychologist who explains evaluation results and testing data.

The Student

Encouraged to attend whenever appropriate, especially for transition planning (ages 14+).

Others Who May Attend:

  • Related Service Providers: Speech pathologists, Occupational Therapists (OT), or Physical Therapists (PT).
  • Advocates: You have the right to bring a professional advocate or educational consultant.
  • Translators: Socorro ISD provides Spanish/English interpretation services for ARD meetings upon request.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Socorro ISD

Pre-Meeting Steps

  • Review your child's previous IEP and current progress reports.
  • Review work samples and grades through the SISD Tyler Parent Portal.
  • Request a Draft IEP from the teacher 3-5 days before the meeting to review proposed goals.
  • Gather any private evaluations or medical reports from doctors or therapists in El Paso.

Documenting Your Concerns

  • Write down a list of your child’s successes and your current concerns.
  • List questions regarding specific accommodations or service minutes.
  • Think about "Transition" goals if your child is approaching high school.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Typical Meeting Agenda in Socorro ISD:

  1. Introductions: Establishing the roles of each committee member.
  2. PLAAFP Review: Discussing the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance."
  3. New Evaluation: If a re-evaluation was completed, the Diag presents the findings.
  4. Parent Comments: Your opportunity to voice concerns and priorities.
  5. Annual Goals: Reviewing and revising the academic and functional goals.
  6. Related Services: Determining the frequency and duration of services like Speech or OT.
  7. LRE/Placement: Deciding the "Least Restrictive Environment" for the student.
  8. Consensus: The team works to reach a mutual agreement on the plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break if the meeting feels overwhelming.
  • ✓ You can ask for the meeting to be recessed and finished at another time.
  • ✓ You can request clarification on any acronyms or "Edu-speak."
  • ✓ Recording: You are allowed to audio record the meeting. It is best practice to notify the school at the start.
  • ✓ You have the right to disagree with any part of the school's proposal.

Communication Tips:

  • Keep the focus on the student's needs and the data.
  • Ask "What does this service look like in the classroom?"
  • If you disagree, ask the district to provide the data that supports their recommendation.
  • Remember that you don't have to sign "Agree" immediately if you need time to think.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

Outcomes of the Meeting:

Full Agreement

The committee agrees on the entire IEP. Services begin as scheduled, and you receive a final copy of the document.

Partial Agreement

You may agree with the goals but disagree with the placement. You can sign "Agree" to implement the goals while documenting your disagreement on the placement.

Disagreement and Recess

In Texas, if there is a disagreement, the committee must offer a 10-day recess. This allows both parties to cool off, gather more information, and try to reach consensus at a later date.

Monitor Implementation:

Check in with your child's teachers a few weeks after the ARD. Ensure that the new accommodations are being used and that your child is adjusting well to any changes in their schedule or services. If progress isn't happening, you can always request a new ARD meeting.
