Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Killeen ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas specific name for IEP team).
  • Mandated at least once a year to review progress and set new educational goals.
  • Parental Equality: You are a legally recognized, equal member of the decision-making team.
  • Killeen ISD often supports highly mobile military families from Fort Cavazos (formerly Fort Hood).
  • You have the right to request an ARD meeting at any time in writing.

ARD Meeting Guide for Killeen ISD Parents

Serving Killeen, Harker Heights, and Nolanville. This guide empowers Killeen ISD families to navigate the ARD committee process with confidence and clarity.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently PCSed to Central Texas or moved from another state, the term "ARD" may be new. Outside of Texas, this is commonly referred to as an "IEP Meeting."

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD represents the three main stages of the process:

  • Admission: Determining if a student is eligible for special education.
  • Review: An annual check-in to review progress and modify the plan.
  • Dismissal: Deciding when a student no longer requires specialized services.

In Killeen ISD, the ARD committee is responsible for creating the Individualized Education Program (IEP). This document is a legal contract that ensures your child receives the specific supports they need to succeed in school.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Killeen ISD?

The committee consists of a dedicated group of professionals and the family, all focused on the student's success.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

The Child's Best Advocate. You provide the essential context about your child’s history and needs outside the classroom.

Special Education Teacher

The primary provider who monitors and implements the specialized instruction described in the IEP.

General Education Teacher

Ensures the student has access to grade-level curriculum and explains how accommodations work in a large classroom setting.

Campus Administrator

A Principal or Assistant Principal who can commit Killeen ISD resources and ensure legal compliance.

Diagnostician or LSSP

The professional who explains testing data and helps the team understand how the child's disability impacts learning.

The Student

Strongly encouraged to attend to share their own goals, especially during transition planning (ages 14+).

Additional Support:

  • Military Liaison: For families at Fort Cavazos, an EFMP (Exceptional Family Member Program) representative can sometimes offer support.
  • Related Service Providers: Speech Pathologists, OTs, or PTs as needed.
  • Advocates: You are legally allowed to bring a professional advocate or educational consultant.

Notification: Killeen ISD asks that you notify them in advance if you plan to bring an advocate or attorney so they can ensure the meeting room is appropriately sized.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Killeen ISD

Phase 1: Review and Gather

  • Review your child's current IEP goals and the latest progress reports.
  • Review report cards and teacher notes via the KISD Parent Portal (Home Access Center).
  • Gather any private evaluations or military medical records (EFMP data) to share.
  • Request a Draft IEP from the campus at least 3-5 days before the meeting.

Phase 2: Defining Priorities

  • Identify your top 3 academic or social concerns for the coming school year.
  • Prepare a list of specific questions about accommodations or service minutes.
  • Draft a "Parent Statement" to ensure your voice is officially recorded in the minutes.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

A Typical Killeen ISD Meeting Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Establishing the role of each member.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of the student's "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance."
  3. Evaluation Review: Discussion of any new testing or FIE (Full Individual Evaluation) results.
  4. Parent Input: Your opportunity to share concerns, home observations, and goals.
  5. Goal Review and Revision: Writing the new, measurable goals for the IEP.
  6. Services and Accommodations: Finalizing the time spent in specialized instruction and related services.
  7. Placement/LRE: Determining the "Least Restrictive Environment" for the student's success.
  8. Consensus: The committee works to reach an agreement on the proposed plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a short break if you need to gather your thoughts.
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any educational jargon or acronyms.
  • ✓ Recording: Texas law allows audio recording of the meeting. We suggest notifying the team at the beginning.
  • ✓ You have the right to disagree with any portion of the district's proposal.
  • ✓ You can take the IEP document home to review for up to 5 days before signing.

Communication Tips:

  • Ask "What does the data show?" when discussing goal progress.
  • Stay student-focused; use "I" statements to share your perspective.
  • If you disagree, ensure your specific reasons are captured in the "Minutes" or "Deliberations" section.
  • Collaborate: View the school staff as partners in your child's success.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The Meeting Outcomes:

Full Agreement

The committee reaches a consensus. The IEP is implemented, and you receive a final copy. Services typically begin on the start date listed in the document.

Partial Agreement

You may agree with some services but not others. In Texas, you can sign "Agree" for the parts you support so they start immediately, while noting your disagreement on others.

Disagreement and Recess

If no agreement is reached, the committee must offer a 10-day recess. This allows everyone time to gather more data and meet again to attempt to reach a resolution.

Monitor Progress:

Check your child's progress reports every 9 weeks. If you notice your child isn't meeting their goals, you don't have to wait for the annual ARD. You can request a "Brief ARD" in writing to review and adjust the IEP at any time.
