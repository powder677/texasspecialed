About Fort Bend ISD Special Education

Fort Bend ISD serves a diverse community across Sugar Land, Missouri City, and surrounding areas. The district's special education department is notable for its active Special Education Parent Advisory Committee (SEPAC), which includes parent representatives from various campuses. The SEPAC's mission is to advise the district on policy and training needs, giving you a structured voice in how services are delivered.

The district offers a continuum of placement options, ranging from mainstream inclusion support to specialized self-contained programs. Specific programs you may encounter include FLASH (Functional Living and School Health) for students with significant medical or cognitive needs, and ABC (Academic Behavior Class) for students requiring intensive behavioral support. Fort Bend ISD also utilizes the M.R. Wood Center for Learning as a specialized campus for students who need a smaller, more therapeutic environment than a traditional comprehensive campus can provide.

Navigating the chain of command in Fort Bend ISD is critical for resolving issues effectively. The department is organized by program and grade level, with specific Program Managers assigned to oversee areas like Autism, Behavior, or Evaluation. If you have a disagreement with your campus ARD committee, contacting the specific Program Manager for your child's instructional arrangement (e.g., the Program Manager for Elementary ABC) is often the most effective next step before escalating to a Director.

Recent district initiatives have focused on improving the "Change of Placement" process to ensure data-driven decision-making. This helps protect your child's right to the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE) by ensuring that moves to more restrictive settings, such as a localized program, are based on documented needs rather than administrative convenience.
