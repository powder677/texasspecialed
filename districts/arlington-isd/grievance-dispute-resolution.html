File a Special Education Complaint in Arlington ISD

Understand your dispute resolution options in Arlington Independent School District, which serves approximately 55,000-60,000 students in the urban Dallas-Fort Worth metro area. Learn the escalation process, know your rights, and access templates for Arlington ISD's campus-first resolution and formal grievance structure.

Quick Answer: To file a special education complaint in Arlington ISD, start with your campus ARD chair or principal. If unresolved, escalate to the district level by submitting a Level One grievance form to the Human Resources Employee Relations department at HREmployeeRelations@aisd.net. You can also file directly with the Texas Education Agency (TEA) or request mediation. Arlington ISD, which serves a significant special education population in Tarrant County, utilizes a formal multi-level appeal process (FNG Local) and routes complaints through the Specialized Learning Services department to ensure legal compliance.

Quick Facts: Dispute Resolution in Arlington ISD

  • Your Rights: You can disagree with any ARD decision without retaliation under IDEA.
  • Start Here: Campus-level resolution with the ARD chair or Special Education Coordinator.
  • Escalate To: Arlington ISD Human Resources / Employee Relations (Grievance Portal).
  • State Option: File a formal TEA complaint within 1 year of the incident.
  • Legal Option: Due process hearing with an Administrative Law Judge.

Common Questions About Arlington ISD Dispute Resolution

How do I file a special education complaint in Arlington ISD?

Start at campus level. Contact your child's ARD committee chair or campus principal first to attempt informal resolution. If the issue is not resolved within 15 district business days, escalate to the district level by submitting the official Level One grievance form via email to HREmployeeRelations@aisd.net. Arlington ISD assigns a tracking protocol to these grievances to ensure they are reviewed by Specialized Learning Services leadership.

What is the difference between a district complaint and a TEA complaint?

District complaints are handled internally; TEA complaints trigger state investigation. A district complaint is filed directly with Arlington ISD and follows Board Policy FNG(LOCAL). A TEA (Texas Education Agency) complaint is a formal state-level investigation into IDEA violations with a 60-day investigation timeline. In Arlington ISD, parents are encouraged to use local grievances first, as campus-level issues can often be resolved faster than the state-level process.

What happens if I disagree with an ARD meeting decision?

You have the right to dissent. If you disagree with a decision made during an ARD meeting in Arlington ISD, you can request a 10-day recess to gather more information or consider the district's proposal. If agreement is still not reached, you can file a written statement of disagreement in the ARD document and subsequently initiate the Arlington ISD grievance process or request mediation through TEA.

Dispute Resolution Escalation Path in Arlington ISD

Follow this recommended sequence to resolve special education disputes effectively:

Level 1

Campus-Level Resolution (Start Here)

Contact: Your child's ARD committee chair, campus special education coordinator, or principal.

Timeline: Request a meeting or response within 5 business days.

Method: Send a "follow-up" email after every conversation to document what was discussed.

Level 2

Arlington ISD Formal Grievance

If campus level does not resolve the issue: Submit a Level One grievance within 15 business days of the incident.

Texas Education Agency (TEA) Complaint

Arlington ISD Complaint System Details

Does Arlington ISD require campus-level resolution?

Highly Recommended. Arlington ISD Board Policy FNG(LOCAL) strongly encourages parents to attempt resolution at the campus level. If you file a formal Level One grievance without first speaking to the principal, the district administrator may refer you back to the campus to ensure the local team has had an opportunity to address the concern.

How does Arlington ISD's complaint tracking work?

Email and Case Management. Complaints submitted to HREmployeeRelations@aisd.net are logged by the Employee Relations department and assigned a timeline for a Level One conference. All documents are maintained in a central grievance file to track the history of the dispute through potential Level Two and Level Three appeals to the Board of Trustees.

Protection Against Retaliation

Parents in Arlington ISD who file complaints are protected by federal law from retaliation. The district cannot reduce your child's services, exclude you from ARD meetings, or treat your child negatively because you advocated for their rights.

If you believe Arlington ISD is retaliating against you, document the specific incidents and file a complaint with the Office for Civil Rights (OCR) or TEA immediately.
