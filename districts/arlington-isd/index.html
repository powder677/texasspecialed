Proactive Advocacy in Arlington ISD

Arlington Independent School District (AISD) functions under a "Specialized Learning Services" umbrella. Due to a current student-to-staff growth trend—where over 12% of the student body now qualifies for services—parents must navigate a hierarchy that begins at the campus level but often requires intervention from the Mac Bernd Professional Development Center staff.

Under Board Policy FB(LOCAL), the district ensures protection against discrimination, yet families often face hurdles in program placement. You must be specific about the "Continuum of Placement." In Arlington, this includes:

  • ABLE (Academic and Behavior Learning Environment): Focused on functional academics and communication at campuses like Bowie High School or Bailey Junior High.
  • SEAS (Social Emotional and Academic Success): Therapeutic settings for students with significant behavioral needs.
  • Turning Point: Specialized support for secondary students requiring non-traditional environments.

A distinctive "Policy Anchor" for Arlington families is the transition from Early Childhood Intervention (ECI). If your child is turning three, Board Policy EHBAA(LEGAL) requires the district to have an IEP in place by the child's third birthday. In AISD, this process is managed centrally at the Kooken Education Center. If your child is older and you suspect a disability, do not rely on "Response to Intervention" (RTI) as a delay tactic; Board Policy EHBAC(LEGAL) explicitly states that RTI cannot be used to deny or delay an FIE request.
