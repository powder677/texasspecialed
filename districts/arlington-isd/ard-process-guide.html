Answer: In **Arlington ISD**, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education meeting where parents and district staff determine eligibility, services, and placement. Parents must receive at least **five school days’ written notice**, have the right to **audio record** the meeting, and are recognized as **equal members** of the committee under Texas law.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Arlington ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Arlington ISD

  • Notice Timeline: You must be provided with written notice at least 5 school days before the meeting.
  • Recording Rights: You may audio record the meeting; Texas law allows this without school consent.
  • Parent Role: Parents are equal decision-makers on the committee with the same authority as district staff.
  • Annual Requirement: Meetings must occur at least annually to review your child's progress and services.
  • Initial ARD Timing: Initial ARD meetings must be held within 30 days of the completion of a Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

Common ARD Questions

How do I request an ARD meeting in Arlington ISD?

Parents in Arlington ISD can request an ARD at any time to discuss concerns. The district must respond to your request to meet regarding your child's Individualized Education Program (IEP).

What happens if I disagree with the committee's decision?

You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee reaches a disagreement during the meeting. You have the right to disagree with recommendations and have it noted in the formal record.

Can I bring an advocate to my meeting?

Yes, you have a legal right to bring an advocate or professional support to any Arlington ISD ARD meeting. The district generally asks for advance notice if an attorney will be present.

How to Prepare for an ARD Meeting

  1. Gather Data: Review the current IEP, the last three progress reports, and the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  2. Define Goals: Identify 3-5 specific changes or new goals you want to propose for your child.
  3. Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for proposed goals at least one week before the meeting to review them in advance.
  4. Write a Parent Statement: Draft a "Parent Concerns" statement to be formally included in the official IEP deliberations.
  5. Confirm Attendance: Notify the school if you are bringing an advocate and confirm the meeting location or virtual link.

How ARD Meetings in Arlington ISD May Differ From Other Districts

Arlington ISD frequently utilizes specialized roles such as ARD Facilitators at secondary levels and Campus Compliance Coordinators at elementary levels to lead meetings. Additionally, the district emphasizes self-advocacy by encouraging student participation in meetings starting at age 14 or earlier. Virtual attendance via platforms like Zoom or Microsoft Teams is commonly offered for parent convenience.
