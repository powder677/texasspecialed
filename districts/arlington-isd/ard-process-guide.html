Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Arlington ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for the IEP team).
  • Meetings must occur at least annually to review your child's progress and services.
  • Parents are equal decision-makers on the committee with the same authority as district staff.
  • You have a legal right to bring an advocate or professional support to any meeting.
  • Initial ARD meetings must be held within 30 days of the completion of a Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

ARD Meeting Guide for Arlington ISD Parents

Navigating the special education process in Arlington ISD. This guide helps DFW-area families prepare for and participate effectively in ARD committee meetings.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to Arlington from outside of Texas, you are likely looking for an "IEP meeting." In Texas, these are officially called ARD Committee Meetings.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: Determining if a student meets eligibility criteria for special education services.
  • Review: An annual review of the student's progress and the effectiveness of their Individualized Education Program (IEP).
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting a student from special education when they no longer require specialized services.

In Arlington ISD, the ARD committee is the group of people legally responsible for making all decisions regarding your child’s special education program.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Arlington ISD?

The committee is comprised of individuals who offer unique insights into your child's education and development.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

That's you! You are a mandatory and equal member of the committee. Your input on your child’s goals and strengths is essential.

Special Education Teacher

The professional who provides specialized instruction and monitors progress toward specific IEP goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if your child is, or may be, participating in a general education classroom environment.

District Representative

Typically a campus principal or assistant principal who is authorized to commit district resources.

Evaluator

A Diagnostician or LSSP who interprets evaluation results and testing data.

The Student

Required for transition discussions (age 14+) and encouraged for younger students to build self-advocacy skills.

You May Also Bring:

  • Special Education Advocate: A professional hired to help you protect your rights and your child's interests.
  • Legal Counsel: You have a right to an attorney. Arlington ISD generally asks for advance notice if an attorney will be present.
  • Private Specialists: Outside therapists or physicians who can share relevant data for the committee's consideration.
  • Translators: AISD must provide an interpreter if English is not your primary language.

How Arlington ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Arlington ISD usually holds meetings at the student's campus. To ensure efficiency and compliance, the district frequently uses ARD Facilitators at secondary levels and Campus Compliance Coordinators at elementary levels to lead the meeting and manage the paperwork. Virtual attendance via Zoom or Microsoft Teams is often available for parent convenience.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Arlington ISD

Preparation is key to moving from being a recipient of a plan to being an architect of your child's education.

2 Weeks Before: Gather Data

  • Thoroughly review the current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Gather recent work samples, report cards, and private clinical reports.
  • Ask your child what they feel is working well in their classes and what is not.

1 Week Before: Define Goals

  • Draft a "Parent Concerns" statement to be formally included in the IEP deliberations.
  • Identify 3-5 specific changes or new goals you want to propose.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for proposed goals so you can review them before the meeting.
  • Notify the school if you are bringing an advocate or recording the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

ARD meetings follow a legally mandated agenda to ensure all required components of the IEP are addressed.

The Typical ARD Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and ensuring all required members are present.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Your designated time to share concerns, observations, and vision.
  4. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the next 12 months.
  5. Services & Accommodations: Determining modifications, classroom support, and therapy minutes.
  6. LRE & Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment where services will be provided.
  7. Review of Minutes: Reading the deliberations to ensure they accurately reflect the discussion.

Your Rights at the Table:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point during the meeting.
  • ✓ You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee reaches a disagreement.
  • ✓ You can audio record the meeting (Texas allows this without school consent).
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any term or acronym (e.g., LRE, BIP, FAPE).
  • ✓ You can disagree with recommendations and have it noted in the record.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting is the plan; the following weeks are the execution.

Full Agreement

If everyone agrees and signs, services typically begin within 5-10 school days.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to some parts (like goals) while disagreeing with others (like placement). The agreed portions can start while you work to resolve the dispute.

Formal Disagreement

If you cannot reach consensus, you can request a recess, file a grievance, or pursue mediation.

Meeting Frequency

Arlington ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: To update and review the current IEP.
  • Triennially: For a full reevaluation (REED process) every 3 years.
  • On Request: Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss concerns.
