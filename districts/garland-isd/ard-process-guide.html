Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Garland ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas-specific term for IEP team).
  • Required at least annually to review your child's educational program.
  • You are an equal member of the committee with a legal right to participate in all decisions.
  • Initial ARD meetings must be held within 30 days of a completed evaluation.
  • Parents have the right to record meetings and bring advocates for support.

ARD Meeting Guide for Garland ISD Parents

Everything you need to know about preparing for and participating in ARD committee meetings within the Garland Independent School District.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to the DFW area from another state, you might be looking for an "IEP meeting." In Texas, these are officially known as ARD Committee Meetings.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The process of determining eligibility and admitting a student into special education.
  • Review: The annual review of the student's progress and the Individualized Education Program (IEP).
  • Dismissal: The process used when a student no longer qualifies for special education services.

In Garland ISD, the ARD committee is the decision-making body responsible for your child's specialized education plan.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Garland ISD?

The committee consists of a group of professionals and you, working together to create an appropriate plan for your child.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

That's you! You are an equal member. Your input on your child's strengths and needs is vital for a complete plan.

Special Education Teacher

The provider responsible for implementing specialized instruction and tracking progress toward goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in a general education classroom environment.

District Representative

Usually a campus administrator who is authorized to commit district resources.

Evaluator

A Diagnostician or LSSP who interprets evaluation results and testing data.

The Student

Required for transition discussions (age 14+) and encouraged for self-advocacy whenever appropriate.

You May Also Invite:

  • Special Education Advocate: Someone hired to help you protect your rights and your child's interests.
  • Legal Counsel: You have a right to an attorney. (Note: GISD usually requests notice if an attorney will be present).
  • Outside Specialists: Private therapists or physicians who can share relevant data for consideration.
  • Translators: Garland ISD must provide an interpreter if English is not your primary language.

How Garland ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Garland ISD typically holds meetings at the student's home campus. To maintain efficiency and compliance, the district utilizes **ARD Facilitators** at many campuses to manage documentation and help keep the meeting focused on student outcomes. Virtual options (via Zoom or Microsoft Teams) are often available to accommodate parent schedules.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Garland ISD

Preparation is key to being a proactive member of the committee. Don't wait until the meeting begins to voice your goals.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review the current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Gather recent work samples, report cards, and private medical reports.
  • Talk with your child about what is helping them in class and what is difficult.

1 Week Before: Document Priorities

  • Draft a "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the official meeting deliberations.
  • Identify 3-5 specific changes or new goals you want to propose.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a copy of proposed goals to review in advance.
  • Notify the campus coordinator if you are bringing an advocate or planning to record.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

ARD meetings follow a legally mandated agenda to ensure all necessary parts of your child's education are covered.

Typical ARD Meeting Agenda in GISD:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and ensuring all required members are present.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Sharing your concerns, observations, and vision for your child.
  4. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the next 12 months.
  5. Services & Accommodations: Determining modifications, classroom support, and therapy minutes.
  6. LRE & Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment where services will be provided.
  7. Review of Minutes: Checking the "deliberations" for accuracy before signing.

Your Rights at the Table:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point during the meeting.
  • ✓ You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee reaches a stalemate.
  • ✓ You can audio record the meeting (Texas allows this without school consent).
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any term or acronym (e.g., PLAAFP, LRE, FAPE).
  • ✓ You can disagree with recommendations and have it noted in the record.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting is the plan; the following weeks are where the plan is put into action.

Full Agreement

If everyone agrees, services typically begin within 5-10 school days unless a later start date is specified.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to some parts (like goals) while disagreeing with others (like placement). The agreed portions start while you resolve the dispute.

Formal Disagreement

If you cannot reach a consensus, you can request a recess, file a grievance, or pursue mediation.

Meeting Frequency

Garland ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: To update and review the current IEP.
  • Triennially: For a full reevaluation (REED process) every 3 years.
  • On Request: Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss concerns.
