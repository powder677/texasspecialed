⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Many complaints have strict filing deadlines. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. District grievances often have even shorter deadlines (10-15 business days). Don't delay if you believe your child's rights have been violated.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Eanes ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Eanes ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or ARD committee decisions.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Dispute resolution is a safeguard under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA). In Eanes ISD, while the district maintains a high reputation for academic excellence, procedural or substantive disagreements can still occur. You should consider formal dispute resolution if you encounter the following:

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP lists specific services (speech therapy 2x/week, inclusion support, etc.) but Eanes ISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

Eanes ISD has missed the Texas 45-school-day evaluation timeline or refused to evaluate your child for a suspected disability without valid justification.

ARD Meeting Issues

The district refuses to hold an ARD meeting, excludes you from participating in decisions, or ignores parent-provided private evaluations.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child needs a more restrictive or less restrictive educational setting (LRE) than what the ARD committee is offering.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated differently because of their disability, including unfair disciplinary actions or denial of access to extracurriculars.

Retaliation

You feel district staff are retaliating against you or your child because you advocated for rights or filed a previous grievance.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

In many cases, informal resolution is faster and preserves the working relationship with your campus team. Consider:

  • Discussing concerns directly with your child's teacher or Case Manager.
  • Requesting a meeting with the Campus Principal or the Special Education Department Chair.
  • Contacting the Eanes ISD Special Education Administration (view leadership contact info).
  • Requesting an ARD meeting to address concerns and documenting "Parent Concerns" in the IEP.

Always document all communication in writing. If informal resolution does not yield results, proceed with the formal processes outlined below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

The following hierarchy illustrates the paths available to parents, moving from local district control to state and federal intervention.

Level 1

District Grievance Process

Best for: Implementation issues, staff conduct, and localized campus problems.

Timeline: Strict deadlines; usually 15 days from the incident to file Level One.

Cost: Free.

Binding: No - can be appealed to the Board of Trustees and then TEA.

Learn about Eanes ISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Clear violations of IDEA law, state rules, or failing to follow the IEP.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year; TEA must issue a finding within 60 days.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - TEA orders corrective action (e.g., compensatory services).

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: Reaching a compromise when both parties are willing to negotiate.

Timeline: Voluntary; can occur at any time if both parties agree.

Cost: Free (TEA provides and pays for the mediator).

Binding: Yes, once a Mediation Agreement is signed by both parties.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution Portal

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Fundamental disagreements on FAPE, eligibility, or placement.

Timeline: Must file within 1 year; involves legal discovery and a trial-like hearing.

Cost: Significant if hiring an attorney ($300-$500/hour). Expert witnesses also cost money.

Binding: Yes - legally enforceable in court.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Disability discrimination, harassment, or Section 504 violations.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the alleged discrimination.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - OCR can withhold federal funding to ensure compliance.

The Eanes ISD Local Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Eanes ISD follows a standard three-level grievance process for parents and students (Board Policy FNG Local). This process is not specific to special education but is often used for general complaints against staff or campus decisions.

Level One: Campus/Department Level

The first formal step is filing a written complaint with the lowest level administrator who has the authority to remedy the situation (usually the Principal). This must be filed within 15 days of the event. A meeting will be held within 10 days of the filing, and a written response provided within 10 days of that meeting.

Level Two: District Administration

If you are unsatisfied with the Level One decision, you may appeal to the Superintendent's designee. This is typically an Assistant Superintendent. They will review the Level One record and hold a meeting to hear your appeal.

Level Three: Board of Trustees

The final local step is an appeal to the Eanes ISD Board of Trustees. You will be given a limited amount of time to present your case to the board in a closed or open session. The Board's decision is final at the local level.

Note: If your complaint involves a denial of FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education), the TEA Special Education Complaint or Due Process paths are often more effective than a local school board grievance.

Filing a TEA Special Education Complaint

A State Complaint is a written document submitted to the Texas Education Agency alleging that a school district has violated a requirement of the IDEA or the Texas Administrative Code. This is an "investigative" process rather than a "hearing" process.

What to Include:

  • A statement that the district has violated a requirement of IDEA.
  • The facts on which the statement is based.
  • The signature and contact information for the person filing.
  • If alleging a violation regarding a specific child: name, address, and the name of the school the child attends.
  • A proposed resolution to the problem.

You must send a copy of the complaint to the Eanes ISD Superintendent at the same time you file with TEA. TEA will assign an investigator, and a final "Findings of Fact" will be issued within 60 calendar days.

Due Process Hearings

Due process is the most formal of the resolution options. It is a legal proceeding before an Independent Hearing Officer (IHO). In Eanes ISD, due process hearings are often complex and involve legal representation for both the family and the district.

The Resolution Session

Before a hearing can proceed, the law requires a "Resolution Session" where the district and parents try to settle the case. This is a final "last chance" to avoid trial. You can waive this only if both parties agree in writing to go to mediation instead.

Stay Put Provision

Once a Due Process request is filed, the "Stay Put" provision usually applies. This means your child remains in their current educational placement until the dispute is resolved, preventing the district from making unilateral changes during the legal battle.

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaints

If you believe Eanes ISD is discriminating against your child based on their disability (a violation of Section 504 of the Rehabilitation Act), you can file a complaint with the U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights.

Common OCR complaints in special education include:

  • Physical barriers to access in school buildings.
  • Failure to provide 504 accommodations.
  • Disproportionate discipline of students with disabilities.
  • Bullying based on disability that the district failed to address.

OCR does not typically look at "educational quality" but rather at "equal access."

Crucial Documentation for Any Dispute

Success in any grievance or complaint depends on the quality of your evidence. Start organizing these items immediately:

The Paper Trail: Keep copies of all IEPs, ARD meeting minutes, and Progress Reports.
Communication Log: Maintain a log of every phone call, email, and meeting with Eanes ISD staff, including dates and summaries.
Prior Written Notice (PWN): If the district refuses a request, they MUST provide you with a PWN explaining why. Save these; they are vital for complaints.
Independent Evaluations: If you have private therapist reports or outside testing (IEE), ensure you have proof you provided them to the district.
