How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Eanes ISD

A comprehensive guide to starting the special education evaluation process in Eanes ISD, Austin/Westlake, Texas.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal IDEA law and Texas Education Code, Eanes ISD has a "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with disabilities (birth through age 21) who may need special education services, regardless of the severity of their disability. This legal mandate applies even if your child is currently passing their classes but is struggling in other areas such as social-emotional development or executive functioning.

You Have the Right To:

  • Request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) at any time, in writing, regardless of whether your child is in public school, private school, or homeschooled.
  • Receive a response—known as Prior Written Notice—within 15 school days of your written request.
  • Have your child evaluated in all areas of suspected disability, including health, vision, hearing, social and emotional status, general intelligence, academic performance, communicative status, and motor abilities.
  • Receive evaluation results in your native language or other mode of communication.
  • Disagree with the district's evaluation and request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense if you believe the district's assessment was flawed.

New to Special Education in Eanes?

If you're concerned about your child's learning, behavior, or development, requesting an evaluation is the first step toward specialized support. You do not need a doctor's diagnosis to request an evaluation from Eanes ISD; the district's evaluation serves to determine educational eligibility, which is distinct from a medical diagnosis.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Eanes ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

Send a formal written evaluation request to Eanes ISD. While verbal requests to a teacher or principal are technically valid, written requests are essential because they trigger specific legal timelines and protections under Texas law. It is best to email or hand-deliver your request and obtain a receipt.

2

District Reviews Your Request

Eanes ISD will review the information you provided and any existing data (such as grades, state testing, or teacher observations) to determine if an evaluation is warranted. Within 15 school days, the district must provide you with:

  • Prior Written Notice explaining why they are moving forward with an evaluation or why they are refusing to evaluate.
  • A copy of the Procedural Safeguards (your legal rights).
  • If agreeing to evaluate: Official Consent for Evaluation forms that outline the specific tests they intend to perform.
3

Sign Consent for Evaluation

Once you receive the consent form from the district, review it carefully. It should list all areas being evaluated (academic achievement, speech/language, psychological, etc.).

Crucial Point: The 60 calendar days evaluation timeline in Texas does NOT start until Eanes ISD receives your signed consent in writing. In Texas, "school days" apply to the 15-day response, but "calendar days" apply to the evaluation itself.

Tip: Date your consent form and keep a digital copy. This marks "Day 1" of the 60-day legal countdown to the completion of the report.
4

Evaluation is Conducted

The Eanes ISD evaluation team (which may include educational diagnosticians, LSSPs, and therapists) will assess your child. This may include:

  • Academic testing (reading fluency, math reasoning, written expression)
  • Speech and language evaluation
  • Psychological or cognitive assessment (IQ testing and processing)
  • Occupational or Physical Therapy evaluations
  • Functional Behavioral Assessment (FBA)
  • Dyslexia screening: Required under Texas HB 3928, which mandates that dyslexia evaluations occur under IDEA (special education) protocols rather than just a 504 screening.
5

Initial ARD Meeting is Scheduled

Within the 60 calendar days window (or 30 days after the report is finished), the district must:

  • Complete all formal evaluations and the written FIIE (Full Individual and Initial Evaluation) report.
  • Schedule an Initial ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting at a mutually agreeable time.
  • Provide you with the written evaluation reports before the meeting so you can prepare.

At the ARD meeting, the committee (including you, a district representative, and teachers) will determine if your child meets one of the 13 eligibility categories and requires specially designed instruction.

What If Eanes ISD Misses the Deadline?

If the district does not complete the evaluation or hold the ARD meeting within the 60 calendar days, you have the right to:

  • Request a written explanation for the delay from the special education director.
  • File a formal state complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA).
  • Request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at district expense if the delay results in a lack of appropriate data.

Common Questions About Evaluations in Eanes ISD

Can Eanes ISD refuse to evaluate my child?

Yes, if the district does not suspect a disability exists that impacts educational performance. However, they must provide "Prior Written Notice" explaining their refusal and the data they used to make that decision. You can challenge this via mediation or a due process hearing.

Do I need an outside diagnosis for Autism or ADHD first?

No. Under Child Find, Eanes ISD must evaluate based on educational need. While a private medical diagnosis can be helpful data for the committee, the school's evaluation is the final word on whether a student qualifies for special education services in the school setting.

How does HB 3928 change dyslexia testing in Eanes?

As of Texas HB 3928 (passed in 2023), all initial dyslexia evaluations must be conducted through the IDEA (Special Education) process. This ensures parents have full procedural safeguards and that students who qualify receive an IEP/ARD plan rather than just a 504 plan if they need specialized instruction.
