Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Eanes ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (The Texas term for an IEP team).
  • Meetings occur at least annually to review and update your child's educational program.
  • You are a mandatory equal member of the committee; your input is essential for decision-making.
  • Parents have a legal right to bring an advocate, attorney, or support person to any meeting.
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time to address new concerns or progress issues.

ARD Meeting Guide for Eanes ISD Parents

Navigating the special education landscape in Eanes ISD (Austin/Westlake) requires specific knowledge of Texas procedures. This guide helps you prepare effectively for your child's ARD committee meetings.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

In most of the United States, the group that develops a student's special education plan is called the IEP Team. However, in Texas and Eanes ISD, this group is legally known as the Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) Committee.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

The ARD acronym refers to the three primary functions of the committee:

  • Admission: Determining if a student meets the criteria for special education and "admitting" them into the program.
  • Review: An annual (or more frequent) "review" of the student's progress, goals, and the effectiveness of current services.
  • Dismissal: Determining when a student no longer requires specialized instruction and "dismissing" them from special education services.

While the committee is called an ARD committee, the document they produce is still called an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Eanes ISD?

Eanes ISD meetings are designed to be collaborative. Federal and state law mandate that certain individuals must be present to make the committee legal.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

Your role is vital. You are the expert on your child. In Eanes ISD, parent participation is highly valued as a cornerstone of student success.

Special Education Teacher

The provider who understands specialized instruction techniques and tracks progress on specific IEP goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if your child is, or may be, participating in the general education environment. They provide insight into curriculum standards (TEKS).

District Representative

Typically a campus administrator (Principal or AP) who has the authority to commit district resources and funds.

Evaluation Professional

A Diagnostician or LSSP (Licensed Specialist in School Psychology) who can interpret evaluation data and testing results.

The Student

Required starting at age 14 for transition planning, but Eanes ISD encourages student attendance earlier when appropriate to foster self-advocacy.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocates: Professional special education advocates can help you navigate the complexities of the law.
  • Attorneys: You have the right to legal counsel; Eanes ISD usually requests 48-72 hours' notice if an attorney will attend.
  • Private Providers: Outside therapists (Speech, OT, or ABA) who have data on your child's performance in other settings.
  • Support: A trusted friend or family member to help take notes or provide emotional support.

Unique ARD Features in Eanes ISD

Eanes ISD is known for its high level of parental involvement and "Facilitated ARD" options. If a meeting is expected to be particularly complex or contentious, the district may utilize a trained facilitator to ensure the committee remains focused on the student's needs and maintains a productive dialogue.

For specific questions about your campus procedures, you should contact your school's Assistant Principal or Special Education Coordinator.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Eanes ISD

Being prepared is the best way to ensure your child receives the supports they need. Use the following timeline to stay organized.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Read the current IEP and the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Review the latest progress reports—are goals being met as expected?
  • Gather work samples, private evaluations, and any medical updates.
  • Talk to your child about their feelings toward school, what is hard, and what they enjoy.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Write down your "Top 3" concerns or goals for the next year.
  • Request a draft IEP from the teacher. Reviewing proposed goals early prevents surprises during the meeting.
  • Prepare a list of questions regarding specific accommodations or service minutes.
  • Notify the school of any additional people you are bringing to the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

While every campus in Eanes ISD has its own culture, the structure of the meeting follows a legal sequence designed to protect student rights.

Typical Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: Everyone identifies their role and confirms they have received their procedural safeguards.
  2. Review of Progress: Discussing how the student performed on the previous year's goals.
  3. Parent Input: This is your time to share your observations, concerns, and goals. Don't skip this.
  4. PLAAFPs: Developing the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance"—the baseline for all new goals.
  5. Goals & Objectives: Reviewing and refining the specific skills the student will work on.
  6. Accommodations & Services: Discussing the "where, when, and how" of instruction, including Speech, OT, or PT.
  7. Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE) where the student will receive services.
  8. Consensus: Working toward an agreement on the final plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ Right to Record: You may audio record any ARD meeting in Texas. It is professional to notify the team at the start of the meeting.
  • ✓ Right to Breaks: You can ask for a 5-10 minute recess if you need to process information or speak with your advocate.
  • ✓ Right to Clarification: You have the right to ask for an explanation of any term, acronym, or data point you don't understand.
  • ✓ Right to Disagree: You can disagree with the district's recommendations and request a "10-day recess" to gather more information.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Stay Data-Focused: Use specific examples or work samples to support your requests.
  • Ask "Why": If a service is being reduced, ask to see the data that supports the committee's belief that it is no longer needed.
  • Take Notes: If you aren't recording, have a dedicated note-taker so you can focus on the conversation.
  • Wait to Sign: You are not required to sign the IEP immediately. You can take it home to review one last time.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting is just the beginning of the implementation phase.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

The committee is in consensus. The IEP is signed, and services begin on the agreed-upon date (usually within 5-10 days).

Partial Agreement

You agree with most of the plan but disagree with a specific goal or service. In Texas, you can implement the "agreed-upon" portions while working to resolve the disputed sections.

Full Disagreement

If no agreement can be reached, the meeting ends in "Non-Consensus." You have the right to request mediation or file for a due process hearing.

Implementation Timeline:

Once signed, the school must implement the IEP as written. We recommend checking in with your child's teachers about two weeks after the ARD to ensure accommodations are being provided in all settings.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

While the district must hold an ARD at least once a year, you can request an ARD meeting at any time. If your child is struggling or if a new medical need arises, don't wait for the annual review.
