Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Lovejoy ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas equivalent of an IEP team).
  • Annual meetings are required to update your child's goals and specialized services.
  • You are an equal member of the committee; your consent is required for initial placement.
  • Parents may bring advocates, outside providers, or legal counsel to any meeting.
  • You can request a "Brief ARD" or amendment at any time to adjust accommodations.

ARD Meeting Guide for Lovejoy ISD Parents

Serving the Allen, Lucas, and Fairview communities, Lovejoy ISD is known for high expectations. This guide ensures parents are equipped to navigate the ARD process to secure the best outcomes for their children.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If your family is new to Lovejoy ISD from another state, the term "ARD" might be unfamiliar. While the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) is a federal law, Texas has its own specific terminology for implementation.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

The ARD acronym breaks down as:

  • Admission: The process of qualifying a student for special education services.
  • Review: The required annual meeting to check progress and update the IEP.
  • Dismissal: The process used when a student has met their goals and no longer requires specialized instruction.

Ultimately, the committee develops an Individualized Education Program (IEP) that is legally binding for Lovejoy ISD to implement.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Lovejoy ISD?

Lovejoy ISD emphasizes a "small district" feel where teachers and parents often know each other well, but the meeting must still include specific required personnel.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

Your role is critical. You provide the essential context of how your child functions at home and in the community.

Special Education Teacher

Typically the case manager who oversees the implementation of the student's specialized instruction.

General Education Teacher

A teacher who knows the grade-level expectations (TEKS) and how the child performs among their peers.

District Representative

A campus administrator who ensures the district can provide the services the committee agrees upon.

Evaluation Professional

A Diagnostician or LSSP who interprets the testing data and eligibility requirements.

The Student

Starting at age 14, students are invited to discuss their own goals for life after high school (Transition Planning).

You May Also Bring:

  • Educational Advocate: To help you understand your procedural safeguards and rights.
  • Outside Therapists: If your child receives private ABA, Speech, or OT, these providers can offer valuable data.
  • Legal Counsel: You have a right to an attorney; Lovejoy ISD typically asks for notice if counsel will be present.
  • Friend or Spouse: Having a second set of ears is often helpful for taking notes.

How Lovejoy ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Lovejoy ISD meetings are often held on the student's campus. Because of the district's size, there is a strong emphasis on relationship-building. However, parents should remain focused on data and specific goal tracking. For coordinator details, see the Leadership Directory.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Lovejoy ISD

Being prepared is the most effective way to be an equal member of the committee.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review the previous IEP and the latest Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Check the most recent progress reports—are the goals being reached?
  • Collect work samples that show where your child is succeeding or struggling.
  • Gather any new medical reports or private evaluation results.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask your child's teacher to email the proposed goals so you can review them early.
  • Write down your "Parent Concerns" statement to ensure it is included in the formal record.
  • Make a list of the 3 most important things you want your child to achieve this year.
  • Notify the district if you are bringing an advocate or recording the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Most Lovejoy ISD meetings follow a standard agenda to maintain legal compliance.

Typical Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: Everyone states their name and role.
  2. Data Review: The committee looks at testing, classroom data, and progress.
  3. Parent Input: You share your observations and concerns. This is your most important moment.
  4. PLAAFPs: Writing the "Present Levels" which describe exactly where the student is currently.
  5. Goals & Objectives: Developing the plan for the next year.
  6. Accommodations & Services: Discussing supports like extra time or specialized equipment.
  7. Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).
  8. Closing: Signing the document and recording any disagreements.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ Right to Record: Texas is a one-party consent state; you can audio record your ARD meeting.
  • ✓ Right to Breaks: You can ask for a 5-minute recess at any time if you feel overwhelmed.
  • ✓ Right to Information: You can ask for clarification on any term or data point you don't understand.
  • ✓ Right to Disagree: You can refuse to sign the IEP if you believe it does not provide FAPE.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The implementation of the IEP is where the real work happens.

Possible Outcomes:

Agreement

The committee is in consensus. The new plan begins on the date listed in the IEP.

10-Day Recess

In Texas, if you disagree with the district, the meeting is recessed for up to 10 school days so both parties can try to reach a compromise.

Disagreement

If agreement isn't reached after a recess, the district may implement their plan, and you may seek mediation or due process.
