How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Lovejoy ISD

A step-by-step guide for families in Allen/Fairview to start the special education evaluation process in Lovejoy ISD.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal and state law, Lovejoy ISD (LISD) has a legal "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with suspected disabilities within district boundaries. This mandate covers children from birth through age 21, including those in private schools or homeschool programs.

Key Rights in the Evaluation Process:

  • The right to request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) in writing at any time.
  • The right to receive "Prior Written Notice" within 15 school days if the district agrees or refuses to evaluate.
  • The right to have your child assessed in all areas of suspected disability by qualified professionals (e.g., LSSPs, Diagnosticians).
  • The right to an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense if you disagree with the district's findings.

Did You Know?

You do not need an outside medical diagnosis to request an evaluation. While external reports are helpful, Lovejoy ISD is required to conduct its own assessment to determine educational eligibility.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Lovejoy ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

Send a formal written request for evaluation to LISD. While verbal requests are possible, written requests officially trigger legal timelines.

2

The 15-Day Response Window

Within 15 school days of receiving your request, Lovejoy ISD must respond. They will provide you with:

  • Notice of Evaluation: Consent forms if they agree to evaluate.
  • Procedural Safeguards: A manual explaining your legal rights.
  • Prior Written Notice: An explanation if they refuse to evaluate.
3

Sign Informed Consent

The evaluation timeline only begins once Lovejoy ISD receives your signed, written consent.

Crucial Fact: In Texas, the district has 45 school days to complete the Full Initial and Individual Evaluation (FIIE) after you sign this form.

Parent Tip: Ensure the consent form covers all your concerns—such as behavior, sensory needs, or reading—before signing.
4

The Comprehensive Evaluation

LISD staff, including Educational Diagnosticians and School Psychologists, will assess your child. Evaluations may include:

  • Psychological testing and behavior assessments.
  • Academic and cognitive testing.
  • Speech and language pathology services.
  • Dyslexia Testing: Under HB 3928, dyslexia must now be evaluated as a specific learning disability through the special education process.
5

Initial ARD Meeting

Within 30 calendar days of completing the evaluation, Lovejoy ISD must hold an Initial ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting.

The ARD committee (which includes you) will review the results to determine if your child qualifies for special education and related services.

Absences & Late Requests

If a student is absent 3+ days, the 45-day deadline may be extended. If consent is signed 35-45 days before the end of the school year, the report is due by June 30th.

Frequently Asked Questions in Lovejoy ISD

Can Lovejoy ISD use RTI to delay an evaluation?

No. Under Child Find, a district cannot use Response to Intervention (RTI) to delay or deny an evaluation for a student suspected of having a disability.

How does HB 3928 change dyslexia services?

Dyslexia is now clearly classified as a specific learning disability under IDEA. This means evaluations and services (like Take Flight) are provided through the special education framework rather than just Section 504.

What if my child attends a private school in Allen?

LISD is responsible for evaluating children in private schools located within district boundaries. Contact the Special Education department directly for private school referral information.
