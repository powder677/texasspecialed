⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Many complaints have strict filing deadlines. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. District grievances (Policy FNG) often have much shorter deadlines, typically requiring a Level One filing within 15 business days of the incident. Don't delay if you believe your child's rights have been violated.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Lovejoy ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Lovejoy ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or ARD committee decisions.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's Individualized Education Program (IEP) lists specific services, but Lovejoy ISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

The district has missed state evaluation timelines or refused to evaluate a suspected disability without valid justification.

ARD Meeting Issues

Lovejoy ISD refuses to hold an ARD meeting, excludes you from participation, or refuses to provide written notice for a denial.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child needs a different educational setting than what the ARD committee is offering.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated differently or harassed because of their disability.

Retaliation

You feel Lovejoy ISD is retaliating against you or your child because you advocated for their rights.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

Informal resolution is often faster and preserves the working relationship with your campus team. Consider:

  • Discussing concerns directly with your child's teacher or case manager.
  • Requesting a meeting with the campus principal or campus special education coordinator.
  • Contacting the Director of Special Education, Allison Claunch, at 469-742-8048.
  • Requesting an ARD meeting to address concerns formally.

Document all communication attempts. If informal resolution doesn't work, proceed with formal processes below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides multiple pathways for resolving special education disputes, from local level to federal intervention.

Level 1

District Grievance (Policy FNG)

Best for: Campus-level implementation issues, staff conduct, or local policy concerns.

Timeline: Typically 15 days to file a Level One grievance.

Cost: Free.

Binding: No; either party can escalate to the Board of Trustees.

Learn about Lovejoy ISD's process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of federal IDEA law, state special education rules, or procedural errors.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes; if TEA finds a violation, Lovejoy ISD must implement corrective actions.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: Reaching a mutual compromise with the help of a neutral third party.

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 2–4 weeks if both parties agree.

Cost: Free (TEA provides the mediator).

Binding: Yes, only if both parties sign a written mediation agreement.

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disputes about eligibility, placement, or the provision of FAPE.

Timeline: File within 1 year; results in a trial-like hearing before a hearing officer.

Cost: High; usually requires a special education attorney.

Binding: Yes; the hearing officer's decision is legally binding.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination, harassment, or Section 504 violations based on disability.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the last act of discrimination.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes; OCR can enforce compliance and federal oversight.

Learn about OCR complaints ↓

The Lovejoy ISD Local Grievance Process (Board Policy FNG)

Lovejoy ISD follows a three-level formal grievance process for students and parents. This process is governed by Board Policy FNG (Local).

Level One: Campus Administrator

A formal written grievance is submitted to the campus principal. A conference will be held within 10 days of submission to discuss the issue. The administrator provides a written decision within 20 days.

Level Two: District Administration

If the Level One decision does not provide the requested relief, you may appeal to the Superintendent or a designee. They will review the record and hold a Level Two conference.

Level Three: Board of Trustees

The final local step is an appeal to the Lovejoy ISD Board of Trustees. The Board will hear the appeal and make a final local determination.

Filing a TEA Special Education Complaint

A state complaint alleges that a school district has violated a specific special education law or rule.

Requirements for Filing:

  • The complaint must be in writing and signed.
  • It must include your contact information and the child's school information.
  • You must describe the specific violations and the facts supporting them.
  • You must send a copy of the complaint to Lovejoy ISD at the same time you send it to TEA.

TEA Contact: spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov | Fax: (512) 463-9560.

Due Process Hearings

Due process is a formal legal proceeding where a hearing officer makes a binding decision on a student's special education program.

Resolution Session

After filing for due process, the district must hold a resolution meeting within 15 days to try and settle the case before the hearing.

Stay Put Provision

During a due process proceeding, your child generally stays in their current educational placement (the "stay put") until the dispute is resolved.

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaints

OCR investigates claims of discrimination or harassment based on disability. In Lovejoy ISD, you may contact Rodricka Taylor at 469-742-8000 for accessibility and disability-related concerns.

Typical OCR complaints involve:

  • Disability-based bullying that the school failed to address.
  • Failure to provide Section 504 accommodations.
  • Unequal access to facilities, clubs, or athletics.

Crucial Documentation for Disputes

Evidence is vital for any formal dispute resolution. Maintain an organized file of:

Communication Logs: Records of all phone calls, emails, and meetings.
ARD Documents: All current and historical IEPs and ARD meeting minutes.
Prior Written Notice (PWN): All notices where the district refused or proposed a change.
Student Data: Work samples, progress reports, and outside evaluations.
