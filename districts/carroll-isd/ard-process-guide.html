Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Carroll ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas equivalent of an IEP team).
  • Meetings are legally required at least once per year to update your child's educational program.
  • You are a mandated member of the committee with equal standing to district staff.
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate, attorney, or support person to Southlake ARD meetings.
  • An ARD meeting can be requested by a parent at any time to address academic or behavioral concerns.

ARD Meeting Guide for Carroll ISD Parents

Success in Southlake’s Carroll ISD requires active parental participation. This guide provides the tools and knowledge necessary to navigate the ARD committee process effectively for your child.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are new to Southlake from outside of Texas, the terminology can be confusing. While federal law (IDEA) governs special education nationwide, Texas uses the term "ARD" to describe the process that other states call "IEP meetings."

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial determination of eligibility for special education services.
  • Review: The annual or periodic check-in to ensure the program is working.
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting a student from special education when services are no longer required.

The final product of these meetings is still the Individualized Education Program (IEP), but in Carroll ISD, the committee tasked with creating and managing it is the ARD committee.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Carroll ISD?

Carroll ISD follows strict state guidelines regarding who must be present to make committee decisions legally binding.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are an equal member. Your insights into your child's life outside of school are critical for developing effective goals.

Special Education Teacher

Provides data on specialized instruction, progress on current goals, and suggestions for future accommodations.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in general education classrooms to discuss curriculum access.

District Representative

An administrator (often a Principal or Assistant Principal) who can commit district funds and resources for services.

Diagnostician or LSSP

Interprets the Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) and explains how the child's disability impacts learning.

The Student

Must be invited starting at age 14 for transition planning, but parents may choose to include them earlier if appropriate.

You May Also Bring:

  • Special Education Advocate: To help ensure your child's rights are protected and the law is followed.
  • Attorney: You have the right to legal counsel (Southlake families typically notify the district in advance if an attorney is attending).
  • Private Specialists: Outside therapists (Speech, OT, etc.) who can provide a different perspective on your child's needs.
  • Friend or Relative: For emotional support or to act as a note-taker.

How Carroll ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Meetings in Carroll ISD are typically held on the child's home campus. While the district values collaboration, Southlake's high academic standards often mean that meetings are data-driven. Parents should expect a focus on how special education services will support the student in meeting the district's rigorous curriculum goals.

For specific questions about your school's practices, contact the Carroll ISD special education department.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Carroll ISD

Walking into an ARD meeting prepared is the best way to reduce stress and advocate effectively for your child.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Thoroughly review your child's current IEP and most recent evaluation.
  • Look over progress reports—are the current goals actually being met?
  • Gather independent evaluations or letters from private therapists.
  • Review recent classwork and tests to identify where your child is struggling.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Create a list of your top 3 concerns or priorities for the upcoming year.
  • Write down questions about specific services or accommodations.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher to send home draft goals so you can review them before the meeting starts.
  • Confirm with any advocates or support persons you have invited.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Most ARD meetings in Southlake follow a predictable agenda. Staying focused on the student's needs is the priority.

Typical Meeting Flow in Carroll ISD:

  1. Introductions: Everyone confirms their name and role.
  2. Review of Progress: Discussing how the student did on last year's goals.
  3. Evaluation Data: Reviewing any new testing or medical updates.
  4. Parent Input: This is your chance to share your concerns and your vision for your child.
  5. Goal Setting: Developing new academic and functional goals for the coming year.
  6. Accommodations: Deciding what supports (extra time, small groups, etc.) the child needs.
  7. Placement & Services: Determining where the services will be provided and for how many minutes.
  8. Closing: Signing the documents and noting any areas of disagreement.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ Recording: You have the right to audio record the meeting in Texas (just inform the team you are doing so).
  • ✓ Breaks: You can ask for a break at any time if you feel overwhelmed or need to speak with your advocate.
  • ✓ Clarification: You have the right to ask for an explanation of any "jargon" or acronyms used by district staff.
  • ✓ Disagreement: You can disagree with any part of the IEP and request a 10-day recess to gather more data.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Ask for data. If a teacher says a goal is met, ask to see the tracking logs.
  • Focus on the "Why." If you want a specific accommodation, explain how it helps your child learn.
  • Stay collaborative. Use "I" statements like "I am concerned that..." rather than "You aren't doing..."
  • Don't feel pressured to sign the final document on the spot if you need time to think.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The work doesn't end when the meeting is over. Implementation and monitoring are key.

Possible Outcomes:

Consensus (Agreement)

If everyone agrees, the IEP is finalized. Carroll ISD will begin implementing the plan on the agreed-upon date.

Disagreement

In Texas, if there is a disagreement, you can request a "10-day recess." The committee meets again within 10 school days to try and reach a compromise.

Non-Consensus

If agreement still can't be reached after a recess, the district may implement their plan, and the parent can seek mediation or due process.

Learn about dispute resolution in Carroll ISD →

Implementation Timeline:

Once signed, Carroll ISD must implement the services as written. We recommend checking in with teachers about 30 days after the meeting to ensure everything is running smoothly.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

Beyond the annual review, you can request an ARD meeting at any time. You do not have to wait until next year if things aren't working.
