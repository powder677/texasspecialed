⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Many complaints have strict filing deadlines. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. District grievances often have even shorter deadlines (10-15 business days). Don't delay if you believe your child's rights have been violated.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Carroll ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Carroll ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or IEP.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP lists specific services (speech therapy 2x/week, resource support, etc.) but Carroll ISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

Carroll ISD has missed the 60-day evaluation timeline or refused to evaluate without valid justification.

ARD Meeting Issues

Carroll ISD refuses to hold an ARD meeting, excludes you from decisions, or won't consider your input.

Placement Disagreements

You believe your child needs a different educational setting than what Carroll ISD is offering.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated differently because of their disability (bullying, exclusion from activities, denial of accommodations).

Retaliation

You feel Carroll ISD is retaliating against you or your child because you advocated for their rights.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

In many cases, informal resolution is faster and preserves relationships. Consider:

  • Discussing concerns directly with your child's teacher or case manager
  • Requesting a meeting with the campus special education coordinator
  • Contacting the district special education director (view contact info)
  • Requesting an ARD meeting to address concerns

Document all communication attempts. If informal resolution doesn't work, proceed with formal processes below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides multiple pathways for resolving special education disputes. Here's the hierarchy from informal to formal:

Level 1

District Grievance Process

Best for: Service delivery issues, communication problems, implementation concerns

Timeline: Varies by district; typically 5-10 business days per level

Cost: Free

Binding: No - either party can escalate

Learn about Carroll ISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of IDEA, state special education rules, procedural errors

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of violation; TEA investigates within 60 days

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds violation, district must comply with corrective actions

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties want to negotiate with a neutral third party

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 2-4 weeks

Cost: Free (state-provided mediator)

Binding: Only if both parties agree in writing

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disputes about eligibility, placement, FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education)

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of violation; hearing within 45 days

Cost: May require attorney ($250-400/hour); can request attorney fees if you prevail

Binding: Yes - hearing officer's decision is legally binding

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504, ADA violations)

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of discrimination; OCR investigates

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - if OCR finds violation, district must comply

District Grievance Process (Carroll ISD)

Carroll ISD, like most Texas districts, follows a multi-level grievance process (often referred to as Board Policy FNG). This is the primary local path for resolving disputes before involving state authorities.

Level One: Campus Administrator

The process begins with a formal written grievance submitted to the principal or campus administrator. You must typically file within 15 days of the incident. A conference will be held to discuss the issue, and the administrator will provide a written response.

Level Two: District Administration

If you are not satisfied with the Level One response, you can appeal to the Superintendent or a designee. They will review the record and provide a new decision based on district policy and special education law.

Level Three: Board of Trustees

The final local step is a hearing before the Carroll ISD Board of Trustees. While the Board often defers to administrative decisions, this is your opportunity to present the issue to elected officials.

TEA Special Education Complaints

If Carroll ISD is in clear violation of state or federal law (e.g., failing to implement a signed IEP), you can file a complaint with the Texas Education Agency. Unlike a local grievance, TEA investigators will review the evidence and issue a "Finding of Fact."

  • Scope: Procedural violations (missing timelines, not providing services).
  • Outcome: TEA can order the district to provide "Compensatory Services" to make up for missed education.
  • Filing: Must be done in writing and include specific facts about the violation.

Due Process Hearings

Due process is the most formal "legal" route. It is essentially a trial before a Special Education Hearing Officer. This is the path used for complex disagreements over whether a child is receiving FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education).

Note: Due to the legal nature of these hearings, it is highly recommended to consult with a parent advocate or attorney. If you win your case, Carroll ISD may be required to pay your attorney's fees.

OCR Complaints (Discrimination)

If you believe Carroll ISD is discriminating against your child based on their disability—such as exclusion from a school trip or failure to provide accessibility—you can file with the federal Office for Civil Rights.

OCR does not look at the quality of the IEP, but rather whether the child has equal access compared to non-disabled peers.

Documentation: Your Best Tool

Whether you are filing locally or with the state, your evidence is everything. Ensure you have organized the following:

  • The Paper Trail: Keep copies of all IEPs, Progress Reports, and ARD meeting minutes.
  • Communication Logs: Keep a record of every email, phone call, and meeting with Carroll ISD staff, including dates and summaries.
  • Prior Written Notice (PWN): If Carroll ISD refuses a request (for an evaluation, a service, or a placement), they MUST provide you with a PWN explaining why.
  • Progress Data: If you are arguing a child isn't progressing, bring work samples or private evaluation data.
