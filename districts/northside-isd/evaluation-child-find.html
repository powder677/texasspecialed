How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Northside ISD

A comprehensive guide to starting the special education evaluation process in Northside ISD, San Antonio, Texas.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal IDEA law and Texas Education Code, Northside ISD has a "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with disabilities (birth through age 21) who may need special education services, regardless of severity.

You Have the Right To:

  • Request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) at any time, in writing
  • Receive a response (Prior Written Notice) within 15 school days of your request
  • Have your child evaluated in all areas of suspected disability
  • Receive evaluation results in your native language
  • Disagree with the district's evaluation and request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense

New to Special Education?

If you're concerned about your child's learning, behavior, or development, requesting an evaluation is the first step. You do not need a doctor's diagnosis to request evaluation from Northside ISD.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Northside ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

Send a written evaluation request to Northside ISD. While verbal requests are accepted, written requests trigger specific legal timelines and protections under Texas law.

2

District Reviews Your Request

Northside ISD will review your request and determine whether an evaluation is warranted. Within 15 school days, the district must provide you with:

  • Prior Written Notice explaining why they are or are not conducting the evaluation
  • A copy of the Procedural Safeguards
  • If agreeing to evaluate: Official Consent for Evaluation forms
3

Sign Consent for Evaluation

Once you receive the consent form from the district, review it carefully. It should list all areas being evaluated (academic, speech, behavior, etc.).

Crucial Point: The 60 calendar days evaluation timeline in Texas does NOT start until Northside ISD receives your signed consent in writing.

Tip: Date your consent form and keep a digital copy. This marks Day 1 of the 60-day countdown.
4

Evaluation is Conducted

Northside ISD will assess your child using a variety of tools. This may include:

  • Academic testing (reading, math, writing)
  • Speech and language evaluation
  • Psychological or cognitive assessment (IQ testing)
  • Occupational or Physical Therapy evaluations
  • Functional Behavioral Assessment (FBA)
  • Dyslexia screening: Required under Texas HB 3928, which mandates that dyslexia evaluations occur under IDEA (special education) protocols
5

Initial ARD Meeting is Scheduled

Within the 60 calendar days window, the district must:

  • Complete all formal evaluations
  • Schedule an Initial ARD (Admission, Review, and Dismissal) meeting
  • Provide you with the written evaluation reports (FIIE) before the meeting so you can prepare

At the ARD meeting, the committee (which includes you) will determine if your child meets one of the 13 eligibility categories and requires special education services.

What If Northside ISD Misses the Deadline?

If the district does not complete the evaluation within 60 calendar days, you have the right to:

  • Request a written explanation for the delay
  • File a formal state complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA)
  • Request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at district expense

Common Questions About Evaluations in Northside ISD

Can the school refuse to evaluate my child?

Yes, if they do not suspect a disability. However, they must provide "Prior Written Notice" explaining their refusal. You can challenge this via mediation or a due process hearing.

Do I need a doctor's diagnosis first?

No. Under Child Find, Northside ISD must evaluate based on educational need. A medical diagnosis (like ADHD or Autism) can be helpful, but the school's evaluation determines educational eligibility.

Does dyslexia require special education in Texas?

Yes, as of Texas HB 3928 (passed in 2023), dyslexia is now clearly defined as a disability that should be evaluated under IDEA (special education) rather than just Section 504.
