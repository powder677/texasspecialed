Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Northside ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate or attorney
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to San Antonio from another state, the term "ARD" might be unfamiliar. In the rest of the United States, these are simply referred to as IEP meetings. However, Texas law uses the "ARD" terminology to describe the committee's specific functions.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The formal entry of a student into special education after eligibility is determined.
  • Review: The required annual meeting to review progress and update the educational plan.
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting special education when a child no longer requires specialized services.

In Northside ISD, the ARD committee is responsible for developing the Individualized Education Program (IEP) that governs your child's education.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Northside ISD?

Because Northside ISD is a large district, you may see various specialists involved in your child's meeting depending on their specific needs.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

That's you! You are an equal member of the ARD committee. You bring essential knowledge about your child’s life outside the classroom.

Special Education Teacher

The teacher responsible for implementing the specialized instruction and tracking progress toward IEP goals.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in the general education environment.

District Representative

Usually a campus administrator who can authorize the allocation of district resources and staff.

Evaluator

A diagnostician or school psychologist who explains testing data and evaluation results.

The Student

Required for transition planning (age 14+) but encouraged whenever the student can meaningfully participate.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocate: A professional who helps you navigate the legal requirements of the ARD process.
  • Attorney: You have the right to legal counsel; however, Northside ISD typically requests advance notice if an attorney will attend.
  • Private Specialists: If your child sees an outside therapist or doctor, their input can be shared with the committee.
  • Support System: A trusted friend or relative who can help you take notes and remain focused.

Note: You should inform your campus ARD facilitator in advance of any additional attendees you intend to invite.

How Northside ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Northside ISD often utilizes dedicated ARD Facilitators or Special Education Coordinators to manage the documentation and ensure the meeting stays on track. Meetings are held at the student's home campus, though virtual options (via Zoom or Google Meet) are frequently available for parent convenience.

For specific questions about Northside ISD's ARD procedures, contact the special education department.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Northside ISD

Advocacy starts at home. Being prepared allows you to participate as a confident, equal partner in the decision-making process.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Thoroughly review the current IEP and previous progress reports.
  • Look over recent grades, work samples, and standardized test scores.
  • Check for any new medical reports or private evaluations from San Antonio providers.
  • Note any significant changes in your child's behavior or home life.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Create a list of your top 3 concerns regarding your child's education.
  • Draft a list of questions about specific services or accommodations.
  • Request a "draft" of the proposed IEP from the school to review beforehand.
  • Verify the meeting time and ensure your support person/advocate is confirmed.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

A typical ARD meeting in San Antonio's Northside ISD follows a standardized agenda to ensure all components of the IEP are addressed.

Typical Meeting Flow in Northside ISD:

  1. Introductions and Statement of Purpose - Establishing who is present and why the meeting was called.
  2. Review of Evaluation Data - Looking at current levels of academic and functional performance (PLAAFP).
  3. Parental Input - This is your time to share your vision and concerns for your child.
  4. Review of Goals - Assessing progress on old goals and drafting new, measurable ones.
  5. Determination of Services - Discussing therapy, modifications, and classroom support.
  6. Placement Discussion - Deciding the "Least Restrictive Environment" (LRE) for the student.
  7. Closing and Signatures - Finalizing the document and indicating agreement or disagreement.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break if the meeting becomes overwhelming.
  • ✓ You can request the meeting be recessed (paused) for up to 10 days if there is a disagreement.
  • ✓ You can ask for an explanation of any acronyms or technical terms used by staff.
  • ✓ You can voice a formal "disagreement" and have it recorded in the meeting minutes.
  • ✓ You can record the meeting (Texas is a "one-party consent" state for audio recording).

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Focus on the "Why" — If the school suggests a change, ask for the data that supports it.
  • Keep it student-centered — Ensure every decision is based on your child's unique needs, not "what the school usually does."
  • Ask for a copy of the minutes (deliberations) before you leave.
  • Don't feel rushed — If you need to take the document home to read before signing, you have that right.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting is just the beginning of the implementation phase.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You agree with the plan and sign the document. Services typically begin within 5 school days or as specified in the IEP.

Partial Agreement

You agree with some parts (like the goals) but not others (like the placement). You can sign "Agreement" for the parts you like so they start immediately, while noting your "Disagreement" on others.

Full Disagreement

If the committee cannot reach a consensus, the district must offer a 10-day recess to gather more information. If agreement still isn't reached, you can explore mediation or due process.

Implementation Timeline:

Northside ISD is required to provide the services as written in the IEP. If you notice a delay or your child reports that a service isn't happening, contact the campus administrator immediately.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

At a minimum, Northside ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually (the "Annual ARD").
  • Every three years for a full reevaluation (the "REED").
  • Whenever a parent or teacher requests one in writing to address a specific concern.
