Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Frisco ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team).
  • Must be held at least annually to review and revise your child's educational program.
  • Parents are equal members with the right to participate in all decisions.
  • Meetings must be scheduled at a mutually agreeable time and place.
  • Texas law allows parents to audio record ARD meetings without prior school consent.

ARD Meeting Guide for Frisco ISD Parents

Empowering families in the Frisco Independent School District to navigate the ARD process with clarity, confidence, and collaborative advocacy.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are new to the DFW area from another state, you may be familiar with "IEP Meetings." In Texas, these are legally termed ARD Committee Meetings.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial meeting where a student is admitted into special education services.
  • Review: The annual meeting where the committee reviews progress and updates the Individualized Education Program (IEP).
  • Dismissal: The process used when a student no longer qualifies for special education services.

In Frisco ISD, the ARD committee is the decision-making body that determines eligibility, placement, and the specific services your child receives.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Frisco ISD?

The committee is comprised of individuals who bring different expertise regarding your child's education and development.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are an equal member. Your knowledge of your child's history and out-of-school needs is irreplaceable.

Special Education Teacher

The provider responsible for implementing the specialized instruction and tracking goal progress.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates (or may participate) in general education classrooms.

District Representative

An administrator (Principal or Assistant Principal) who can authorize district resources.

Evaluator

A Diagnostician or LSSP (Psychologist) who interprets testing data and evaluation results.

The Student

Required for transition discussions (beginning at age 14) and encouraged whenever appropriate.

Optional Attendees:

  • Professional Advocate: Someone you hire to help protect your rights and your child's interests.
  • Attorney: Parents have the right to legal representation. Frisco ISD typically requests notice if an attorney will attend.
  • Private Specialists: Outside therapists or doctors who can share relevant medical or therapeutic data.
  • Support Person: A family friend or relative who can help you take notes or provide emotional support.

Frisco ISD Specific Practices

Frisco ISD often utilizes ARD Facilitators at secondary campuses to ensure meetings remain student-focused and move through the agenda efficiently. For students who are Deaf or Hard of Hearing, a specialized teacher must be part of the committee, which may require additional lead time for scheduling.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Frisco ISD

Being prepared allows you to move from a recipient of information to a proactive partner in the process.

2 Weeks Before: Research & Documentation

  • Thoroughly review the current IEP and the last few progress reports.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  • Gather recent work samples, report cards, and private clinical reports.
  • Ask your child about their classroom experience—what helps them and what is hard.

1 Week Before: Goal Setting

  • Draft your "Parent Concerns" statement; this is a required section of the IEP deliberations.
  • Identify 3-5 specific changes or additions you want to propose.
  • Request a draft of the proposed IEP goals from the school.
  • Notify the campus coordinator if you are bringing an advocate or recording the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Frisco ISD meetings follow a specific legal structure. Understanding the agenda helps you know when to introduce your points.

The Typical ARD Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Establishing roles and ensuring all required members are present.
  2. Review of PLAAFP: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Sharing your observations, concerns, and vision for your child.
  4. Goal Development: Setting specific, measurable objectives for the coming year.
  5. Services & Accommodations: Determining modifications, classroom support, and therapy minutes.
  6. LRE & Placement: Deciding where services will be provided in the Least Restrictive Environment.
  7. Deliberations & Minutes: Reviewing the written summary of the meeting for accuracy.

Your Rights at the Table:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point.
  • ✓ You can request the meeting be recessed for up to 10 days if a disagreement arises.
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any acronym or technical term used.
  • ✓ You have the right to an interpreter if English is not your primary language.
  • ✓ You can disagree with recommendations and have that disagreement noted in the records.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting is only the beginning of the new IEP year.

Meeting Outcomes:

Full Agreement

If everyone agrees, services typically begin within 5-10 school days unless a different date is set in the IEP.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to the goals while disagreeing with the placement. This allows the agreed-upon services to start while you work through the dispute.

Formal Disagreement

If you cannot reach a consensus, you can request a recess, file a grievance, or pursue mediation.

Future Meetings

Frisco ISD is required to hold an ARD:

  • Annually: To review and update the IEP.
  • Triennially: For a full reevaluation (REED process).
  • On Demand: Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss concerns.
