Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Lewisville ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Meetings are typically held at the student's home campus and last about one hour
  • You are an equal member of the committee and a vital partner in goal development
  • LISD provides draft IEPs (PLAAFPs and goals) prior to the meeting for your review
  • If a disagreement occurs, a 10-day recess is standard protocol

ARD Meeting Guide for Lewisville ISD Parents

Navigating the special education journey in North Texas requires understanding the specific procedures used by Lewisville ISD to support student success.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have recently moved to the DFW metroplex from another state, you might be looking for "IEP meetings." In Texas, the state legislature uses the term ARD, though the outcome remains the creation of an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial determination of eligibility for special education services.
  • Review: The annual or periodic check-in to adjust goals and monitor progress.
  • Dismissal: The formal process of exiting a student from special education when they no longer require specialized instruction.

In Lewisville ISD, the ARD process is designed to be collaborative. The district emphasizes that parents bring "expertise on your child" while staff bring "expertise on education". Together, you form the committee that governs every aspect of your child’s school day.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Lewisville ISD?

The committee must include specific members to ensure all legal and educational requirements are met during the decision-making process.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

The Most Important Member: Your participation is legally mandated, and your input on your child's strengths and needs is fundamental to the IEP.

Special Education Teacher

In LISD, this may be an "SDI Strategist" who provides Specially Designed Instruction. They report on goal progress and instructional strategies.

General Education Teacher

Required if your child participates in any general education classes. They provide input on how your child is performing relative to grade-level TEKS.

District Representative

Typically a campus administrator (principal or assistant principal) who can commit district resources and ensure the IEP is implemented with fidelity.

Diagnostician or Psychologist

Commonly referred to as the "Diag," this member interprets evaluation data and explains how your child's disability impacts their learning.

The Student (as appropriate)

Required starting at age 14 for transition planning, but LISD encourages student participation at younger ages if parents feel they are ready.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocates: Professional special education advocates are welcome. LISD asks that you notify the school in advance if an advocate or attorney will attend.
  • Outside Providers: Private therapists (OT, PT, Speech) who work with your child can provide valuable data.
  • Support Person: A friend or family member for emotional support or note-taking.

Unique Lewisville ISD Parent Supports

Lewisville ISD provides several unique resources to help parents feel more confident as ARD members:

  • The Special Education Family Center: Located at 400-A High School Dr. in Lewisville, this center offers free programs on Tuesday evenings, including childcare, for LISD special education families.
  • "The Spot": A lending library in Room 110 of the Central Special Education Admin Building where you can borrow books and learning kits.
  • FOCUS Series: Evening events specifically designed for families of children with Autism.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Lewisville ISD

Effective preparation transforms the ARD meeting from a presentation into a collaborative dialogue.

10-14 Days Before: Review and Request

  • Review the Draft IEP: Lewisville ISD typically shares the draft PLAAFPs and goals before the meeting. Read these carefully.
  • Check progress reports: Are goals being mastered, or has progress stalled?
  • Visit "The Spot" to find resources on specific disabilities or advocacy techniques.
  • Gather any private evaluations from outside therapists to share with the LISD team.

5 Days Before: Document Your Vision

  • Write down your "Parent Concerns." These must be included in the official ARD document.
  • Prepare a list of 3-5 priority goals you want your child to achieve this year.
  • If you plan to audio record the meeting, notify the campus administrator. (Texas law allows this, and LISD will often record as well if the parent does).
  • Confirm the meeting location (usually your child's campus) or the virtual link.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

A typical LISD ARD follows a structured agenda to ensure all legal requirements are addressed efficiently.

The Typical LISD Meeting Flow:

  1. Introductions: Everyone states their name and role.
  2. Review of Progress (PLAAFP): The team discusses "Present Levels." LISD focuses on how the student accesses the curriculum.
  3. Review of Evaluation: The Diagnostician explains any new testing data.
  4. Parent Input: You share your observations and concerns. This is a critical moment for advocacy.
  5. Goals & Objectives: The committee reviews and revises the proposed IEP goals.
  6. Accommodations & Modifications: Discussion of supports like extra time, shortened assignments, or specialized software.
  7. Placement Discussion: The team determines the "Least Restrictive Environment" (LRE). In LISD, this might include settings like "Mainstream," "SDI In-Class Support," or "BASE".
  8. Consensus & Signatures: The committee works toward agreement on the final plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a short break if the meeting becomes overwhelming.
  • ✓ You have the right to ask for clarification on any acronyms or educational jargon.
  • ✓ You can disagree with any part of the plan. You do not have to sign "Agree" if you feel the plan is insufficient.
  • ✓ You can request a 10-day recess if consensus cannot be reached.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Ask to see the data supporting the district's recommendations for service minutes.
  • Focus on Measurable Goals: Ensure every goal has a clear way to track progress.
  • Ask about In-Home Training: If your child struggles with generalizing skills (like potty training or communication) from school to home, ask if an IHT evaluation is appropriate.
  • Stay Student-Centered: Keep the conversation focused on what your child needs to access their education.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The conclusion of the meeting is just the beginning of the new IEP's implementation.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You and LISD agree on all sections. The new IEP is finalized, and services begin as scheduled. You will receive a copy of the final document.

Partial Agreement

In Texas, you can agree to the parts of the IEP you like while noting your disagreement with specific sections. The agreed-upon portions will be implemented while you work to resolve the other issues.

The 10-Day Recess

If you disagree with the committee's final recommendation, LISD protocol offers a 10-day recess. During this time, the team can gather more data or consult with the Special Education Director to find a resolution.

Implementation and Monitoring:

Once the ARD is signed, Lewisville ISD staff must implement the services exactly as written. We recommend checking in with your child's teacher two weeks after the ARD to ensure accommodations are in place and the student is adjusting well to any changes in placement or services.

When to Request Another ARD:

While an ARD is required annually, you can request an ARD meeting at any time if you have concerns about your child's progress, safety, or service delivery. Lewisville ISD must respond to a written request for an ARD within 15 school days.
