Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Lamar CISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (the Texas specific name for the IEP team).
  • These meetings are held at least once a year to evaluate and update your child's educational roadmap.
  • You are an equal partner in the committee, holding the same decision-making weight as school administrators.
  • You have the legal right to bring outside support, including advocates or private therapists.
  • In Lamar CISD, you can request an ARD committee meeting at any time if you feel your child's needs have changed.

ARD Meeting Guide for Lamar CISD Parents

Navigating the special education process in Rosenberg, Richmond, and Fulshear. This comprehensive guide helps Lamar CISD families prepare for, participate in, and follow up on ARD committee meetings.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you are new to the Houston metro area or have moved from another state, you might be looking for "IEP meeting" information. In Texas, we use the term ARD Committee, which describes the group of people responsible for making all special education decisions.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

The ARD acronym stands for:

  • Admission: The process of determining eligibility and admitting a student into special education services.
  • Review: The annual (or more frequent) review of the student's Individualized Education Program (IEP).
  • Dismissal: The decision-making process for when a student no longer requires specialized services.

While the terminology is unique to Texas, the outcome is the same: the creation of a legally binding Individualized Education Program (IEP) tailored to your child's specific needs within Lamar CISD.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Lamar CISD?

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are the most important member. You provide the longitudinal history and home-based observations that are vital to creating a realistic plan.

Special Education Teacher

The provider who often serves as the "Case Manager," reporting on progress toward goals and instructional strategies.

General Education Teacher

Provides insight into the grade-level curriculum (TEKS) and how your child is performing relative to their peers.

Administrator

Usually a Principal or Assistant Principal who can commit Lamar CISD resources and funding to the plan.

Diagnostician or LSSP

The Licensed Specialist in School Psychology (LSSP) or Diagnostician who interprets evaluation data and testing results.

The Student

Included when transition services are discussed (starting at age 14) or whenever the parents feel it is appropriate.

Additional Attendees:

  • Related Service Providers: Speech Pathologists, Occupational Therapists (OT), or Physical Therapists (PT) if your child receives these services.
  • Advocates: You may bring a professional special education advocate or a knowledgeable friend.
  • Translators: Lamar CISD is required to provide an interpreter if your primary language is not English.

Pro-Tip: Lamar CISD campuses appreciate a few days' notice if you plan to bring an advocate or attorney so they can ensure the room size is adequate and their own staff is prepared.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Lamar CISD

Phase 1: Information Gathering

  • Review your child's current IEP goals and the most recent progress reports.
  • Review work samples and report cards from the Lamar CISD Parent Portal (Skyward).
  • Gather any recent private evaluations from outside therapists or doctors.
  • Request a draft IEP from the campus 3-5 days before the meeting to review proposed goals.

Phase 2: Goal Setting

  • Write down your top three concerns for your child's academic or social development.
  • Draft a list of questions about specific accommodations (e.g., extra time, small group testing).
  • Note any medical changes or behavioral concerns observed at home.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

The Standard Meeting Agenda in Lamar CISD:

  1. Introductions: Establishing everyone's role in the meeting.
  2. PLAAFPs: Reviewing the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance" (the "Where are we now?" section).
  3. Evaluation Results: If a new Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) has been completed.
  4. Parent Input: Your opportunity to voice concerns and your vision for your child.
  5. Goal Review: Collaboratively writing or revising annual goals.
  6. Services and Accommodations: Determining the frequency and duration of special education services.
  7. LRE/Placement: Deciding where the services will be delivered (General Ed, Resource, Self-Contained).
  8. Consensus: The committee works toward agreement on the plan.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any time to regroup.
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any "edu-jargon" or acronyms.
  • ✓ Recording: Texas law allows you to audio record the meeting. We recommend notifying the team at the start of the session.
  • ✓ You can disagree with the school's recommendations and refuse to sign "Agree."
  • ✓ You can take the IEP home to review for up to 5 days before making a final decision.

Tips for Effective Advocacy:

  • Ask "How will this goal be measured?"
  • Request to see the data that supports moving a child to a more or less restrictive environment.
  • Use "I" statements, such as "I notice that [Student] struggles with..."
  • Remember that the IEP is a living document—it can be changed if it's not working.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

Outcome 1: Full Consensus

If you and the district agree, you sign "Agree," and the new plan is implemented on the agreed-upon start date. You should receive a final copy of the IEP via email or paper within a few days.

Outcome 2: Partial Agreement

You can agree to the goals but disagree with the placement. In Texas, you can implement the agreed-upon parts while documenting your disagreement on the disputed sections.

Outcome 3: Non-Consensus and Recess

If the committee cannot reach an agreement, Lamar CISD will offer a 10-day recess. This allows everyone to cool off, gather more data, and meet again to try and find a middle ground. If you still disagree after the second meeting, you have several dispute resolution options.

Ongoing Monitoring:

The work doesn't end when the meeting stops. Monitor your child's progress reports every 9 weeks. If they are not making progress toward their goals, don't wait for the next annual meeting—request a "Brief ARD" to address the issues immediately.
