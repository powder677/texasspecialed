Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Cypress-Fairbanks ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Required at least annually to review your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee
  • You have the legal right to bring an advocate or attorney
  • You can request an ARD meeting at any time

ARD Meeting Guide for Cypress-Fairbanks ISD Parents

Everything you need to know about preparing for and participating in ARD committee meetings in Cypress-Fairbanks ISD, Texas.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you're new to the Houston area or special education in general, you may have heard the term "IEP meeting" used in other states or in national news. In Texas, we use specific terminology that defines the lifecycle of a student's special education journey.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The process of determining if a student is eligible for special education and "admitting" them into the program.
  • Review: The ongoing process of reviewing current services, progress on goals, and adjusting the plan (at least annually).
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting from special education services when a student no longer meets eligibility criteria.

The outcome of an ARD meeting is the Individualized Education Program (IEP). In Cypress-Fairbanks ISD, the ARD committee is the group of people legally responsible for developing, reviewing, and revising your child's IEP to ensure they receive a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE).

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Cypress-Fairbanks ISD?

The composition of the ARD committee is mandated by law to ensure all perspectives—instructional, administrative, and parental—are represented.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

That's you! You are an equal member of the ARD committee. Your input carries the same weight as district staff, and you are the expert on your child's history and needs outside of school.

Special Education Teacher

This teacher provides input on specialized instruction, modifications, and progress toward specific IEP goals. They are often the primary point of contact for your child's services.

General Education Teacher

Required if your child spends any time in general education classes. They discuss how the child is performing in the general curriculum and what accommodations are being used.

District Representative

Typically an administrator (principal or assistant principal) who has the authority to commit district resources and ensure services will be provided as written.

Diagnostician or Psychologist

These professionals interpret evaluation results and help the committee understand how the student’s disability impacts their learning and behavior.

Your Child (if appropriate)

Required when transition services are discussed (starting at age 14 in Texas). Cypress-Fairbanks ISD encourages older students to lead or participate in their meetings to build self-advocacy skills.

You May Also Bring:

  • Advocate or Educational Consultant: Someone knowledgeable about special education law and Cy-Fair ISD policies to support you.
  • Attorney: You have the right to bring legal counsel. If you do, it is standard courtesy (and often required) to notify Cypress-Fairbanks ISD in advance so they can have their own counsel present.
  • Outside Evaluator: If you have had private testing done, you can bring the therapist or psychologist to explain the findings.
  • Family Member or Friend: ARD meetings can be emotional; having a support person to take notes can be invaluable.

Note: You should inform your campus ARD coordinator in advance of any additional attendees you plan to bring to ensure the room size or virtual link is appropriate.

How Cypress-Fairbanks ISD Conducts ARD Meetings

Cy-Fair ISD offers both in-person and virtual ARD meeting options. For virtual meetings, the district typically utilizes Zoom or Microsoft Teams. The district often utilizes "ARD Facilitators" for complex meetings to help keep the conversation focused on the student's needs and move through the agenda efficiently.

For specific questions about Cypress-Fairbanks ISD's ARD procedures, contact the special education department.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Cypress-Fairbanks ISD

Preparation is the key to moving from a passive participant to an active advocate. Don't wait until you walk into the school building to think about your goals.

2 Weeks Before: Review & Gather

  • Review your child's current IEP in detail—what worked and what didn't?
  • Review recent progress reports to see if goals are being met.
  • Gather recent report cards, district-wide assessment scores, and classroom work.
  • Collect work samples showing specific areas where your child is struggling.
  • Review any private evaluations or medical reports you want the committee to consider.
  • Note any changes in behavior or medication that might impact school performance.

1 Week Before: Plan & Document

  • Write down your top 3-5 concerns or goals for the upcoming year.
  • List specific questions you want to ask the teachers or therapists.
  • Identify any specific services or accommodations you believe your child needs.
  • Confirm if your advocate or support person can attend.
  • Notify the school of any additional attendees you are bringing.
  • Request a draft of the proposed IEP from the teacher so you can review it before the meeting.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Meetings in Cy-Fair ISD follow a structured agenda to ensure all legal requirements are met. Understanding this flow helps you know when to speak up.

Typical Meeting Flow in Cypress-Fairbanks ISD:

  1. Introductions - Everyone identifies their name and role in the meeting.
  2. Review of Current Status - A discussion of how the student is doing now compared to last year.
  3. Evaluation Results - If a Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) was recently completed, the diagnostician will explain it.
  4. Parent Input - This is your dedicated time to share your observations and concerns.
  5. Discussion of Services - The committee discusses what support the student needs to reach their goals.
  6. IEP Development - Setting new goals and determining the "Placement" (where services happen).
  7. Consensus Building - The committee works to ensure everyone is on the same page.
  8. Signature and Next Steps - Formally closing the meeting and signing the paperwork.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for breaks at any time to process information or speak with your advocate.
  • ✓ You can request the meeting be recessed and rescheduled if you feel you need more time to review documents.
  • ✓ You can ask for "plain language" explanations if the district uses too much "edu-speak" or acronyms.
  • ✓ You can disagree with the district's recommendations and have that disagreement recorded in the minutes.
  • ✓ You can request changes to any part of the IEP, including goals and accommodations.
  • ✓ You can refuse to sign "agreement" on the IEP if you are not satisfied with the outcome.
  • ✓ Recording: You can audio record the meeting. In Texas, you do not need the district's consent, though it is professional courtesy to notify them.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Take notes during the meeting or bring someone specifically to take notes for you.
  • Don't be afraid to ask, "How will this goal be measured?"
  • Use "I" statements to stay collaborative: "I've noticed he struggles with homework after these sessions."
  • Always bring the discussion back to the data: "What data shows that this accommodation is no longer needed?"
  • Request specific, measurable goals instead of vague ones like "will improve reading."
  • You do not have to sign immediately. You have the right to take the document home and review it before signing agreement.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting might be over, but the work of ensuring the IEP is followed has just begun.

Possible Outcomes:

Full Agreement

You and Cypress-Fairbanks ISD agree on all aspects of the IEP. You sign, and the services are typically implemented within 5-10 school days.

Partial Agreement

You agree with the majority of the plan but have a specific disagreement (e.g., about the number of speech therapy minutes). You can sign in agreement for the services you want to start, while formally noting your disagreement on the specific section.

Full Disagreement

If you cannot reach an agreement, the ARD committee may offer a "10-day recess." This gives both parties time to gather more information and return to the table to try again.

Implementation Timeline:

Once the IEP is signed, Cy-Fair ISD is legally bound to follow it. Check in with your child's teachers after two weeks to ensure they have received the new IEP and are implementing the accommodations.

When is the Next ARD Meeting?

At a minimum, Cypress-Fairbanks ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: To review and update the IEP every 12 months.
  • Triennially: Every three years for a reevaluation of eligibility.
  • Upon Request: You can request an ARD at any time if you feel the current plan isn't working.
  • Change in Placement: Before any major change in where or how your child is taught.
