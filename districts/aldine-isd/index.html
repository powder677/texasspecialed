About Aldine ISD Special Education

Aldine Independent School District operates a centralized Special Education Department located at the Aldine Westfield Annex, separate from the main administration building. This distinction is important for parents dropping off records or attending district-level meetings. Aldine ISD serves a high percentage of economically disadvantaged students, and its special education department works closely with federal programs to ensure equitable access to resources across the district.

The district is known for specific specialized programs like the **GAP Center** (located at the Lane School), which focuses on empowering students to learn independent cognitive and behavioral skills. For younger children, Aldine ISD places a heavy emphasis on **Early Childhood Special Education (ECSE)**, offering assessments for children ages 3-5 who are not yet enrolled in school but may have developmental delays. If you have a preschool-aged child, the district coordinates these "Child Find" efforts to ensure early intervention.

Navigating the chain of command in Aldine usually starts with the "Department Chair" or "Lead Teacher" at your specific campus before moving to the Program Directors at the central office. Unlike some districts that rely heavily on email, Aldine ISD encourages the use of its "Let's Talk" platform for submitting questions or concerns, ensuring they are routed to the correct department.

Recent state monitoring has focused on ensuring Aldine ISD meets compliance for evaluation timelines. If you have requested a Full and Individual Initial Evaluation (FIIE) for your child, be aware that the district is strictly monitored on these timelines, and you have the right to receive your evaluation report 30 days following its completion.
