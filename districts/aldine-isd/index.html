About Houston ISD Special Education

Houston ISD (HISD) provides a vast array of special education services designed to meet the diverse needs of its student population. As the largest school district in Texas, Houston ISD operates under a vision where every student with a disability has access to grade-level learning through high-quality instruction. Whether your child requires inclusion support in a general education setting or highly specialized programs, the district offers a continuum of placements. These include "Resource" settings for small-group instruction and "Skills for Learning and Living (SLL)" for students with significant cognitive impairments who need practical life skills training.

```

One of the unique features of Houston ISD is its dedicated team of Special Education Parent Liaisons. These professionals are specifically tasked with supporting you through the complex ARD process and helping bridge the gap between home and school. Furthermore, HISD has implemented Evidence-Based Dyslexia Instruction (EBDI), ensuring that students with dyslexia receive multisensory, systematic reading support. This focus aligns with the Texas Dyslexia Handbook and recent legislative shifts that emphasize identifying dyslexia through the special education lens to ensure all students receive the specific SDI (Specially Designed Instruction) they need.

Recent changes within Houston ISD have focused on improving student outcomes through more rigorous alignment with the Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS). While the district has undergone significant administrative shifts recently, the legal protections for your child under Child Find remain steadfast. This means the district is responsible for identifying and evaluating any child suspected of having a disability. If you live within the Houston ISD boundaries, even if your child attends a private school or is homeschooled, you have the right to request an evaluation to determine eligibility for services.

For parents new to the system, it is important to know that HISD utilizes the "Admission, Review, and Dismissal" (ARD) committee model, which is the Texas-specific version of an IEP team. Your participation as a parent is not just encouraged; it is a legal requirement. You are a full member of the committee that determines your child's placement, goals, and accommodations. Understanding how to navigate this large system is key to ensuring your child receives the Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) they are entitled to under federal and state law.
```
