File a Special Education Complaint in Katy ISD

Understand your dispute resolution options in Katy Independent School District, which serves approximately 90,000-95,000 students in the West Houston and Katy area. Learn the escalation process, know your rights, and access templates for Katy ISD's specific Level One through Level Three grievance structure.

Quick Answer: To file a special education complaint in Katy ISD, start with your campus ARD chair or principal. If unresolved, escalate to the district level by submitting a formal Level One grievance form according to Katy ISD Board Policy FNG(LOCAL). Katy ISD, which serves a large population in West Houston, manages complaints through its legal and compliance department, routing special education issues to the Executive Director of Special Education for review. You can also file directly with the Texas Education Agency (TEA) if you believe a federal or state law has been violated.

Quick Facts: Dispute Resolution in Katy ISD

  • Your Rights: You can disagree with any ARD decision without fear of retaliation.
  • Start Here: Informal campus-level resolution with your child's ARD chair.
  • Escalate To: Formal Level One Grievance (Policy FNG) via the Superintendent's office.
  • State Option: File with TEA within 1 year of the alleged violation.
  • Legal Option: Due process hearing (highly recommended to seek an attorney).

Dispute Resolution Escalation Path in Katy ISD

If you disagree with a decision regarding your child's evaluation, placement, or services, follow this recommended sequence to resolve the dispute in Katy ISD:

Level 1

Campus-Level Resolution (Start Here)

Contact:

  • Your child's ARD committee chair
  • Campus Special Education Coordinator
  • Campus Principal

Timeline: Informal; request a response or a meeting within 5-10 business days.

Method: Send a professional email detailing your concerns. If the ARD meeting ended in disagreement, you may request a 10-day recess to review data before reconvening.

Level 2

District-Level Complaint (FNG Local)

If campus level does not resolve the issue:

Katy ISD Formal Grievance Process:

Method: Submit the formal Level One Grievance form. Katy ISD requires this to be filed within 15 days of the incident.

Contact: Executive Director of Special Education

Address: 6301 S. Stadium Lane, Katy, TX 77494

Phone: 281-396-6000

System: Katy ISD assigns a hearing officer for Level One grievances to conduct a formal conference with the parents.

Level 3

Texas Education Agency (TEA) Complaint

If district-level attempts fail:

File with TEA: TEA Special Education Complaints Portal

Timeline: TEA has 60 calendar days to investigate Katy ISD's compliance.

Outcome: If Katy ISD is found out of compliance, TEA will mandate "Corrective Action Plans" which may include compensatory services for your child.

Common Questions About Katy ISD Dispute Resolution

How do I file a special education complaint in Katy ISD?

Start at campus level. Contact your child's ARD committee chair or campus principal first to attempt informal resolution. If the issue is not resolved, escalate to the district level by submitting a formal Level One grievance form to the Katy ISD Superintendent’s office. Katy ISD uses a formal tracking system for Board Policy FNG grievances and will assign a district administrator to hear your case and provide a written decision within 10 days of your conference.

What if Katy ISD refuses to provide services in the IEP?

This is a "Failure to Implement" issue. If your child is not receiving the speech minutes, inclusion support, or accommodations listed in their ARD document, document the missed dates. If the campus does not rectify this immediately, a TEA complaint is often the most effective route, as TEA has a specific mandate to ensure IEP implementation. Katy ISD must provide "Compensatory Services" to make up for any missed minutes if they are found in violation.

Does Katy ISD have a parent liaison for special education?

Yes. Katy ISD often employs Parent Liaisons or "Instructional Officers for Compliance" who can help bridge communication between parents and the campus. If you feel stuck, asking for the "Instructional Officer" assigned to your campus's feeder pattern is a proactive way to get a district-level set of eyes on your child's file without filing a formal legal complaint.

What is a "Recess" in an ARD meeting?

A 10-day cooling-off period. In Texas, if you disagree with the ARD committee on a required element (like placement or goals), you have the right to request a recess. The committee will reconvene within 10 school days. Katy ISD uses this time to gather more data or have a district-level coordinator review the proposed plan. If agreement is still not reached at the second meeting, the district will implement their plan, and you can then file for mediation or a TEA complaint.

Katy ISD Complaint System Details

Does Katy ISD require campus-level resolution?

Recommended but not strictly required. Katy ISD strongly encourages parents to attempt resolution at the campus level. If you bypass the principal and go straight to the Executive Director, you will often be asked if you have already held a "Staffing" or a "Resolution Meeting" with campus leadership. For systemic issues, however, you may file directly with the district office.

How does Katy ISD track complaints?

Tracking via Policy FNG. When a formal grievance is filed, it is logged by the Superintendent’s office. Each complaint is assigned a tracking number, and a record of the proceedings, including the audio recording of the grievance conference, is maintained as part of the district's administrative record.

Protection Against Retaliation

Federal law (IDEA) and Section 504 strictly prohibit Katy ISD from retaliating against you or your child for filing a complaint. Retaliation includes things like making your child's schedule less favorable, being "too busy" to meet with you, or treating your child differently in the classroom because you advocated for their rights.

Protect Your Records:

  • Keep a "Communication Log" of all calls and meetings.
  • Always follow up a verbal conversation with an email: "As we discussed today..."
  • Save all Progress Reports; they are your best evidence of whether a child is making progress.
