Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Aldine ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team).
  • Meetings must be held at least once per year to update your child's educational plan.
  • You are a vital member of the committee with equal standing to district staff.
  • You have the legal right to record meetings and bring an advocate for support.
  • Initial ARD meetings follow within 30 days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

ARD Meeting Guide for Aldine ISD Parents

Essential guidance for families in Aldine Independent School District navigating the ARD process. Learn how to prepare, participate, and advocate for your child's success in Houston.

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

Families moving to the Aldine area from outside Texas may be searching for "IEP team meetings." In the Texas public school system, these are officially known as ARD Committee Meetings.

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

ARD stands for:

  • Admission: The initial determination of eligibility and admission into special education.
  • Review: The annual review of the student's progress and current services.
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting a student from special education services when they no longer qualify.

The outcome of these meetings is the Individualized Education Program (IEP). In Aldine ISD, the ARD committee is responsible for ensuring your child receives a Free Appropriate Public Education (FAPE) tailored to their unique needs.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Aldine ISD?

The committee is a multi-disciplinary team. Depending on your child's specific disability and the campus size in Aldine ISD, the following people will attend:

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

You are the expert. Your input on your child's history, interests, and home life is critical to the team.

Special Education Teacher

Provides data on progress toward goals and proposes specialized instructional methods.

General Education Teacher

Required if the student participates in the general education curriculum.

District Representative

A school administrator (Principal or Assistant Principal) with the authority to commit district resources.

Evaluation Specialist

A Diagnostician or LSSP who explains evaluation findings and testing data.

The Student

Mandatory for transition planning (beginning at age 14) and encouraged for self-advocacy.

Additional Attendees You May Bring:

  • Special Education Advocate: To help you navigate the procedural safeguards and advocate for services.
  • Attorney: You have the legal right to bring an attorney. Aldine ISD usually requests advance notice for legal counsel.
  • Outside Providers: Private therapists or physicians who can offer relevant data for the committee.
  • Support System: A family member or friend to take notes or offer emotional support.

Aldine ISD Meeting Format

Aldine ISD typically holds ARD meetings at the student's home campus. Given the district's size and geographic spread across North Houston, virtual attendance (via Zoom or Microsoft Teams) is often available to help parents participate despite work or transportation barriers.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Aldine ISD

Preparation is the key to ensuring your voice is heard. Do not wait for the meeting to start to begin your advocacy.

2 Weeks Before: Gather Your Data

  • Review the current IEP and the last three progress reports.
  • Gather any private medical reports or outside evaluations.
  • Look through your child's recent work samples and report cards.
  • Review the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

1 Week Before: Define Your Priorities

  • Write down your "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the meeting minutes.
  • Identify the top 3 goals or service changes you want to propose.
  • Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a draft of the proposed goals.
  • Confirm the attendance of your advocate or support person.

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Meetings follow a legal sequence to ensure all required components are addressed.

The ARD Meeting Agenda:

  1. Introductions: Confirming all required members are present.
  2. Reviewing PLAAFPs: Discussion of Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance.
  3. Parent Input: Your opportunity to share your concerns and your child's progress at home.
  4. Setting Goals: Developing specific, measurable skills for the student to master.
  5. Accommodations & Services: Determining what support and classroom modifications are needed.
  6. Review of Minutes: Checking the "deliberations" (meeting notes) for accuracy.
  7. Signature: Formalizing agreement or disagreement.

Your Legal Rights in the Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break at any point during the discussion.
  • ✓ You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee cannot reach an agreement.
  • ✓ You can audio record the meeting (Texas allows this without school consent).
  • ✓ You can ask for clarification on any term or acronym (e.g., LRE, BIP, FBA).
  • ✓ You can refuse to sign agreement if you feel the plan is not appropriate for your child.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

The meeting ends, but the implementation is where the results happen.

Full Agreement

Services typically start within 5 school days or on the date specified in the IEP.

Partial Agreement

You can agree to the goals while disagreeing with the minutes or placement. The agreed-upon portions begin while you resolve the dispute.

Disagreement

If you disagree, you can explore mediation, file a grievance, or request a due process hearing.

Required Meeting Frequency

Aldine ISD must hold an ARD meeting:

  • Annually: At minimum, once every 12 months.
  • Triennially: For a full reevaluation (REED) every 3 years.
  • On Request: Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss new concerns.
