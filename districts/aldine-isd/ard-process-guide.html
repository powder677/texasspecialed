Answer: In **Aldine ISD**, an ARD meeting is the legally required Texas special education meeting where parents and district staff determine eligibility, services, and placement. Parents must receive at least **five school days’ written notice**, have the legal right to **audio record** meetings, and are recognized as **equal members** of the committee.

This page describes ARD procedures specific to Aldine ISD and may not reflect practices in other Texas school districts.

Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Aldine ISD

  • Notice Timeline: You must be notified in writing at least 5 school days before the meeting.
  • Recording Rights: You can audio record the meeting; Texas allows this without school consent.
  • Parent Role: You are a vital member of the committee with equal standing to district staff.
  • Annual Requirement: Meetings must be held at least once per year to update the educational plan.
  • Initial ARD: Meetings follow within 30 days of a completed Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).

Common ARD Questions

How do I request an ARD meeting?

Parents can request an ARD at any time to discuss new concerns. Under Texas law, the district must respond to your request to meet regarding your child's Individualized Education Program (IEP).

What happens if I disagree with the committee?

You can request a "10-day recess" if the committee cannot reach an agreement during the meeting. You also have the right to refuse to sign agreement if you feel the plan is not appropriate for your child.

Can I bring someone with me?

Yes, you may bring a special education advocate, attorney, or support person to the meeting. Aldine ISD usually requests advance notice if you plan to bring legal counsel.

How to Prepare for an ARD Meeting

  1. Gather Your Data: Review the current IEP, the last three progress reports, and the most recent Full Individual Evaluation (FIE).
  2. Define Your Priorities: Identify the top 3 goals or service changes you want to propose for your child.
  3. Request a Draft IEP: Ask the teacher for a draft of the proposed goals at least one week before the meeting.
  4. Write a Parent Statement: Prepare a "Parent Concerns" statement to be included in the official meeting minutes.
  5. Confirm Attendance: Notify your advocate or support person and confirm the meeting time with the Aldine ISD campus.

How ARD Meetings in Aldine ISD May Differ From Other Districts

Due to the district's size and geographic spread across North Houston, Aldine ISD often utilizes virtual attendance via Zoom or Microsoft Teams to help parents participate. Additionally, required participants like the Evaluation Specialist (Diagnostician or LSSP) are typically assigned at the campus level but may represent central district resources during complex transition or placement ARDs.
