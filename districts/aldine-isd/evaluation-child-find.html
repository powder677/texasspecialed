Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal and Texas law, Aldine ISD has a "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with disabilities residing within the district who may need special education services. This mandate covers children from birth through age 21, including those in private schools or homeschool environments.

You Have the Right To:

  • Request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) at any time, provided it is in writing.
  • Receive a response (Prior Written Notice) within 15 school days of your written request.
  • Have your child evaluated in all areas of suspected disability by qualified professionals.
  • Receive a copy of the Procedural Safeguards notice in your native language.
  • Disagree with the district's findings and request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense.

Diagnosis vs. Eligibility

In Aldine ISD, a medical diagnosis from a doctor does not automatically qualify a student for services. The district must conduct its own evaluation to determine if the disability impacts your child’s educational performance and requires specialized instruction.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Aldine ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

The evaluation process formally begins with a written request. If your child is enrolled in an Aldine ISD school, submit the request to the Campus Principal. For children not yet in school (ages 3-5), contact the Special Education Department directly.

2

The 15-Day Response

Once Aldine ISD receives your written request, they have 15 school days to respond. They will either provide you with a Notice of Evaluation and consent forms or a Prior Written Notice explaining why they refuse to evaluate.

3

Sign Consent for Evaluation

The evaluation cannot begin until you sign the Written Consent form. By signing, you are agreeing to the testing, not to the placement of your child in special education.

Critical Detail: The 45 school day timeline for the district to complete the evaluation report begins the day they receive your signed consent.

Tip: Always hand-deliver your consent or send it via certified mail and keep a copy for your records to track the deadline.
4

Comprehensive Evaluation (FIIE)

Aldine ISD specialists, including Diagnosticians and LSSPs, will assess your child. This process involves:

  • Intellectual and developmental assessments.
  • Academic achievement testing in areas like reading and math.
  • Observations of the child in the classroom setting.
  • Dyslexia Screening: Under Texas HB 3928, dyslexia is now evaluated as a specific learning disability under IDEA protocols.
5

Initial ARD Meeting

Within 30 calendar days of the report being completed, an ARD committee meeting must be held. You are a vital member of this committee. Together, you will review the evaluation and determine if the child qualifies for an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Texas Timeline Requirements (15-45-30)

The special education process in Aldine ISD is governed by strict state-mandated timelines:

15 School Days

Request to Consent/Refusal.

45 School Days

Consent to Completed Evaluation.

30 Calendar Days

Evaluation to ARD Meeting.

What if Timelines are Missed?

If Aldine ISD exceeds these deadlines, your child's rights may have been violated. Parents should:

  • Email the Campus Principal and Evaluation Coordinator immediately.
  • Contact SPEDTex at 1-855-773-3839 for advocacy support.
  • Submit a formal grievance to the district or the TEA.

Common Questions for Aldine ISD Families

How much does the evaluation cost?

Evaluations provided by Aldine ISD are conducted at no cost to the parents. This is part of the "Free Appropriate Public Education" (FAPE) guaranteed by federal law.

What if my child is in a private daycare?

If the daycare is within Aldine ISD boundaries, the district is still responsible for "Child Find" activities and must evaluate if a disability is suspected.

What is an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE)?

If you disagree with the results of the Aldine ISD evaluation, you have the right to request an IEE from an outside professional, paid for by the district.
