How to Request a Special Education Evaluation in Conroe ISD

A comprehensive guide to starting the special education evaluation process in Conroe ISD, Houston Metro Area, Texas.

Child Find & Your Rights

Under federal IDEA law and the Texas Education Code, Conroe ISD has a "Child Find" duty to identify, locate, and evaluate all children with disabilities (birth through age 21) who may need special education services. This active process includes children who are homeschooled or attending private schools within the district's jurisdiction.

You Have the Right To:

  • Request a Full Individual and Initial Evaluation (FIIE) at any time, in writing.
  • Receive a response (Prior Written Notice) within 15 school days of your request.
  • Have your child evaluated in all areas of suspected disability.
  • Receive a copy of the Procedural Safeguards explaining your legal rights.
  • Disagree with the district's evaluation and request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at public expense.

New to Special Education?

If you're concerned about your child's learning, behavior, or development, requesting an evaluation is the first step. You do not need a doctor's diagnosis to request evaluation from Conroe ISD.

Step-by-Step Evaluation Process in Conroe ISD

1

Submit a Written Request

Send a written evaluation request to Conroe ISD. For children already enrolled, address the letter to the Campus Principal. For pre-school aged children not yet enrolled, address the letter to the Director of Special Education.

2

District Reviews Your Request

Conroe ISD will review your request and determine if an evaluation is warranted. Within 15 school days, the district must provide you with:

  • Prior Written Notice explaining why they are or are not conducting the evaluation.
  • A copy of the Procedural Safeguards.
  • If agreeing to evaluate: Official Consent for Evaluation forms.
3

Sign Consent for Evaluation

Review the consent form carefully to ensure it lists all suspected areas of disability. The 45 school days evaluation timeline in Texas does NOT start until Conroe ISD receives your signed consent in writing.

Tip: Keep a digital copy of your signed and dated consent form as it marks Day 1 of the legal countdown.
4

Evaluation is Conducted

Conroe ISD specialists (LSSPs, Diagnosticians, and Therapists) will assess your child. This may include:

  • Academic testing in reading, math, and writing.
  • Speech and language evaluation.
  • Psychological or cognitive assessment.
  • Dyslexia assessment: Per Texas HB 3928, dyslexia must be evaluated through the IDEA special education process.
5

Initial ARD Meeting is Scheduled

Within 30 calendar days of completing the evaluation report, the district must hold an Initial ARD meeting.

At this meeting, the committee (including you) will review the report and determine if your child meets one of the 13 eligibility categories and requires an IEP.

Texas Timeline Requirements (15-45-30)

The evaluation process in Conroe ISD follows the "15-45-30" rule mandated by Texas law:

15 School Days

To provide consent form or refusal notice after your written request.

45 School Days

To complete the evaluation after receiving signed consent.

30 Calendar Days

To hold the ARD meeting after the evaluation report is finished.

What If Conroe ISD Misses a Deadline?

If the district fails to meet these timelines, you have the right to:

  • Request a written explanation for the delay.
  • File a formal state complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA).
  • Request an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE) at district expense.

Common Questions About Evaluations in Conroe ISD

Can the school refuse to evaluate my child?

Yes, if they do not suspect a disability. However, they must provide Prior Written Notice and explain why they are refusing. You can challenge this refusal through mediation or a due process hearing.

Does a medical diagnosis from my doctor count?

A medical diagnosis is helpful and will be reviewed, but Conroe ISD must still conduct its own evaluation to determine educational eligibility for services.

How is dyslexia handled?

Under Texas law (HB 3928), all dyslexia evaluations are now part of the IDEA (special education) process, ensuring students receive full ARD protections.
