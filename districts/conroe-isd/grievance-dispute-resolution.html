⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Many complaints have strict filing deadlines. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. Local grievances with Conroe ISD (CISD) must typically be filed within 15 district business days of the incident or decision. Missing this deadline can result in your complaint being dismissed immediately.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Conroe ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Conroe ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or IEP.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP lists specific services (e.g., speech therapy, resource time, transportation) but CISD is not providing them as written.

Evaluation Delays

The district has missed the 45-school-day timeline for completing an evaluation or failed to hold the ARD meeting within 30 days of the report.

ARD Meeting Issues

The district refuses to hold an ARD meeting, proceeds without you, or refuses to consider your input regarding your child's programming.

Placement Disagreements

You disagree with the instructional setting Conroe ISD is proposing (e.g., they suggest a centralized program while you believe your child belongs in general education).

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated differently, excluded from activities, or disciplined unfairly because of their disability.

Retaliation

You feel district staff are retaliating against you or your child because you advocated for their rights or filed a previous complaint.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

In many cases, informal resolution is faster and preserves relationships. Consider:

  • Discussing concerns directly with your child's teacher or case manager.
  • Requesting a staffing with the campus Special Education Coordinator.
  • Contacting the CISD Special Education Department at 936-709-7813.
  • Reaching out to Executive Director Dr. Kendra Wiggins.

Tip: Document all communication attempts. If informal resolution doesn't work, proceed with the formal processes below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides multiple pathways for resolving special education disputes. Here's the hierarchy from informal to formal:

Level 1

District Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Best for: Campus-specific issues, staff conduct, failure to follow policy, communication breakdowns.

Timeline: Strict deadlines apply (15 business days).

Cost: Free.

Binding: No - can be appealed to the School Board.

Learn about CISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of IDEA, state special education rules, procedural errors (e.g., missed timelines).

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of violation; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds violation, district must comply with corrective actions.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties want to negotiate a solution with a neutral third party.

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 2-4 weeks.

Cost: Free (state-provided mediator).

Binding: Only if both parties agree to the resolution.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disputes about eligibility, placement, or FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education).

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of violation; hearing within 45 days.

Cost: High. May require attorney ($250-400/hour); usually requires significant preparation.

Binding: Yes - hearing officer's decision is legally binding.

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504, ADA violations), retaliation, harassment.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of discrimination.

Cost: Free.

Binding: Yes - if OCR finds violation, district must comply.

Conroe ISD Grievance Process

Conroe ISD follows Board Policy FNG(LOCAL) for student and parent complaints. This is a formal administrative process with strict timelines.

The Three Levels of Grievance

Level One: Campus Principal

You must file a written complaint form with the campus principal or the District's legal office. In CISD, this must be done within 15 district business days of the date you first knew, or with reasonable diligence should have known, of the decision or action giving rise to the complaint.

  • The hearing officer (often the principal) will schedule a conference within 10 days of receipt.
  • You will receive a written response (decision) within 10 days following the conference.

Level Two: Superintendent's Designee

If you are not satisfied with the Level One decision, you may appeal to the Superintendent's office.

  • You must file the appeal notice within 10 district business days of the date of the written Level One response.
  • A central office administrator will hold a conference.
  • You will receive a written response within 10 days following the conference.

Level Three: Board of Trustees

If you are still dissatisfied, you may appeal to the CISD Board of Trustees.

  • You must file the appeal within 20 calendar days of the date of the written Level Two response.
  • The Board will hear your complaint at a scheduled meeting (usually within 60 days of the previous decision).
  • The Board's decision is final within the district administrative process.

Take Action: Contact the Legal Department at 3205 W. Davis St., Conroe, TX 77304 or call 936-709-7700 to request the official grievance forms.

Filing a Complaint with the Texas Education Agency (TEA)

A TEA Special Education Complaint is appropriate when you believe CISD has violated a specific state or federal special education law. This is different from a local grievance because it goes directly to the state agency.

Requirements

  • The complaint must be in writing and signed.
  • It must allege a violation of the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act (IDEA) or state special education rules.
  • It must state the facts on which the allegation is based.
  • The violation must have occurred not more than one calendar year prior to the date the complaint is received.

The Process

  1. Submission: Send your complaint to TEA and forward a copy to the Conroe ISD Superintendent.
  2. Investigation: TEA has 60 calendar days to investigate. They may request documents from you and the district.
  3. Decision: TEA will issue a written decision. If they find the district at fault, they will order "corrective actions" (e.g., compensatory services, staff training) that CISD must complete.

Due Process Hearings

A Due Process Hearing is a formal legal proceeding similar to a trial. You present evidence and witnesses before a Special Education Hearing Officer. This path is typically reserved for severe disputes regarding eligibility, placement, or FAPE.

Note: Due process is complex and adversarial. While parents are allowed to represent themselves ("pro se"), it is highly recommended to consult with a special education attorney or advocate before filing for due process, as the district will be represented by legal counsel.

Common Reasons for Due Process

  • Disagreement over eligibility (e.g., district says child does not have a disability).
  • Disagreement over placement (e.g., LRE disputes).
  • Failure to provide FAPE (Free Appropriate Public Education).

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaints

The U.S. Department of Education's Office for Civil Rights enforces Section 504 and Title II of the ADA. If your dispute involves discrimination, harassment, or retaliation based on disability, this is the appropriate venue.

TEA Complaint vs. OCR Complaint

Feature TEA Complaint OCR Complaint
Law Focus IDEA (Special Education Services) Section 504 & ADA (Civil Rights/Discrimination)
Typical Issues IEP minutes, goals, evaluations Access, bullying, harassment, exclusion, retaliation
Timeline 1 Year 180 Days (approx. 6 months)

To file a complaint with OCR, you do not need to have a lawyer. The process is generally more accessible to parents than a due process hearing.

Documentation is Key

Regardless of which dispute resolution path you choose, success depends on documentation. Keep a "Parent Communication Log" containing:

  • Dates and times of all conversations.
  • Names of staff members you spoke with.
  • Follow-up emails summarizing verbal conversations ("Just to recap our phone call...").
  • Copies of all ARD documents, evaluations, and progress reports.
