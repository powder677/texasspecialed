Quick Facts: ARD Meetings in Pasadena ISD

  • ARD = Admission, Review, and Dismissal Committee (Texas term for IEP team)
  • Mandated at least annually to review and update your child's program
  • You are an equal member of the committee with shared decision-making power
  • You have the legal right to record meetings and bring advocates
  • You can request an ARD committee meeting at any point during the school year

What is an ARD Committee? (Texas vs. Other States)

If you have moved to Pasadena from outside of Texas, you may be looking for "IEP team meetings." In Texas, the process is called the ARD committee meeting, though it still results in an Individualized Education Program (IEP).

Other States:

IEP Team

IEP Meeting

Texas:

ARD Committee

ARD Meeting

The ARD acronym refers to the committee's three primary functions:

  • Admission: The initial meeting to determine if a student qualifies for special education.
  • Review: The ongoing review of progress and adjustments to the IEP.
  • Dismissal: The process of exiting special education when services are no longer required.

In Pasadena ISD, the ARD committee is the sole authority for making decisions regarding your child’s eligibility, placement, and services. You are not just an attendee; you are a voting member of this committee.

Who Attends ARD Meetings in Pasadena ISD?

Pasadena ISD ensures that a multidisciplinary team is present to address all aspects of your child's education.

Required Participants:

Parent(s) or Guardian(s)

Your role is vital. You provide the longitudinal history and home-based observations that school staff lack.

Special Education Teacher

Reports on progress toward goals and provides input on specialized instructional strategies.

General Education Teacher

Provides insight into grade-level expectations and how your child is performing in the general classroom.

District Representative

Usually a campus administrator who has the authority to commit district resources and funding.

Diagnostician or LSSP

The Licensed Specialist in School Psychology (LSSP) or Diagnostician explains evaluation data and testing results.

The Student

Whenever appropriate, and required starting at age 14 for transition planning.

Additional Attendees:

  • Specialists: Speech therapists, OTs, or PTs if your child receives these related services.
  • Advocates: You may bring a professional advocate or a friend for support.
  • Outside Professionals: If your child sees a private therapist, they can attend to share data.

PISD Practice: If you plan to bring an attorney or record the meeting, it is recommended to notify the campus 48 hours in advance so they can ensure the appropriate district personnel are also present.

How to Prepare for Your ARD Meeting in Pasadena ISD

2 Weeks Before: The Data Phase

  • Review your child's current IEP and the last 3 progress reports.
  • Review work samples from the current school year.
  • Note any new medical diagnoses or medication changes.
  • Request a draft copy of the proposed IEP goals from your child’s teacher.

1 Week Before: The Planning Phase

  • Create a list of your child’s strengths and your biggest concerns.
  • Prepare a "Parent Vision Statement" for what you want your child to achieve this year.
  • List questions you have about specific accommodations or service minutes.
  • If you disagree with a recent evaluation, research your right to an Independent Educational Evaluation (IEE).

During the ARD Meeting: What to Expect

Typical Meeting Flow in Pasadena ISD:

  1. Introductions: Establishing names and roles.
  2. Review of PLAAFPs: Discussing the "Present Levels of Academic Achievement and Functional Performance."
  3. Evaluation Review: If a new FIE (Full Individual Evaluation) was completed.
  4. Goal Setting: Collaboratively deciding on measurable annual goals.
  5. Accommodations: Determining the supports needed (extra time, small group, etc.).
  6. Placement: Deciding the Least Restrictive Environment (LRE).
  7. Closing/Signatures: Confirming agreement or noting disagreement.

Your Rights During the ARD Meeting:

  • ✓ You can ask for a break if you feel overwhelmed.
  • ✓ You can ask for the meeting to be recessed and finished on another day.
  • ✓ You can request that any jargon or acronyms be explained in plain language.
  • ✓ Recording: Texas law allows you to audio record the meeting. We recommend informing the committee at the start.
  • ✓ You can refuse to sign the IEP if you do not agree with the recommendations.

Tips for Effective Participation:

  • Ask "How will we know when this goal is mastered?"
  • Ask for the data that supports moving a child to a more restrictive setting.
  • Take your own notes or bring someone to take notes for you.
  • If the school mentions a service is not available "due to staffing," remind them that services are based on student need, not district availability.

After the ARD Meeting: Next Steps

Understanding the Outcome:

Full Consensus

The committee agrees on the plan. The IEP is implemented, and you should receive a final copy within a few days.

Partial Agreement

You agree with parts of the IEP but not others. You can sign "Agree" for the portions you support while formally noting your disagreement on the disputed sections.

Formal Disagreement

In Texas, if you disagree, the committee must offer a 10-day recess to gather more data and attempt to reach an agreement. If you still disagree after the reconvened meeting, you have the right to request mediation or a due process hearing.

Ongoing Monitoring:

Check your child's progress reports every grading period. In Pasadena ISD, these should arrive at the same time as report cards. If your child is not making progress toward their goals, you do not have to wait for the annual ARD—you can request a "Brief ARD" to review the goals and make adjustments.
