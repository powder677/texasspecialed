⚠️ Important Notice About Timelines

Strict deadlines apply to complaints. TEA complaints must be filed within one year of the alleged violation. Local district grievances in Pasadena ISD (FNG Local) typically must be filed within 15 business days of the time you knew or should have known of the issue. Missing this window often leads to automatic dismissal.

Filing Complaints & Resolving Disputes with Pasadena ISD Special Education

A comprehensive guide to your options when you disagree with Pasadena ISD about your child's special education services, evaluation, or IEP implementation.

When Should You Consider Filing a Complaint?

Services Not Being Provided

Your child's IEP requires specific services (e.g., speech therapy 30 mins/week, inclusion support, behavioral intervention), but Pasadena ISD is not providing them.

Evaluation Delays

Pasadena ISD has missed the 45-school-day timeline for completing the Full Individual Evaluation (FIE) or refuses to evaluate despite your written request.

ARD Meeting Issues

The district refuses to hold an ARD meeting, proceeds without you, or refuses to consider private evaluation data during the committee meeting.

Placement Disagreements

You disagree with the instructional setting (e.g., General Ed vs. Life Skills vs. specialized behavior program) proposed by the district.

Discrimination Concerns

You believe your child is being treated unfairly or excluded from school activities specifically because of their disability.

Retaliation

You feel that district staff are punishing your child or creating a hostile environment because you have advocated for their special education rights.

Before Filing a Formal Complaint:

Formal complaints can be lengthy. Informal resolution is often the fastest path to getting what your child needs. Consider:

  • Emailing the campus principal or Assistant Principal for Special Education.
  • Requesting a staffing or "pre-ARD" meeting to discuss concerns.
  • Contacting the Pasadena ISD Special Education Department at (713) 740-0220.
  • Contacting the Executive Director of Special Education.
  • Requesting a formal ARD meeting to document your disagreement in the deliberations.

Tip: Always document these conversations. Follow up phone calls with an email summary ("As we discussed..."). If informal methods fail, proceed below.

Levels of Dispute Resolution in Texas

Texas provides a hierarchy of options for resolving special education disputes, ranging from local to federal levels:

Level 1

District Grievance Process (FNG Local)

Best for: Disputes about district policy, specific staff conduct, or failure to follow the IEP.

Timeline: Must file within 15 district business days of the incident.

Cost: Free

Binding: No - decisions can be appealed to the School Board and then to the Commissioner of Education.

Learn about Pasadena ISD's grievance process ↓
Level 2

TEA Special Education Complaint

Best for: Violations of special education law (IDEA), missed timelines, or procedural errors.

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year of the violation; TEA investigates within 60 days.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - if TEA finds a violation, Pasadena ISD is legally required to correct it.

Learn about TEA complaints ↓
Level 3

Special Education Mediation

Best for: When both parties are willing to compromise and want a neutral third party to facilitate.

Timeline: Voluntary; typically scheduled within 30 days.

Cost: Free (TEA covers the cost of the mediator).

Binding: Agreements signed in mediation are legally binding contracts.

Request mediation through TEA: TEA Dispute Resolution

Level 4

Due Process Hearing

Best for: Serious disagreements regarding eligibility, placement (LRE), or the appropriateness of the program (FAPE).

Timeline: Must be filed within 1 year; hearing typically held within 45 days.

Cost: High (legal fees); families may be reimbursed for attorney fees if they win.

Binding: Yes - the Hearing Officer's decision is final (unless appealed to federal court).

Learn about due process ↓
Level 5

Office for Civil Rights (OCR) Complaint

Best for: Discrimination based on disability (Section 504/ADA) or retaliation.

Timeline: Must be filed within 180 days of the discrimination.

Cost: Free

Binding: Yes - OCR enforcement is federal.

Pasadena ISD Grievance Process (Level 1)

Pasadena ISD utilizes Board Policy FNG (LOCAL) for student and parent complaints. This formal process ensures your complaint is heard by administration, but strict adherence to the form and timeline is required.

Where to Submit Grievances

Address: Pasadena ISD Administration Building, 1515 Cherrybrook Lane, Pasadena, TX 77502

Attention: Associate Superintendent for Campus Development (or designated Grievance Officer)

Phone: (713) 740-0000

Note: We recommend submitting via certified mail or hand-delivering to the front desk and asking for a date-stamped receipt.

Step-by-Step Filing Guide

  1. Download the Level 1 Form

    Obtain the "Level One Student/Parent Complaint Form" from the PISD website or the administration building. Do not just write a letter; use the official form to ensure it is processed correctly.

  2. File Within 15 Days

    You must file the complaint within 15 district business days of the incident or the date you first knew of the problem. If you wait too long, the district can dismiss your grievance purely on timing.

  3. Level 1 Conference

    The appropriate administrator (often a Principal or Director) will schedule a conference with you to hear the complaint. They must provide a written decision within 10 business days after this meeting.

  4. Appeals (Level 2 & 3)

    If the Level 1 decision does not resolve the issue, you can appeal to the Superintendent (Level 2) and subsequently the Board of Trustees (Level 3). Appeals must be filed within 10 business days of receiving the prior decision.

Filing a Complaint with TEA

If you believe Pasadena ISD has violated federal or state special education laws (IDEA), you may file a complaint directly with the Texas Education Agency.

Common Violations TEA Investigates

  • District failure to implement the IEP (e.g., student not receiving specified minutes of service).
  • District failure to provide Prior Written Notice (PWN) before changing a placement.
  • District failure to meet evaluation timelines (45 school days).
  • District failure to include parents in the ARD meeting.

How to File:

  • Download the TEA Special Education Complaint Form.
  • Submit the form via email to: spedcomplaints@tea.texas.gov
  • Crucial: You must forward a copy of the complaint to the Pasadena ISD Superintendent at the same time you send it to TEA.

Due Process Hearings

A Due Process Hearing is the most formal dispute resolution option. It acts like a courtroom trial where an independent Hearing Officer hears evidence and issues a legally binding decision.

Legal Counsel: While you are allowed to represent yourself ("pro se"), Due Process is highly technical. Most parents find it necessary to hire a special education attorney or a qualified advocate.

When is Due Process Used?

  • Eligibility Disputes: PISD says your child does not have a disability, or has a different disability than you believe.
  • Placement (LRE): You believe your child needs a private placement or a more inclusive setting than offered.
  • FAPE Denial: The proposed IEP is not sufficient to allow your child to make meaningful progress.

Statute of Limitations: You have one year from the date of the alleged violation to request a hearing in Texas.

Office for Civil Rights (OCR)

The U.S. Department of Education Office for Civil Rights enforces Section 504 and the ADA. This is the correct venue for discrimination and civil rights issues, rather than educational programming disputes.

Grounds for OCR Complaints

  • Failure to provide disability-related accommodations (e.g., wheelchair access, large print).
  • Disability harassment or bullying that the school ignores.
  • Retaliation against a student or parent for asserting disability rights.
  • Different treatment in discipline (e.g., harsher suspensions for students with disabilities).

Documentation is Critical

In any dispute resolution process, the party with the best documentation usually prevails. Maintain a binder with:

  • All current and past IEPs and FIEs.
  • A communication log (dates, times, names, summary of conversations).
  • Printed copies of all emails (do not rely on them staying in your inbox).
  • Work samples, progress reports, and report cards.
  • Any audio recordings of ARD meetings (if you notified the district in advance).
